The press release printed below was issued by the Arts Council of Great Britain in 1983 and was the first poll of its sort that gave official credence to the popularity of Alan Ayckbourn's work. Also reprinted are a contemporary news report from The Times and a cartoon from the Sunday Mirror.

![Arts Council of Great Britain press release, embargoed Wednesday 2 November, 12 noon, headed "Ayckbourn more popular than Shakespeare: new plays attracting regional audiences; theatre audiences increasing this year; Arts Council to issue quarterly figures for regional repertory theatres". It reports that plays by Alan Ayckbourn have been attracting larger audiences in the subsidised regional theatres than those by Shakespeare, with Willie Russell in third place; that the top new plays for 1982–83 included Blood Brothers, Andy Capp, John Lennon and Restoration, the most performed of all plays being Cider with Rosie; and that after a fall in performances between 1981 and early 1983 the first two quarters of the financial year show that trend reversing, with theatres taking more at the box office in real terms than three years earlier and seats costing 20 per cent more than in 1980](./_images/83aa-artscouncil.jpg)
