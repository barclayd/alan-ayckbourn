### Season's Greetings

**Play Number:** 26  
**World Premiere:** 25 September 1980  
**Venue:** Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round, Scarborough

**Premiere Staging:** In-the-round

**Published:** **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/1709/seasons-greetings/)**  
**Other Media:** Television; Radio

**Cast:** 5m / 4f  
**Run Time:** 2hr 30m

**Synopsis:** A family gathering at Christmas exposes family animosity, whilst a new guest arouses differing passions.

</aside>

- *Season's Greetings* is Alan Ayckbourn's 26th play.
- The world premiere - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - was held at the **[Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round](/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre-in-the-round)**, Scarborough, on 25 September, 1980.
- The London premiere - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - was held at The Round House on 13 October 1980. Although this is officially regarded as the London premiere of the play, the 1982 Greenwich Theatre production - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - which opened on 28 January 1982 and later transferred to the Apollo Theatre in the West End, is sometimes incorrectly identified as the London premiere.
- *Season's Greetings* was not actually the play Alan Ayckbourn intended to write. His new play was announced as the thriller *Sight Unseen* in 1980, however when Alan could not write it, he took several of the character names, the hallway setting and the Christmas period and then wrote an entirely new play called *Season's Greetings* in its place.
- The play was originally produced as three-act play before it was substantially edited and revised into a two act play by the playwright.
- *Season's Greetings* was originally promoted as Alan Ayckbourn's 25th play and the world premiere production featured a programme with a silver cover to mark this. It is actually his 26th full length play though, as when first produced in 1980, Alan did not include ***[Jeeves](/plays/jeeves)*** in his official play canon.
- It is the first Ayckbourn play in which a character is shot - and if the inept character Doctor Longstaff had his way, it would be the first Ayckbourn play in which someone was murdered!
- The London production at The Round House marked the first time Alan had directed one of his plays in-the-round in London and the first time the original Scarborough company of an Ayckbourn play performed in London.
- *Season's Greetings* has been adapted for the radio twice by the BBC and also for television; the television adaptation is regarded as one of the best screen adaptations of Alan Ayckbourn's plays and is recognised by the British Film Institute as a significant example of the 'television play.'
- It was the first Ayckbourn play to be published as an ebook when Faber published it in 2010 to tie in with the National Theatre's revival of the play.
- The 2010 National Theatre revival of the play marked the first time the National Theatre had staged a revival of an Ayckbourn play rather than. the London premiere.
- Alarmingly, Alan Ayckbourn has noted the play draws on his own experiences of Christmas including his attempt to once stage a puppet-show for his children!
- Although published as a play text by **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/1709/seasons-greetings/)**, *Season's Greetings* has also been published by Faber.
