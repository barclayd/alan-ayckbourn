<aside>

### Sugar Daddies

**Play Number:** 63  
**World Premiere:** 22 July 2003  
**Venue:** Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough

**Premiere Staging:** In-the-round

**Published:** **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/7631/sugar-daddies/)**  
**Other Media:** No

**Cast:** 2m / 3f  
**Run Time:** 2hr 20m

**Synopsis:** A young woman is rescued from a near miss by 'Father Christmas', a shady father figure who is not all he seems. But can Sasha see him for what he is or is she blinded by the gifts and attention he lavishes on her?

</aside>

- *Sugar Daddies* is Alan Ayckbourn's 63rd play.
- The world premiere - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - was held at the **[Stephen Joseph Theatre](/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre)**, Scarborough, on 22 July 2003.
- Alan Ayckbourn actually wrote *Sugar Daddies* after completing ***[Orvin - Champion Of Champions](/plays/orvin-champion-of-champions)***, but as *Sugar Daddies* was produced prior to *Orvin*, it is considered the earlier play in the canon (*Orvin* is Alan Ayckbourn's 64th play).
- *Sugar Daddies* is often considered as an extension of the ***[Damsels In Distress](/plays/damsels-in-distress)*** trilogy dealing as it does with similar themes; although it lacks the London Docklands setting of the trilogy.
- It is one of the few Ayckbourn plays to be specifically set in London. Other London-set plays include ***[Virtual Reality](/plays/virtual-reality)***, ***[GamePlan](/plays/gameplan)***, ***[FlatSpin](/plays/flatspin)***, ***[RolePlay](/plays/roleplay)*** and ***[Private Fears In Public Places](/plays/private-fears-in-public-places)***.
- *Sugar Daddies* is one of Alan Ayckbourn's 'Christmas' plays being set during the Christmas period. Other 'Christmas' plays include ***[Dad's Tale](/plays/dads-tale)***, ***[Christmas V Mastermind](/plays/christmas-v-mastermind)***, ***[Absurd Person Singular](/plays/absurd-person-singular)***, ***[Joking Apart](/plays/joking-apart)*** (act II, scene I), ***[Season's Greetings](/plays/seasons-greetings)*** and ***[Life & Beth](/plays/life-and-beth)***.
- One of the play's major themes (alongside the corruption of innocence) is how people pretend to be something else for other people - although we can rarely maintain our new personas before returning to who we really are.
- Mobile phones are a relative rarity in Alan Ayckbourn's plays, but *Sugar Daddies* undoubtedly has his most memorable use of one when a mobile phone is - off-stage - inserted into a character only to ring when doctors are attempting to remove it!
- *Sugar Daddies* marked the point when Alan Ayckbourn had written as many plays as his age (he was 64 in 2003). From this point onwards, the number of his plays has always outstripped his years!
- This is a rare instance where Alan Ayckbourn has subsequently altered a play following its original production. When he revived and directed the play at ACT, Seattle, in 2013 he altered the climax of the play to better reflect Sasha's experiences through the play. The altered scene can be found **[here](/plays/sugar-daddies/sugar-daddies-script-alteration)**.
- Although published as a play text by **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/7631/sugar-daddies/)**, *Sugar Daddies* was also published in the collection *Alan Ayckbourn: Plays 3* (Faber).
