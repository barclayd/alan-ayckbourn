### Hero's Welcome

**Play Number:** 79  
**World Premiere:** 8 September 2015  
**Venue:** Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough

**Premiere Staging:** In-the-round

**Published:** **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/59835/heros-welcome/)**  
**Other Media:** No

**Cast:** 3m / 3f  
**Run Time:** 2hr

**Synopsis:** A war hero returns to the town he fled 17 years ago which threatens to stir up old rivalries and resentments.

</aside>

- *Hero's Welcome* is Alan Ayckbourn's 79th play.
- The world premiere - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - was held at the **[Stephen Joseph Theatre](/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre)**, Scarborough, on 8 September 2015.
- The New York premiere - directed by Alan Ayckbourn - was held at the 59E59 Theaters on 26 May 2016.
- The play is set in the fictional northern town of Hadforth, a play first referenced by Alan Ayckbourn in his 1974 play ***[Confusions](/plays/confusions)***.
- The character of Baba was inspired by Alan Ayckbourn's first meeting with his future daughter-in-law, who at the time spoke very little English.
- The male protagonist, Murray, is an Ayckbourn character who has been in the army following the likes of Dan in ***[Private Fears In Public Places](/plays/private-fears-in-public-places)*** (2004) and Ez in ***[Arrivals & Departures](/plays/arrivals-and-departures)*** (2012); although Ez is the only active member of the services at the time of the play.
- The play features a composite set - several locations featured on the same single set - which Alan Ayckbourn has also used in plays such as ***[How The Other Half Loves](/plays/how-the-other-half-loves)***, ***[Wildest Dreams](/plays/wildest-dreams)***, ***[If I Were You](/plays/if-i-were-you)*** and ***[Life Of Riley](/plays/life-of-riley)***.
- Although *Hero's Welcome* makes reference to Murray being involved in a military conflict, the actual location and conflict is kept deliberately vague and un-named; even the full name of Baba - who is from the area - is ambiguous enough to suggest it could be anywhere from eastern Europe or the middle-east or possibly even further afield.
- BBC *Look North* presenters Harry Gration and Amy Garcia were recorded as the voices of the interviewers at the opening of the world premiere of the play.
- *Hero's Welcome* is one of a number of Alan Ayckbourn plays to have been directed in New York by the playwright himself. The other plays are *Arrivals & Departures,* ***[Bedroom Farce](/plays/bedroom-farce)*** (co-directed with Peter Hall), ***[A Brief History of Women](/plays/a-brief-history-of-women)***, ***[By Jeeves](/plays/jeeves)***, *Confusions,* ***[Intimate Exchanges](/plays/intimate-exchanges)***, ***[My Wonderful Day](/plays/my-wonderful-day)***, ***[Neighbourhood Watch](/plays/neighbourhood-watch)***, *Private Fears In Public Places* and ***[Time Of My Life](/plays/time-of-my-life)***.
- **Spoiler:** *Hero's Welcome* is one of only a handful of Ayckbourn's plays where on-stage characters die off-stage during the course of the action (other examples of this are *A Small Family Business*, *The Revengers' Comedies* and *Neighbourhood Watch*).
- Although published as a play text by **[Samuel French](http://www.concordtheatricals.co.uk/s/59835/heros-welcome/)**, *Hero's Welcome* was also published in the collection *Alan Ayckbourn: Plays 6* (Faber).
