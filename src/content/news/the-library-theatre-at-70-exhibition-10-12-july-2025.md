<p>Tomorrow sees the launch of a free exhibition celebrating the 70th anniversary of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company at Scarborough Library.</p>

<p>On 14 July 1955, <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> launched <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in there Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> on the first floor of Scarborough Library featuring a season of four new plays by four new writers.</p>

<p>It was theatre history in the making and <em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition celebrates this anniversary, going behind the scenes of the company’s 21 years at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>The exhibition, running from 10 – 12 July 2025, has been curated by Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist and theatre historian, <strong><a href="http://www.simon-murgatroyd.com">Simon Murgatroyd M.A.</a></strong> Created in association with  North Yorkshire Council, the exhibition celebrates Scarborough Library’s often forgotten significance in 20th century British theatre history.</p>

<p>Fittingly, the exhibition will be taking place on the first floor of Scarborough Library where theatre history was made in 1955. It centres on a display of 70 photographs charting the life of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre from 1955 to 1976. It includes pictures from every year including productions, behind-the-scenes as well as some of the people who made the company a success, such as its founder Stephen Joseph and his protégé Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p>The exhibition also includes an exclusive video presentation which includes rare archival footage of the Library Theatre from 1976, an interview with Sir Alan Ayckbourn CBE about his memories of working at the Library Theatre as well as other insights into the company and venue.</p>

<p>The exhibition will also incorporate original posters from 1955 to 1976 as well as a number of original pieces from the exhibitions Stephen Joseph ran both at the Library Theatre and in talks about the history of theatre-in-the-round and theatre design.</p>

<p>There is also a small display in the Scarborough Room on the ground floor which includes significant pieces from the Library’s own archival theatre collection including material relating to the founding and first night of the Library Theatre in 1955.</p>

<p>Simon Murgatroyd will be launching his new book – <strong><em><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/page-176/">A Theatrical Revolution</a></em></strong> – which charts the inaugural year of the Library Theatre as well as including an interview with Sir Alan Ayckbourn CBE about the Library Theatre. Priced at £5 and limited to 70 signed and numbered copies, it will be available initially exclusively at the exhibition.</p>

<p>Simon will also be giving a free talk about the significance of Scarborough Library in British theatre history on Thursday 10 July at 6pm and Saturday 12 July at 11am. Admission is free and all are welcome.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition is free and can be seen at Scarborough Library on Thursday 10 and Friday 11 July from 10am – 5pm and on Saturday 12 July from 10am – 2pm (final entry at 1.45pm).</p>

<p>Further details about the exhibition can be found on the <strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">A Round Town</a></strong> website <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/">here</a></strong>. <em>The Library Theatre at 70 </em>exhibition is organised by Simon Murgatroyd in association with North Yorkshire Council.</p>
