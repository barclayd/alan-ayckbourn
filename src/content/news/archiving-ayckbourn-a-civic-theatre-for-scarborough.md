<p><em>Archiving Ayckbourn is a regular feature in which Alan Ayckbourn’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, delves into the archives to highlight items of interest relating to the playwright and theatre in the round.</em></p>

<p><strong>A Civic Theatre For Scarborough</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>I recently gave a talk about Stephen Joseph and his desire to create a adaptable civic theatre space in Scarborough.</p>

<p>It was an unfulfilled dream but over the period of about six years, Stephen – who founded the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in the town – worked hard to find a suitable venue which he could repurpose on a budget in the hope it might appeal to and be funded by the Town Council.</p>

<p>Approximately 10 different venues were considered and rejected before Stephen’s death in 1967. The first proposal of which was actually an audacious idea to convert the first floor of the town’s library into a civic theatre.</p>

<p>This wasn’t quite as mad as it sounds as Stephen’s own company was based in the Concert Room on the first floor of the library operating as Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre. In the library’s archive collection, there is correspondence between Stephen Joseph and the Chief Librarian, Mervyn Edwards, discussing whether it was feasible, essentially, to expand the theatre from a seasonal booking to a year-round permanent performance space.</p>

<p>This idea was ultimately dismissed and as far as the archive records show was never discussed again. In the aftermath, Stephen worked on a proposal to convert the old town hall on St Nicholas Street – later the home of the Penthouse nightclub – into a civic theatre, which went to a council vote, where it was debated in 1961, before the vote was lost.</p>

<p>Ironically – or because as an archivist I seem fated to find key pieces of information just after I need them! – I’ve just discovered the proposal for a civic theatre at Scarborough Library did move further forward and there was a serious move to establish it later in 1961.</p>

<p>Whilst working with Scarborough Library’s archive group, I came across a rare programme for the 1961 theatre in the round festival, which Stephen Joseph adjudicated and which brought companies from around the country to perform in-the-round.</p>

<p>On the back page is an article entitled ‘A Civic Theatre For Scarborough’ which acknowledges the recent vote against the proposal and the interest and desire to move forward with the idea a civic theatre – but, once again, in the Concert Room at Scarborough Library.</p>

<p>It seems inconceivable that such an appeal would have gone out without the knowledge and, at least, tacit support of the library given the event itself was held in the library and was part of a fund-raising appeal under the auspices of the British Theatre League.</p>

<p>What’s interesting is this is a significant development of the existing make-shift theatre in the round space at the Library as it would incorporate an adaptable theatre, suitable for in the round, three-sided and end-stage performance. This would – undoubtedly – have been designed by Stephen Joseph for the space, even though he’s not named as Stephen was advocating an adaptable theatre for the town</p>

<p>The article goes on to appeal for £350 to provide the necessary equipment; this is by far the cheapest proposal I’ve encountered with regard to adapting an existing space. Stephen’s plans generally ran from £5,000 to £30,000 – still cheap for a theatre conversion.</p>

<p>But here, the aim is create a stage within the existing space of the Concert Room – already proven as a theatre space – without any other alterations or additions to the building. The budget of £350 being derived from 24 rostrums at £10 10s each, 4 dimmers at £5 and 8 lights at £13; eight lamps being the bare minimum needed to light an in-the-round space in the Concert Room.</p>

<p>Despite the fact such a comparatively small sum was required, the appeal obviously didn’t reach the target or it was thwarted in some way. There is – as far as I can ascertain – no further reference to an adaptable theatre for the Concert Room in subsequent years and Stephen Joseph himself hit a number of obstacles relating to his attempts to improve just the existing facilities at the Library Theatre over the coming years.</p>

<p>However, this appeal offers another insight into the desire during the 1960s to provide a civic theatre in Scarborough which could be used for amateur and professional production as well as – if it stayed true to Stephen Joseph’s ambitions – a flexible space created by the community for the community which could be used for many different purposes not just theatre performance.</p>

<p>Sadly, Stephen’s ambitions were never realised. The town never built a civic theatre and Stephen closed Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre at the end of the 1965 season. Whilst he did live to see it revived in 1967 just prior to his untimely death, it’s doubtful – given its limited finances and resources – he would have believed it would have survived for any length of time or that his Scarborough project would continue long-term in any form.</p>

<p>He, more than anyone, would probably be hugely surprised to discover that the company not only survived, but thrived under his protégé Alan Ayckbourn and now resides in a purpose built theatre bearing his own name. Perhaps not a civic theatre but a resilient and successful theatre nonetheless.</p>
