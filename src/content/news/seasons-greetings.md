<p>And so another year draws to a close.</p>

<p>But it’s been a very good year for all things Ayckbourn. We’ve seen the world premiere of his 90th play, <em><strong><a href="/plays/show-and-tell">Show &amp; Tell</a></strong></em>, which proved to be popular with critics and audiences alike as Alan reminisced about his lifelong association with theatre.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/show-tell-w350.jpg" alt="" width="350" height="350" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
</div>

<p>The play and its anniversary generated significant features from the BBC, <strong><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/stage/article/2024/aug/05/alan-ayckbourn-first-play-dazzling-career-stroke">The Guardian</a></strong> and the <strong><a href="https://www.msn.com/en-gb/entertainment/tv/alan-ayckbourn-i-think-that-s-it-for-the-play-really/ar-AA1pF4ho">Telegraph</a></strong> with Alan both looking forward and also reminiscing back to his earliest days at Theatre n the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>There was also the exclusive premiere of a previously unseen Ayckbourn play <em><strong><a href="/plays/father-of-invention">Father of Invention</a></strong></em>. Written during lockdown, it was premiered during a special weekend in Scarborough called <em>Mr A’s Amazing Days</em> and was a memorable and unique reading for Ayckbourn fans.</p>

<p>The same weekend also saw the premiere of a new documentary film about Alan in which he candidly talked about his life and career. serving up some of his many memorable stories about a life in theatre. Again, a one-off event, <em>Everything Including The Kitchen Sink</em> was a real treat for the fans.</p>

<p>We also had an acclaimed revival by the playwright himself of his 1997 classic <em><strong><a href="/plays/things-we-do-for-love">Things We Do For Love</a></strong></em> at the Old Laundry Theatre, Bowness-on-Windermere, and there have been a number of other significant revivals this year not least <em><strong><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce">Bedroom Farce</a></strong></em> at the Mill at Sonning and <em><strong><a href="/plays/snake-in-the-grass">Snake in the Grass</a></strong></em> at Dundee Rep. Across the channel, there’s also been significant success in Paris for his works with revivals of <em><strong><a href="/plays/how-the-other-half-loves">How The Other Half Loves</a></strong></em> and <em><strong><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>And it’s been a year of anniversaries. Sixty-five years since he began writing professionally with the world premiere of his first work, <strong><em><a href="/plays/the-square-cat">The Square Cat</a></em></strong>. It’s been the 50th anniversaries of his plays <em><strong><a href="/plays/absent-friends">Absent Friends</a></strong></em> and <em><strong><a href="/plays/confusions">Confusions</a></strong></em> as well as the 50th anniversary of the first broadcast of his only produced screenplay, <em><strong><a href="/plays/service-not-included">Service Not Included</a></strong></em>. It’s also been 25 years since the world premiere of his extraordinary diptych, <em><strong><a href="/plays/house-and">House &amp; Garden</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>I had the pleasure of marking the latter with a talk on Scarborough’s South Cliff gardens as well as marking the end of Scarborough Museum and Galleries excellent exhibition of items from the Ayckbourn Collection. Sadly, ill health meant I had to cancel many of my talks for the latter half of this year, but I’m back on my feet now and I have some exciting plans and news for 2025 to be announced in the new year.</p>

<p>Through all this, there have been improvements and additions to both the playwright’s official website <strong><a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a></strong> and <strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>, its sister site celebrating the legacy of theatre in the round in Scarborough.</p>

<p>So a good year all-in-all. And next year is looking be equally as exciting with a couple of very significant anniversaries…</p>

<p>But I’m going to leave you in suspense until the New Year to reveal some of those plans and events for the  months ahead.</p>

<p>In the meantime, we’ll be taking a break now until 2025. Thank you, as always, for reading the <strong><a href="https://archivingayckbourn.home.blog">blog</a></strong>, visiting <strong><a href="https://www.facebook.com/alan.ayckbourn.playwright/">Facebook</a></strong> and <strong><a href="https://www.instagram.com/ayckbourn_playwright/">Instagram</a></strong> and – most of all – supporting the <strong><a href="/">website</a></strong>. We need all the hits we can get and the support of Ayckbourn fans over more than two decades is much appreciated.</p>

<p>Until 2025, season’s greetings and best wishes to you all. May you have a Christmas that looks nothing like one found in an Alan Ayckbourn play!</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd, 2024</em></p>
