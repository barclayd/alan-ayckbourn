<p>The theatre director and writer Alan Strachan, who has a long history with Sir Alan Ayckbourn, has died at the age of 80.</p>

<p>Alan was Artistic Director of the Greenwich Theatre for over a decade, worked extensively in the West End and was an award-winning biographer. He was closely associated with Alan Ayckbourn between the 1970s and 1990s.</p>

</div>

<p>Their relationship began when Strachan was chosen by the London producer Michael Codron to direct the West End premiere of Ayckbourn’s 1974 classic <em><strong><a href="/plays/confusions">Confusions</a></strong></em> in 1976. The production, which starred John Alderton and Pauline Collins, was a success and led to Strachan being asked to direct the West End premiere of <em><strong><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves">Just Between Ourselves</a></strong></em> in 1977.</p>

<p>This production, which starred Colin Blakely as Dennis and Michael Gambon as Neil, was a critical success and would go onto win the Evening Standard Award for Best Play in 1977. At this point, Ayckbourn began to direct the West End revivals of his work with Ayckbourn asking Strachan coming in to direct future change of casts.</p>

<p>Strachan had a particular affinity for Ayckbourn’s classic comedy <strong><em><a href="/plays/how-the-other-half-loves">How The Other Half Loves</a></em></strong> and  is widely agreed to have salvaged its reputation. When it originally premiered in the West End in 1970, the subtleties and innovations of the play were lost in the all consuming presence of the actor Robert Morley, which led to the play being dismissed as a lightweight farce,</p>

<p>In 1976, Strachan revived the play at Wimbledon Theatre with the Actors’ Company in a highly acclaimed ensemble production which demonstrated there was much more to the play than had been assumed from Morley’s production.</p>

<p>Strachan would revive the play at the Greenwich Theatre in 1988, a production which transferred to the West End at the Duke of York’s Theatre the same year. In 2007 he revived it at the Theatre Royal Bath with The Peter Hall Company and again in 2016 at the Theatre Royal Haymarket. The latter transferred into the West End – again at the Duke of York’s Theatre – the same year in a critically acclaimed production.</p>

<p>Alan Strachan was also, in no small way, responsible for the West End success of <strong><em><a href="/plays/seasons-greetings">Season’s Greetings</a></em></strong> in 1982. Ayckbourn – irritated by the West End system – had attempted to bypass it by bringing his world premiere production of the play to The Round House in 1980. This was not a success and, as a result, the producer Michael Codron felt that it had spoilt the play’s chances for a transfer into the West End.</p>

<p>However, Strachan had seen the play and enjoyed it and asked Ayckbourn if he was interested in reviving it at the Greenwich Theatre. He agreed and Codron arranged an option to transfer the production into the West End if it was successful. The play was a huge hit at Greenwich with an ensemble cast which subsequently transferred to an equally successful run in the West End.</p>

<p>Looking to continue the relationship, Strachan and Ayckbourn arranged for the latter’s musical with Paul Todd, <strong><a href="/plays/making-tracks"><em>Making Tracks</em></a></strong> to transfer from Scarborough in 1983, with the hopes of a similar West End transfer. However, it didn’t make an impression with critics but was a huge success at Greenwich apparently enjoying 97% capacity during its five-week run.</p>

<p>Both Alans were later reunited in Scarborough at Ayckbourn’s home theatre, the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/">Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round</a></strong>. Strachan directed Kaufman and Lardner’s <em>June Moon</em> in 1989 and Frayn’s <em>Alphabetical Order</em> in 1990.</p>

<p>Ayckbourn then asked Strachan to be part of the opening year for the company’s present home, the Stephen Joseph Theatre – which opened in 1996 in a £5.2m conversion of the town’s former Odeon cinema.</p>

<p>Strachan agreed and directed one of the first major successes at the new venue with the world premiere of Ben Brown’s acclaimed play <em><strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-39/page-5/page-89/page111.html">All Things Considered</a></strong></em>. The play, starring Christopher Godwin and Susie Blake, marked the highly acclaimed debut by Brown and Strachan would also direct it at the Hampstead Theatre.</p>

<p>In 1999, Strachan returned to Scarborough for a final time to direct <em>Hobson’s Choice</em>, marking the centennial of the actor Charles Laughton birth in the town. This was followed by the world premiere of Ben Brown’s second play, <em><strong>Larkin With Women</strong></em>.</p>

<p>Starring Oliver Ford Davies, Carolyn Backhouse, Susie Blake and Suzy Aitchinson, this was a remarkable hit for the SJT both critically and commercially. Brown’s play told of the life and loves of the poet Philip Larkin and won the TMA Best New Play award. In 2006 Strachan revived it at the Orange Tree Theatre in Richmond to success.</p>

<p>Strachan was also a noted biographer and in addition to writing biographies of Vivien Leigh, Michael Redgrave and Bernard Miles, he also wrote the biography of his long-term collaborator, the West End producer Michael Codron. The book, <em>Putting It On</em>, also offers a definitive account of Alan Ayckbourn and Michael Codron’s long relationship in the West End as well as encompassing Strachan’s experiences with Ayckbourn in the West End.</p>

<p>Alan Strachan died on 10 June, aged 80, at Ninewells Hospital, Dundee.</p>
