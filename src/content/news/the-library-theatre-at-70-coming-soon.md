<p>A glimpse into the history of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round is coming soon to Scarborough Library.</p>

<p>From 1 May to 27 June, Scarborough Library will be hosting <em>The Library Theatre – 70th Anniversary</em>, a photographic exhibition of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, which was founded in 1955 by Stephen Joseph.</p>

</div>

<p>Originally organised for a three-day run in July 1955 by theatre historian and Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, the revamped exhibition now returns for two months.</p>

<p>The exhibition consists of more than 90 photographs, half of which are new for 2026 and many of these are taken from recently discovered negatives which have never been seen by the public before.</p>

<p>Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre ran from 1955 to 1976, originally under the leadership of Stephen Joseph and then his protégé Alan Ayckbourn from  1972. In 1976, the company moved to its second of three homes, the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round, beneath Valley Bridge.</p>

<p>The exhibition celebrates these first 21 years in the library and includes a production photo from each year, behind-the-scenes images of the company both in and out of the theatre, company photographs, images from the final night in 1976, significant people and – new for 2026 – a section dedicated to Alan Ayckbourn with a photograph from all of the plays he premiered at the venue including <em>Relatively Speaking</em>, <em>Absurd Person Singular</em>, <em>The Norman Conquests</em> and <em>Bedroom Farce</em>.</p>

<p>The free exhibition, which has been made possible by North Yorkshire Council and Scarborough Library in association with Simon, will run from Friday 1 May to Saturday 27 June on the first floor of the library in the crush area next to the Concert Room. Normal library opening hours apply to the exhibition.</p>

<p>More details can be found on <strong><a href="http://theatre-in-the-round.com/page-41/page-118/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>, a website which celebrates the most significant first four decades of theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough under Stephen Joseph and Sir Alan Ayckbourn’s direction.</p>

<p>The exhibition is also part of Scarborough 400. which celebrates the 400th anniversary of becoming the town becoming the UK’s first seaside resort and a number of events are planned throughout the year.</p>
