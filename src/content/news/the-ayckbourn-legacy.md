<p>Following one of my recent talks, a member of the audience approached and noted how they never realised how much Alan Ayckbourn had achieved.</p>

<p>Sometimes I think it’s too easy to take these things for granted. As Alan’s archivist, I obviously know how much he has achieved and his incredible contribution to British theatre and playwriting. I know many other people do too.</p>

<p>But sometimes we forget that we have to pass that knowledge onto a broader audience and shout it out – because otherwise it gets forgotten or even downplayed. Far too often, Alan’s achievements are overlooked or not given the credit they deserve.</p>

<p>As there’s just been a weekend celebrating Alan’s achievements at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in Scarborough, I thought it was a good time to shout these things out.</p>

<p>Unapologetically, today I’m going to trumpet the triumph of Alan Ayckbourn and his work. To set down the facts – and they are facts, not opinions – to emphasise just how important Alan Ayckbourn is as a living playwright and why he is regarded as one of the most significant playwrights of the latter half of 20th century theatre.</p>

<p><strong>Alan Ayckbourn</strong></p>

<ul>
<li>Alan Ayckbourn is the only playwright to have received both the Tony and the Olivier Special Awards.</li>

<li>He and his plays have won awards from practically every major UK and North American theatre award body including Oliviers, Tonys, Evening Standard, Critics’ Circle, Plays &amp; Players and Drama Desk.</li>

<li>He was knighted in 1997 specifically for ‘services to theatre’.</li>

<li>He was the first playwright to be knighted since Terence Rattigan in 1971 and was only the fifth playwright to have been knighted up to that point in the 20th century.</li>

<li>He has written 90 full-length plays – all of which have been professionally produced. His writing career, as of 2024, spans 65 years.</li>

<li>His work crosses genres and includes – but is not limited to – tragicomedies, comedies, musicals, thrillers, science fiction, supernatural, fantasy, farce as well as plays for families and children.</li>

<li>More than 70 of his plays have been published – most of them are kept permanently in print by Samuel French.</li>

<li>39 of these 90 plays have been produced either at the National Theatre, in the West End or by the Royal Shakespeare Company.</li>

<li>At one point he held the record for the most plays running simultaneously in the West End with 5 works: <em><strong><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a></strong></em>, <strong><em><a href="/plays/absent-friends">Absent Friends</a></em></strong>, <a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests"><strong><em>Table Manners</em></strong>, <strong><em>Living Together</em> </strong>&amp; <strong>Round and <em>Round the Garden</em></strong>.</a></li>

<li>He is believed to be the only living playwright to have had productions running simultaneously at the National Theatre, in the West End and on the London fringe.</li>

<li>Between 1970 and 2000 – 31 years – there was not a year when there wasn’t an Ayckbourn production in the West End; most years there were multiple productions.</li>

<li>He once held the record for the most plays running simultaneously on Broadway with 4 works: <em>Absurd Person Singula</em>r, <em>Living Together</em>,<em> Table Manners</em> &amp; <em>Round And Round The Garden</em>.</li>

<li><em>Absurd Person Singular</em> was the longest running comedy on Broadway – 1974 to 1976 – since Noël Coward’s <em>Blithe Spirit</em> (1941 – 1942).</li>

<li>His tenure of 37 years as Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre is one of the longest in British theatre history.</li>

<li>As a theatre director, he has directed almost 400 productions since 1961.</li>

<li>He is considered one of the UK’s most experienced writers and directors of theatre in the round having spent practically all of his professional theatre career since 1957 working in, directing and writing for theatre in the round.</li>

<li>He has directed plays for the National Theatre, the RSC, in the West End, on and off Broadway </li>

<li>At his home theatre, the Stephen Joseph Theatre in Scarborough, he has directed more productions during its 69 year history than the combined total of all the company’s other three artistic directors.</li>

<li>There have been more than 25 filmed adaptations of his plays for television and cinema in the UK, USA and France alone.</li>

<li>The BBC alone has produced more than 30 radio adaptations of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays.</li>

<li>He is 85. He is still writing – having finished his play for 2025, he’s been working on his next for 2026! – and still directing.</li>

<li>And if we may become subjective for just one final point. He has inspired myself and – I know – many, many others both in their love of theatre and to pursue careers in playwriting, directing and all other aspects of theatre.</li>
</ul>

<p>I don’t think we need to say any more. By any standard, it’s an extraordinary list of achievements. </p>

<p>And it deserves reiterating and saying out loud and often. Alan Ayckbourn has had a profound effect on British theatre in the past 65 years and is, inarguably, one of the great playwrights of the 20th century.</p>

<p>Let’s not forget that and celebrate alongside the Stephen Joseph Theatre as they mark his 90th play in his 65th year of playwriting.</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd, 2024</em></p>
