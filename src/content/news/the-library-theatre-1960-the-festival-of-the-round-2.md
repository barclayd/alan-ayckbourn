<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1961 and Alan Ayckbourn’s debut as a director.</p>

<p><strong>1961: Debut of a Director</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>It is a strange irony that whilst the majority of people consider Alan Ayckbourn a <strong><a href="/career/playwright">playwright</a></strong> first and foremost, for the majority of his theatrical life, Alan has considered himself a <strong><a href="/career/directing-career">director</a></strong> first and foremost.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn with Stephen Joseph (right) in 1961 (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Objectively, there’s no argument. He may have written a mind-boggling 91 full-length plays, but he has also directed almost 400 productions since 1961. As he frequently pointed out during the ’80s and ’90s, he only spent the equivalent of a month a year writing and the rest of the time directing.</p>

<p>It’s also the part of the process that, historically, he’s enjoyed the most. In interviews throughout his career, he has described writing as a lonely and difficult job and that its main impetus is for the moment when he steps into a rehearsal room with a group of actors and brings the written word to live as a director.</p>

<p>So when, in 1961, Alan Ayckbourn was given his first opportunity to direct, it is easily one of the most significant moments in his long theatrical career and would also have a long-lasting impact on Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre and its subsequent itinerations.</p>

<p>Alan had joined Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1957 as an stage manager with the intention of becoming a professional actor. The company’s founder and Artistic Director, Stephen Joseph, had encouraged Alan to write and he made his playwriting debut in 1959 with <em><strong><a href="/plays/the-square-cat">The Square Cat</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>Stephen had already reached the conclusion that Alan’s future was not as an actor and, soon afterwards, he offered Alan the opportunity to direct, correctly convinced it would move Alan away from acting.</p>

<p>“Stephen Joseph gradually encouraged me to direct in order to put a spoke in the wheels of my acting career, but that is a poisoned chalice for an actor; if they get the taste for directing, they slowly tire of acting because directing is global and you have a view of the entire production.”</p>

<p>Alan had no experience of directing at this point, although he was the most experienced in-the-round actor with the company and had written three plays for it. Naturally, he went to Stephen and asked for his advice, which he has kept close to his heart throughout his professional directing career.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/screenshot-2025-01-13-at-11-26-59-w818.png" alt="" width="818" height="388" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Advertising copy for Alan Ayckbourn’s directorial debut, Gaslight (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>“Stephen said, ‘Directing is very, very simple, all you need to do is to create an atmosphere in which others feel confident to create.’ And I said, ‘Oh, that’s all it is.’</p>

<p>“But, of course, if you’ve got a room full of actors, all moving at different speeds and with different attitudes and with different aims, somehow you’ve got to create an atmosphere in which these disparate individuals feel drawn towards creating, feel confident of creating, so you put them at ease and yet not too much at ease or they go to sleep! You just have to motivate them, encourage them and – above all – give them a purpose.”</p>

<p>On top of all this, Stephen had also thrown Alan in at the deep end by handing him Patrick Hamilton’s Victorian thriller&nbsp;<em><strong><a href="/career/directing-career/gaslight">Gaslight</a></strong></em>, which was conceived as a proscenium arch production. This was all part of Stephen’s ongoing attempts to prove that anything could be produced in the round, even a very traditional end-stage orientated play.</p>

<p>Alan himself had trepidations, but he found theatre-in-the-round opened up possibilities – as he would discover throughout his career – and that the play could be equally as effective in-the-round, shorn of most other props and trappings as he noted in the programme.</p>

<p>“Certainly, at first sight, a Victorian thriller did not seem ideal ‘round’ material…. It took only one or two rehearsals to dispel this illusion.”&nbsp;</p>

<p>And shorn of its trappings, it most certainly was. Alan had asked Stephen about the budget for the play and had been told there was no budget, but he could afford £5 at a push (approximately £285 today adjusted for inflation). For this pitiful amount, Alan had to find Victorian costumes and the essential parts of the set; impressively he managed to find and scrounge a production within budget.</p>

<p>The company operated a repertoire system with two plays performed each week, with each introduced at regular points during the season. Most of the company performed in most of the plays, so rehearsals took place during the day before the company performed in the evening. Rehearsals were normally for one to two weeks at this point, so it was a quick and challenging process to bring plays to performance level very quickly.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>A publicity postcard for Gaslight at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Alan had a strong company which consisted of Hazel Burt, David Jarrett, Stanley Page, Rosamund Dickson and Philip Clifford and the play opened on 29 June 1961; running in repertoire with Peter Shaffer’s <em><strong><a href="/career/actor/five-finger-exercise">Five Finger Exercise</a></strong></em> and David Campton’s comedy of menace anthology <strong><a href="/career/actor/soldier-from-the-wars-returning"><em>Four Minute Warning</em> </a></strong>– both of which Alan was also acting in. The play – like the majority of other productions that year – would play for just two weeks in total.</p>

<p>The production was apparently well-received by audiences and was also reviewed by the influential newspaper, <em>The Stage &amp; Television Today</em>, which wrote “the production, by Alan Ayckbourn, is polished, building with authority from its quiet opening to the chilling of spines as the tension mounts towards hysteria.”</p>

<p>That this was a promising start to a directorial career is obvious, but few would have realised this was to launch a career that would see Alan become one of the pre-eminent in-the-round directors in the world with approximately 400 productions under his belt in the UK, USA and Europe over the next 60 plus years.</p>

<p>This move into directing would have a significant effect on Alan Ayckbourn’s professional career. It essentially led to him giving up professional acting in 1964 to concentrate on writing and directing and was, in no small part, a significant influence on the decision to appoint him as <strong><a href="/career/artistic-director">Artistic Director</a></strong> of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1972 following Stephen Joseph’s death in 1967.</p>

<p>Back to <em>Gaslight</em> in 1961 though, Alan found himself being cited as an example of how Stephen Joseph was creating a new form of theatre company by encouraging members of this company to break out of their boxes and consider all aspects of theatre as possibilities. </p>

<p>The Yorkshire Post published an article by its theatric critic Desmond Pratt on 10 June 1961 looking at the impact theatre-in-the-round had achieved since Stephen Joseph founded the Scarborough company in 1955, including an appraisal of Alan’s progress which he had observed since 1957.</p>

<p>“Alan Ayckbourn, for instance, is only 22, but he started work in the theatre on leaving school and joined Studio Theatre four years ago as a stage manager with a happy knowledge of lighting and sound effects. He has developed into a most sensitive actor, has become a playwright whose works have been staged by Mr Joseph and is now to produce for the first time.”&nbsp;</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Hazel Burt as Mrs Manningham in Gaslight (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>I’m not sure Alan even would know what a ‘happy knowledge’ of something is! But this is then followed by advice as to where Alan Ayckbourn should be heading with his career.</p>

<p>“It will be a loss to theatre-in-the-round but Mr Ayckbourn, for instance, is ready for the commercial theatre and should progress to the proscenium arch. The extra rewards will be both his and this of the larger audiences to which he can appeal.”&nbsp;</p>

<p>We can only be grateful that Alan ignored Mr Pratt’s advice and chose not to embrace the proscenium arch and abandon the theatre which has become so pivotal in his life and work.</p>

<p>The foundations were now in place for Alan’s future. He was an established, produced playwright with his fourth play, <strong><a href="/plays/standing-room-only"><em>Standing Room Only</em></a></strong>, due to open that same summer – and which would be his first work to be optioned for the West End, although it was never produced. He would also increasingly be chosen to direct productions and within three years had completely retired from professional acting.</p>

<p>He would soon begin to concentrate on the two careers he would become renowned and acclaimed for, playwriting and directing. Both of which started and began to flourish at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>
