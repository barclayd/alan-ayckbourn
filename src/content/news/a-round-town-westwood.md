<p>Of the three venues where theatre in the round has been based in Scarborough, the second is the one Alan Ayckbourn now considers his favourite.</p>

<p>In 1976, the company moved from <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> to a – presumed temporary – home at the town’s old Municipal School. It opened in October 1976 as <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/">Theatre in the Round at Westwood</a></strong>.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn &amp; Robin Herford in Theatre in the Round at Westwood (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Within two years, it became obvious, the promised purpose-built new theatre in the round was not going to be delivered by Scarborough Council and Westwood was renamed the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round and became a permanent home for the company.</p>

<p>There, under the Artistic Direction of Alan Ayckbourn, the company gained a national and international reputation for the quality of its work and its commitment to new writing, championing a host of up and coming playwrights such as Stephen Mallatratt, Vanessa Brooks and Tom Firth.</p>

<p>Between 1976 and 1996, the company staged 233 productions in the main ’round’ auditorium and its smaller end-stage studio. Of these, 106 were new plays including the world premiere of <strong><em><a href="http://thewomaninblack.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk">The Woman in Black</a></em></strong>, one of the most successful plays to have ever transferred into the West End.</p>

<p>And Alan Ayckbourn premiered 29 full-length plays there as well as numerous other works such as revues and children’s plays. He also directed approximately half of all the productions staged at Westwood with more than 110 directing credits to his name.</p>

<p>You can find out a lot more about Westwood, the history of the company and the building itself, the people who worked there, the plays which were produced there as well as a year-by-year account alongside photographs and other information at <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>.</p>

<p>The <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/">website</a></strong> looks at the history of theatre in the round in Scarborough between 1955 and 2009 and is curated by Alan Ayckbourn’s Archivist and theatre historian, Simon Murgatroyd M.A.. Within it’s 1,500 pages, you can find details of all three homes of theatre in the round in Scarborough, the plays which were produced, its history as well as detailed sections on Stephen Joseph and Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p>Theatre in the round is a vital part of Scarborough’s cultural history and <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong> explores this significance and legacy as well as looking at what Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round have contributed to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</p>
