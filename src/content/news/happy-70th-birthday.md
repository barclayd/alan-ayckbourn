<p>Today marks the 70th anniversary of the founding of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> in Scarborough.</p>

<p>On 14 July 1955, theatre pioneer <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founded the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company on the first floor of Scarborough Library, dedicated to new writing.</p>

<p>It was a pioneering venture and the company would notably launch the playwriting and directing careers of <strong><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn</a></strong> as well as playing a key role in the early development of writers such as <strong><a href="http://articles.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-75/page-7/styled-16/">David Campton</a></strong>, James Saunders and <strong><a href="http://articles.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-75/page-7/styled-32/">Harold Pinter</a></strong>.</p>

<p>Stephen Joseph ran the company from 1955 to 1965 at the Library Theatre prior to his untimely death in 1967. In 1972, his protege Alan Ayckbourn became the Artistic Director and ran the company for 37 years through the Library Theatre years (until 1976), then to the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-15/">Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round</a></strong> (1976 – 1995) and, finally, to the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-189/page-16/">Stephen Joseph Theatre</a></strong> (1996 – 2009).</p>

<p>The 70th birthday of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was celebrated last week at Scarborough Library with an exhibition looking at the 21 years the company was based at the library.</p>

<p>As curator and organiser of the exhibition, I’d just like to sincerely thank everyone who supported and attended the exhibition alongside the launch of my book <em>A Theatre Revolution – Celebrating Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</em>. Thank you for helping making it such a success.</p>

<p>In particular, many thanks to all the staff at Scarborough Library without whose help and support, the exhibition would never have come to fruition. Thanks to you all. And special thanks to my wife, Mo Murgatroyd, for all her patience, support and help during the many months I have been working on and preparing the exhibition.</p>

<p>And happy 70th birthday to Stephen Joseph’s pioneering company which helped put the Yorkshire seaside resort of Scarborough on the theatrical map!</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd (2025)</em></p>
