<p>Items from the recently donated Ayckbourn Collection are now on display at Scarborough Art Gallery.</p>

<p>The Ayckbourn Collection consists of significant items relating to Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round in Scarborough and was donated to Scarborough Museum &amp; Galleries by Alan Ayckbourn &amp; Simon Murgatroyd with the support of The Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York.</p>

<p>The display includes items such as Alan Ayckbourn’s first play <em>The Square Cat</em>, as well as one of the original manuscripts from <em>The Norman Conquests</em>, a portrait of Sir Alan by the British ‘kitchen sink’ artist’ John Bratby c.1975 and one of Sir Alan’s little-known first night gifts which had been a tradition of the playwright since 1973.</p>

<p>Equal parity is given to Stephen Joseph, Alan’s most significant mentor and who had an immeasurable effect on his career. Stephen Joseph’s designs for the ‘fish and chip theatre’, some of his drawings, a design for Harold Pinter’s professional directorial debut and an early plan of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre are also part of the display.</p>

<p>The donation of the Ayckbourn Collection was curated by Alan Ayckbourn’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, who is thrilled to see the items on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. </p>

<p>“I was absolutely delighted to facilitate the donation of The Ayckbourn Collection to Scarborough Museum and Galleries, but then equally excited when I learnt items from it would be going on display. The team have done a wonderful job creating this fascinating display. I hope the public gets as much pleasure seeing these items of cultural and historical significance to Scarborough – many of them on public display for the first time – as I have.”</p>

<p>The donation consists of approximately 60 items drawn from both the playwright and his archivist’s personal collections, many of which are going on display for the first time in this exhibition at Scarborough Art Gallery.</p>

<p>The Ayckbourn Collection is at Scarborough Art Gallery, which is open from 10am to 5pm every day except Monday (plus Bank Holidays).</p>

<p>Entrance is free with a £3 annual pass, which also allows unlimited free entry to the Rotunda Museum. Further details can be found at the <a href="https://scarboroughmuseumsandgalleries.org.uk">Scarborough Museums and Galleries website</a>.</p>

<p>Further details about the Ayckbourn Collection, including the collection catalogue and an article by Simon Murgatroyd about the collection, can be found on Alan Ayckbourn’s official website <a href="http://www.alanayckbourn.net/page-10/page18.html">here</a> or at the A Round Town website <a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-41/page-134/page18.html">here</a>. Each week, this blog will also be putting items from the collection under the spotlight to reveal their history and significance.</p>

<p>The Ayckbourns are grateful to the Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York, where the Ayckbourn Archive in its entirety is held,  which has kindly enabled the curated Ayckbourn Collection to be donated to Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries by the Ayckbourns and Simon Murgatroyd.</p>
