<p>The creation of one of Alan Ayckbourn’s most famed plays is the focus of the new video <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fh-7bSVj15U&amp;feature=youtu.be">SpotLight on Ayckbourn: Absent Friends</a>.</p>

<p>Premiered in 1974 at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, <em>Absent Friends</em> was Alan Ayckbourn’s direct response to the challenges of writing his previous work, <em>The Norman Conquests</em> trilogy.</p>

<p>A<em>bsent Friends</em> is diametrically opposed to the trilogy with a small-scale, single set play running in two hours of real time. <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fh-7bSVj15U&amp;feature=youtu.be">SpotLight on Ayckbourn</a> looks at how and why Alan Ayckbourn wrote the piece and how it had its own particular challenges.</p>

<p>Using archive material, audio recordings and rarely seen photographs drawn from various archives, <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fh-7bSVj15U&amp;feature=youtu.be">SpotLight on Ayckbourn</a> offers a fascinating insight behind the scenes of <em>Absent Friends</em> with Alan Ayckbourn’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</p>
