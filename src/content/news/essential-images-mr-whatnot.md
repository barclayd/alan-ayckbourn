<p><em>Essential Images is a regular feature on the Official Alan Ayckbourn Blog in which the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, chooses a significant image from the Ayckbourn Archive relating to the plays.</em></p>

<p><em>This week, the image relates to his sixth play, <a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a>, which opened at the Victoria Theatre, Stoke-on-Trent, on 12 November 1963.</em></p>

<p><strong>Essential Images: Mr Whatnot</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p><em>Mr Whatnot</em> is, arguably, the most significant of Alan Ayckbourn’s early works – this written before <em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></em> in 1965. It is the earliest play in the canon that is available to perform and the earliest of his works to have been published. It is also the first of Alan’s plays to transfer to the West End – although that was a less than satisfactory experience.</p>

<p>Today’s Essential Image neither refers to the world premiere or the West End premiere though, but something altogether more unusual and, ultimately, noteworthy.</p>

<p>The photograph is of Alan Ayckbourn directing an amateur production of <em>Mr Whatnot</em> in Leeds with the lead role of Mint being performed by the noted, late stage and screen actor Bob Peck.</p>

<div><figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn &amp; Bob Peck rehearse Mr Whatnot (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure></div>

<p>It’s a fascinating image given that Alan has rarely ventured into amateur theatre territory and that this is Bob Peck’s first leading role on stage – he would go on to take leading roles at the Royal Shakespeare Company and the National Theatre.</p>

<p>So how did Alan Ayckbourn come to direct <em>Mr Whatnot</em> with Bob Peck in an amateur production in Leeds?</p>

<p>As noted, <em>Mr Whatnot</em> premiered in Stoke-on-Trent in 1963 before transferring for a short-lived, critically mauled, two-week run in the West End in 1964 at the New Arts Theatre with a production featuring Ronnie Barker.</p>

<p>The fallout of <em>Mr Whatnot</em>‘s dire reception and performance in the West End saw Alan take a job at the BBC in Leeds as a Radio Drama Producer. He noted at the time, he had little intention of writing for theatre again after his London experience – thankfully he changed his mind soon afterwards.</p>

<p>But Alan was based at the BBC in Leeds for five years and during that time, he agreed to become the patron of Leeds Art Theatre – no doubt following his success in the West End with <em>Relatively Speaking</em> in 1967.</p>

<p>The company had a planned production for November 1968 but this fell through and at which point, a member of the company pleaded with Alan for help. He agreed to direct a production of <em>Mr Whatnot</em> for them and it was one of only three theatre productions Alan directed between 1964 and 1968; remarkable given how prolific as a director he was to become.</p>

<div><figure><figcaption><em><sub>Bob Peck (left) during rehearsals for Mr Whatnot (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></em></figcaption></figure></div>

<p>For the lead role of Mint, he cast Bob Peck, an aspiring actor Alan had met in Leeds and whom he had been given small roles in some of the radio dramas he was producing. The following year, he would bring Bob into the Scarborough company at the Library Theatre for two years, where he would stage-manage, act and appear in the world premiere of two Ayckbourn plays.</p>

<p>The Leeds production of <em>Mr Whatnot</em> was also of note as it produced what was, for many years, the definitive sound plot for this sound effects heavy play. <em>Mr Whatnot </em>features more than 300 sound cues – not so much a problem in the digital age, slightly more challenging in the analogue age of tape players.</p>

<p>It transpired that the original sound reels from the Victoria Theatre had been lost – they were apparently later found – so Alan created an entirely new reel using, surreptitiously, the technology he had available at the BBC alongside one of his co-workers at the BBC, Barrie Davenport. This was undoubtedly the most polished and accomplished sound plot for <em>Mr Whatnot</em> by any company at the time and for many years afterwards would be hired out to for both professional and amateur productions of the play.</p>

<p><em>Mr Whatnot</em> ran at the Civic Theatre in Leeds from 5 November until 9 November. There’s no record of how it did but given the play was being directed by one of the West End’s hottest properties of the time, one imagines it probably did alright for itself!</p>

<p>Alan has only ever directed one other amateur production with <em>Relatively Speaking</em>, again for Leeds Art Theatre, in April 1970. Surprisingly this marked the first time he would direct one of his most famous and popular works.</p>

<p><em>Mr Whatnot </em>would go on to become a surprisingly popular part of the Ayckbourn play canon through the 1970s and 1980s – less so in recent years. Alan himself would revive it as the opening production at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round Scarborough, in 1976.</p>

<div><figure><figcaption><sub>Imelda Staunton &amp; Bob Peck in the National Theatre’s production of A Chorus of Disapproval (© National Theatre)</sub></figcaption></figure></div>

<p>Bob Peck would meanwhile go on to some measure of success as an actor! He and Alan would be reunited in 1985 when he played the lead role of Guy in Alan’s production of <em><a href="/plays/a-chorus-of-disapproval">A Chorus of Disapproval</a></em> at the National Theatre. Initially, the National was against the casting as it was felt Bob was not well enough known. Not for long.</p>

<p>During the run of the production, the BBC broadcast its seminal ’80s drama <em>Edge of Darkness</em> starring Bob and its success was such that by the time <em>A Chorus of Disapproval</em> was set to transfer into the West End, Bob was too famous and in too much demand to transfer. He would go onto great success on stage and screen – although this author will always have fond memories of him not only in <em>Edge of Darkness</em> but as the hunter Robert Muldoon in <em>Jurassic Park</em> who comes to a sticky end at the claws of the velociraptors… “Clever girl…”</p>

<p>So that’s how Alan Ayckbourn came to be directing Bob Peck in an amateur production of <em>Mr Whatnot</em> in Leeds. Largely still known only due to the existence of several photographs taken during rehearsals. That’s why they are, to me, essential images.</p>
