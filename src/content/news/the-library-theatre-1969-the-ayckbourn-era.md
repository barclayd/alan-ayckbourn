<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1969 and the return of Alan Ayckbourn to the Library Theatre and the first steps towards re-shaping the company for the future.</p>

<p><strong>1969: The Ayckbourn Era </strong>by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>In 1967, the first phase of the Stephen Joseph Theatre’s life essentially came to a close with the death of its founder, Stephen Joseph.</p>

<p>He had founded the company in 1955 as the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company, predicated on promoting new work by new playwrights. At the end of the summer 1965 season, he had closed Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre with no intention it should return.</p>

<p>Yet, in 1967 – with little money but a lot of good will and determination – it returned. Albeit on shaky ground and with Stephen Joseph dying later that same year, no long term plan for survival or progression.</p>

<p>That would arguably come in 1969 with the return of the company’s most well-known protégé – Alan Ayckbourn, laying the foundations for the most successful and significant and continued period of the company’s existence.</p>

<p>But all this needs to be put into context and how what is generally taken for granted as part of the history of the Stephen Joseph Theatre was never a given and is actually more noteworthy than has previously been given credit.</p>

<p>In 1962, Alan Ayckbourn left Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in Scarborough with most of the Studio Theatre company to help launch the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong> in Stoke-on-Trent, the UK’s first permanent professional in-the-round venue.</p>

<p>It’s fair to say that although the previous five years had had a huge impact on Alan’s life and professional career, there doesn’t appear to be evidence of Alan being particularly wedded to Scarborough and Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre; his loyalty was to Stephen Joseph rather than a specific place or venue.</p>

<p>Alan only stayed with the company at Stoke until 1964 when his first London transfer took place with the play <strong><em><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></em></strong>. The failure of which saw Alan join the <strong><a href="/career/bbc-career">BBC</a></strong> as a Radio Drama Producer based in Leeds in 1965 whilst considering leaving theatre for good.</p>

<p>Most accounts of the history of the Stephen Joseph Theatre gloss over this period – even Alan Ayckbourn’s official biography does not place much weight on this period nor the fact the links between Alan Ayckbourn and Scarborough became quite thin.</p>

<p>Between Alan Ayckbourn leaving Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre at the end of summer 1962 and the spring of 1969, he had only a nominal involvement with the company. During these seven years, he wrote and premiered just two plays for Scarborough and only directed one.</p>

<p>Records of Scarborough Theatre Trust also do not mention Alan as being part of the theatre’s board until the Annual General Meeting of 7 December 1969; in reality he probably joined earlier in that year but there are no minutes held in archive for the December 1968 board meeting or AGM.</p>

<p>Which is to say, although Alan had kept contact with Scarborough, his actual connection with the theatre was not terribly strong. During the latter months of Stephen Joseph’s life in 1967, he did spend time with his mentor but other than directing his latest play, <strong><em><a href="/plays/the-sparrow">The Sparrow</a></em></strong>, that summer, there is nothing to indicate he was actively involved in Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in any other way.</p>

<p>In fact, if we look at his work with the BBC, he was obviously fully immersed in his role as radio producer, producing more than 50 broadcast dramas during the course of that year alone.</p>

<p>Yet this is despite the fact that, realistically, he must have been viewed by the theatre board at that point as one of the few people capable of taking Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre forward, certainly given the company was operating on a year-by-year basis and appointments.</p>

<p>Preceding Stephen’s death by serval months, Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre had re-opened on a limited basis during the summer of 1967 following his decision to close the theatre in 1965.</p>

<p>For the 1967 and 1968 seasons, the role of Artistic Director (then Director of Productions) was appointed annually and taken by another of Stephen’s protégés Rodney Wood – the man who had initially employed Alan Ayckbourn at the Library Theatre. But it was obvious a more permanent solution was needed, particularly given the theatre was intent on finding a new home.</p>

<p>In the Scarborough Theatre Trust minutes of 17 March 1968, the need for a permanent Artistic Director is discussed not least in order to work in conjunction with the theatre’s architect, George Alderson, in planning for the proposed – but unrealised – move of the company to the former Pindar print works building on Castle Road.</p>

<p>Sometime between then and early 1969, Alan Ayckbourn was approached about the annual appointment of the Director of Productions at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>The timing is significant as it seems unlikely that anyone would have though he was the long-term future of the company when he left in 1962. He had had success as playwright and director, but David Campton was closer to Stephen, had managed theatre and also had more plays produced than Alan. There was the more obvious successor.</p>

<p>Things had changed considerably though. By 1969, Alan was the most visible success of Stephen Joseph’s company and, significantly, he had shot to fame with the West End premiere of <em><strong><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></strong></em> in 1967; from which the theatre had also benefitted financially thanks to royalties from the production.</p>

<p>It seems no great leap to think the board had begin to potentially see Alan as a long-term solution to the future of the company. He was successful and well-known, he had a long association with the company where he had debuted as playwright and director and, the icing on the cake, he now had considerable management experience due to his work with the BBC.</p>

<p>He was, essentially, the ideal candidate.</p>

<p>Thus in 1969, he was offered and accepted the role of Director of Productions for the summer; although with finances still precarious at the theatre, he was only offered accommodation for the season in lieu of pay for the role.</p>

<p>Alan balanced his job at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre with his full-time contract at the BBC that year when, apparently, his secretary at the BBC would frequently take calls and have to surreptitiously forward them to him in Scarborough!</p>

<p>Alan would be Director of Productions for 1969 and 1970, taking a year away for the American launch of <em><strong><a href="/plays/how-the-other-half-loves">How The Other Half Loves</a></strong></em> in 1971. He also left the BBC in early 1970 when it became known he was now looking for a permanent theatre role.</p>

<p>He would return to Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1972 as Director of Productions when, at the end of the summer season, he was offered a permanent contract and the position of Artistic Director of the company. Then the company’s future was secured.</p>

<p>But the long term future of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre had begun to look more steady from that point in 1969 when Alan accepted the role of Director of Productions.</p>

<p>For then, the theatre was able to benefit from someone who appreciated Stephen Joseph’s legacy and wanted to continue in those footsteps, who was making his own name within theatre in a substantive way and who could help financially support the theatre given every play of Alan’s which opened at the Library and then transferred to the West End, brought in royalties to the venue.</p>

<p>But until that point, it should never be assumed the future of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was safe nor that the man most associated it with, Alan Ayckbourn, was necessarily always going to take over from Stephen Joseph.</p>
