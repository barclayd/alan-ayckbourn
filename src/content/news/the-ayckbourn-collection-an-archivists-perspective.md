<p><em>Last week it was announced Scarborough Museums and Galleries had acquired The Ayckbourn Collection for the town.</em></p>

<p><em>This is the only public collection relating to the playwright in his adopted home-town and items from which will be going on display at Scarborough Art Gallery from 16 September.</em></p>

<p><em>Here the playwright’s archivist, Simon Murgatroyd M.A. – who curated and facilitated the collection and its donation – gives his perspective of the journey and why he feels it’s significant for Scarborough to have this collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>The Ayckbourn Collection: An Archivist’s Perspective</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd M.A.</p>

<p>It has long been my ambition to see a collection relating to the playwright and director Alan Ayckbourn held in his adopted home town of Scarborough.</p>

<div>

</div>

<p>I’ve been the playwright’s archivist since 2005 and in 2011 saw the huge and comprehensive Ayckbourn Archive acquired for the nation by the Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York. Ever since, I’ve hoped that some smaller collection could also be held in the town which has become synonymous with the playwright.</p>

<p>True, the Stephen Joseph Theatre, with which Alan Ayckbourn has been associated since 1957 and of which he was the Artistic Director of for 37 years,  holds an extensive archive with material relating to Alan Ayckbourn – particularly subsequent to the mid 1970s. But it is, understandably, a private archive primarily for the SJT’s own internal use.</p>

<p>Until this year, nothing relating to Alan Ayckbourn and the contribution he, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round have contributed to the cultural heritage and history of Scarborough was held in a publicly accessible archive in the town. It seemed vital to me that this should be rectified for Alan is, indisputably, a key figure in the history of Scarborough’s culture.</p>

<p>Post-lockdown and with my work as Sir Alan’s Archivist increasingly feeding into the wider Scarborough community, I realised this was the right time to begin realising this ambition. With the support and agreement of the Borthwick Institute for Archives, I began to curate a small but significant collection of approximately 50 items which could be donated to Scarborough.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/square-2020-c2a9-tony-bartholomew-w300.jpg" alt="" width="300" height="300" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn ©Tony Bartholomew <a href="http://www.bartpics.co.uk">http://www.bartpics.co.uk</a></sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>I then outlined my plans and proposals to the Ayckbourn themselves, who were incredibly supportive of the idea and for me to allocate time to work on the project. Thus I began to explore what was available and to curate something which, by necessity, had to be quite a limited collection, but which would hold items of significance to Alan Ayckbourn, theatre in the round and Scarborough.</p>

<p>The collection, of course, began with the key item of an original manuscript of Alan Ayckbourn’s first play, <em>The Square Cat</em> – which premiered in Scarborough at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1959 – and developed from there, encompassing a number of rare manuscripts – such as a complete set of original rehearsals scripts for the world premiere of <em>The Norman Conquests</em> trilogy – with occasional additions of items from my personal collection. Some rare examples of posters and programmes also seemed sensible as well as published editions of his work from around the world, illustrating just how far spread these plays have become which originate in Scarborough.</p>

<p>It also seemed to me vital to include material relating to Stephen Joseph, founder of the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough, and the person who is the single most influential person in Alan Ayckbourn’s life – both mentor and father figure. Without Stephen Joseph, I believe inarguably, Alan Ayckbourn would not have become the success and significant theatre figure he is regarded as today.</p>

<p>Key among this material was the decision to donate Stephen Joseph’s unrealised plans for his ‘Fish and Chip Theatre’, a design specifically inspired by his time in Scarborough which was both hugely innovative in its design and aspirations but also clearly showed his desire for the democratisation of theatre to make it appealing and approachable to as wide an audience as possible.</p>

<p>Other items suggested themselves, Stephen’s ground-plan for the layout of the Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre soon after it opened in 1955, a design by Stephen for Harold Pinter’s directorial debut with his play <em>The Birthday Party</em>, which rehearsed in Scarborough Library and Stephen’s own scrapbook pertaining to the early years of his work in Scarborough.</p>

<p>In January 2023, I approached the Chief Executive of Scarborough Museums and Galleries, Andrew Clay, who showed immense enthusiasm about acquiring the collection for the town and as I met other members of the team – particularly the Collections Manager Jim Middleton – it became obvious this enthusiasm was shared and this was the right place for the Collection.</p>

<p>Along the way, it opened the door to a portrait of Sir Alan by John Bratby being loaned to the Art Gallery for an exhibition, which in itself led to the portrait becoming part of the permanent donation.</p>

<p>This all came together in August 2023 with the announcement of the acquisition of The Ayckbourn Collection by Scarborough Museums and Galleries. Six months of work but a dream of many years finally realised.</p>

<p>It also coincided with the recent launch of my new website, A Round Town at <a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk"><strong>www.a-round-town.com</strong></a> which looks at the history of theatre in the round in Scarborough. A fortuitous coincidence, but one which further highlights the importance of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to the cultural heritage and history of Scarborough.</p>

<p>I’m delighted the Ayckbourn Collection has a home in Scarborough and that I’ve been able to contribute to it both in terms of facilitating it, but also donating items. I hope, in the years to come the collection will expand and  I hope to be working with it and Scarborough Museums and Galleries, exploring just what theatre in the round and Alan Ayckbourn means to Scarborough.</p>

<p>I’d like to thank Gary Brannan at the Borthwick Institute for supporting the acquisition and Andrew Clay and his team at Scarborough Museums and Galleries for their enthusiasm for acquiring the Collection for Scarborough.</p>

<p>And, of course, to Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn for listening to and supporting my idea for The Ayckbourn Collection and donation and allowing me to facilitate this wonderful new addition to Scarborough’s collections. It’s been a privilege to have initiated and guided this project.</p>

<p>Simon Murgatroyd, August 2023</p>

<p><em>Items from The Ayckbourn Collection are on display in Scarborough Art Gallery from 16 September to January</em>. <em>Admission to Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries is £5 for an annual pass to all venues.</em></p>
