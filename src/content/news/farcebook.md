<p>Some of you may be wondering why the Alan Ayckbourn Facebook page suddenly vanished this week.</p>

<p>You’re not alone.</p>

<p>I am too.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/facebook-block-w300.png" alt="" width="300" height="294" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
</div>

<p>Earlier in the week, our ‘benevolent’ digital overlords decided that the page had broken ‘Community Standards’.</p>

<p>If you’re familiar with stories such as this, you know how it goes. No specific reasons are given for the suspension and you have one appeal – but can’t address what happened (if you knew what it was anyway) or offer to rectify whatever it is you’ve done wrong. This is followed by an anonymous and – essentially, as there is nothing to suggest otherwise – arbitrary ruling, which you are not privy to, which then closes your page account.</p>

<p>Astonishing. Or perhaps not.</p>

<p>This was despite the fact there was nothing posted that day and if something was objectively wrong, I can’t imagine it compares much to the truly big and important issues Facebook and the other social media are happy to either ignore or just profit from (or mostly both).</p>

<p>So there is no longer – nor will there ever be again – an official Ayckbourn Facebook page. Over the past year I’ve realised – like many others, I suspect – there are very few benefits to having a presence on the site and most of those are outweighed by the drawbacks.</p>

<p>Facebook doesn’t contribute meaningfully to any of the work I do, so the Ayckbourn page is now closed. If you see an ‘official Ayckbourn page’ from now on Facebook, it’s fake.</p>

<p>My main social media interactions will remain here – on WordPress – and occasionally on Instagram (<strong><a href="https://www.instagram.com/ayckbourn_playwright/">Ayckbourn_Playwright</a></strong>), but I’ll be decreasing that too over the coming months. On Facebook itself, my <strong><a href="https://www.facebook.com/simon.murgatroyd">personal page</a></strong> – will be highlighting my own work and upcoming Ayckbourn and theatre events which I’m involved with.</p>

<p>It does mean though, this blog is more important than ever and does need your support. Please keep reading it and recommending it</p>

<p>In the meantime, you can also find news and what’s on – as always – on Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website <strong><a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a></strong>.</p>

<p>Thanks, as ever, for your support of the blog and for reading.</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd (16/08/25)</em></p>
