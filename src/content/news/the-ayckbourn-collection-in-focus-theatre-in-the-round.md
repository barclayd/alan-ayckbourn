<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items within the display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>The Theatre in the Round Plan</strong></p>

<p>In 1955, Stephen Joseph founded the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company based in the Concert Room on the first floor of Scarborough Library.</p>

<p>Why the first professional company? It’s a question I’ve been frequently asked and the answer is that this was not a permanent or purpose-built venue. Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre initially operated for just short summer seasons at the Library Theatre (between 1955 and 1973) as well as shorter winter seasons (1957 – 1961). It was only from 1974 onwards that the company approximated anything like a year-round schedule.</p>

<p>So it was a professional theatre-in-the-round company based in a temporary space which had to be adapted each season into a theatre-in-the-round. The designs for this were predominantly done by Stephen Joseph himself with some input from the architect Stephen Garrett.</p>

<p>Within The Ayckbourn Collection, there is one of the earliest surviving plans for Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre – currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery – and reproduced below. This one is from 1958 and shows how theatre in the round worked in the Concert Room space.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/1958-rostrum-plan-c2a9-haydonning-ltd-w700.png" alt="" width="700" height="495" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>

<p>The Concert Room was never a completely ideal space for theatre in the round as the only two entrances were on the same side and not very far apart. This meant one of the seating blocks was tiny compared to the rest – and without careful stage direction, designs like this can often lead to the equivalent block being viewed as the least important, when all sides should be equally important in theatre in the round.</p>

<p>This plan shows how Stephen Joseph’s portable rostra, designed and created in 1957, were arranged to build up the seating area into four rows. The coloured yellow area is the stage area – which still exists today – and had to be incorporated into the design.</p>

<p>The plan is notable because it is one of Stephen Joseph’s actual designs – as demonstrated by his monogram in the lower right-hand corner. It is also a rarity. Another of Stephen’s plans exists for 1960, held in the Ayckbourn Archive at the Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York.</p>

<p>The only earlier plans are held in the Stephen Joseph and The Library Theatre Collection at Scarborough Library. These include earlier plans drawn up in 1956 and 1957. They also hold Stephen Joseph’s original letter in which he first sketches how the Concert Room will look like as a theatre in the round (see image).</p>

<p>What is interesting is anyone visiting the Concert Room today will see a room which still looks essentially the same as it did in 1955. The colour scheme may have altered, but the floor, staging, two entrances and the ceiling details are all the same. Stepping into the space is like taking a step back in time and standing in the same space where theatrical history was made.</p>

<p>All these plans are significant pieces of both Scarborough’s theatre history and also national theatre history. Professional theatre in the round has been running longer in Scarborough than anywhere else in the UK and Stephen’s designs and ideas would go on to become very influential in theatre designs from the 1960s onwards.</p>

<p>And, as such, when discussing theatre in the round and its history in the UK, it tends to lead back to Scarborough in 1955 where Stephen Joseph created the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company which still survives to this day.</p>
