<p>There’s still time to take a peek into British theatre history with The Library Theatre at 70 exhibition at Scarborough Library.</p>

<p>The exhibition charts the history of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-roiund – Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre – founded in 1955 by Stephen Joseph. It would go on to become synonymous with Alan Ayckbourn and the company continues today in tis third home, the Stephen Joseph Theatre.</p>

<p>The exhibition, curated by Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist and theatre historian Simon Murgatroyd M.A., is running until the end of June and features 90 photographs taken over the 21 years The Library Theatre existed.</p>

<p>The exhibition celebrates includes a production photo from each year between 1955 and 1976, behind-the-scenes images of the company both in and out of the theatre, company photographs, images from the final night in 1976, significant people and – new for 2026 – a section dedicated to Alan Ayckbourn with a photograph from all of the plays he premiered at the venue including <em>Relatively Speaking</em>, <em>Absurd Person Singular</em>, <em>The Norman Conquests</em> and <em>Bedroom Farce</em>.</p>

<p>The exhibition features many photographs which have only recently been discovered and many of which have never before been shown to the public. They include a number of famous people associated with the company such as Alan Ayckbourn, Tom Balker, Robert Powell, Stephanie Turner and John Nettles.</p>

<p>Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre ran from 1955 to 1976, originally under the leadership of Stephen Joseph and then his protégé Alan Ayckbourn from  1972. In 1976, the company moved to its second of three homes, the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round, beneath Valley Bridge.</p>

<p>The free exhibition, which has been made possible by North Yorkshire Council and Scarborough Library in association with Simon, is running until Saturday 27 June on the first floor of the library. Normal library opening hours apply to the exhibition.</p>

<p>More details can be found on <strong><a href="http://theatre-in-the-round.com/page-41/page-118/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>, a website which celebrates the most significant first four decades of theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough under Stephen Joseph and Sir Alan Ayckbourn’s direction.</p>
