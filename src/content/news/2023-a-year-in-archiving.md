<p>Its suddenly struck me in the New Year that despite being Alan Ayckbourn’s Archivist for the past 21 years, I’ve rarely blogged about my actual work as an archivist!</p>

<p>All of which will change in 2024. I’m going to do a monthly column on my work or discoveries relating to Alan Ayckbourn and theatre in the round over the previous month.</p>

<p>But to kick off, I thought I’d just have a look back at some of the highlights of my job in 2023 – an extraordinary year for me which brought exciting new connections and developments alongside the opportunity to meet a lot of wonderful people.</p>

<p>So here’s some highlights – which might also offer an insight into the variety of my work!</p>

<p><strong>◦ The donation of the Ayckbourn Collection to Scarborough Museums and Galleries:</strong> This was an absolute highlight of my year beginning with discussing with the Ayckbourn’s my proposal to gift Scarborough a curated collection of significant material relating to Alan, Stephen Joseph and Theatre in the Round. I then took the proposal to Scarborough Museums and Galleries, whose entire team led by CEO Andrew Clay were incredibly enthusiastic and supportive. And no sooner had the collection been donated, then Scarborough Art Gallery mounted a display of the work placing it into the context of Scarborough’s culture heritage and history. I spent many an hour talking to people about the exhibition and am incredibly proud of being associated with Scarborough Museums and Galleries on this. And to top it all, the exhibition has been extended and re-opens to the public after a short break on Saturday!</p>

<p><strong>◦ Launching my first public talks:</strong> 2023 was the year I decided to get out and about and to start telling some of the amazing stories about Alan and theatre in the round in Scarborough which I love. I launched them with the first public talk at Beeforth’s Haven the South Cliff, spoke in the Concert Room at Scarborough Library – where theatre in the round began in Scarborough in 1955 – for Heritage Open Days, talked to Scarborough Civic Society, led tours of the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Art Gallery and was live-streamed across the world from Filey Library! Along the way, I had the chance to meet people who had known Stephen Joseph or visited the Library Theatre back in the day – all with their own stories to tell and treasure. I’ve loved doing my talks and I’m looking forward to doing more in 2024 (quick plug there!)</p>

<p><strong>◦ A new website with A Round Town <a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk">http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk</a>:</strong> I’m all for creating rods for my own back. So you’ve probably come across my little website called <a href="/"><strong>www.alanayckbourn.net</strong></a> with its 3,500 pages on the playwright and his plays. That celebrated its 21st anniversary in 2023 and continues to grow and expand. But, I thought, we also need a website that puts theatre in the round in Scarborough in context and acts as a sister site to Alan Ayckbourn’s site. So I created A Round Town at <a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk"><strong>www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk</strong></a> which celebrates the history of theatre in the round from 1955 to 2009, celebrating the various homes of the company founded by Stephen Joseph in 1955 and its contribution to Scarborough’s cultural heritage. The result is a deep-dive into the history, people and plays focused on the company’s first two homes at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (1955 – 1976) and the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round (1976 – 1996). And it’s just the start, there’s already about a 1,000 pages and more will be added in the future as I celebrate Scarborough’s significant place as – essentially – the birthplace of the professional theatre in the round movement in the UK and one of the oldest continuous new writing venues in the UK (which precedes the Royal Court by a year!)</p>

<p><strong>◦ The expansion of the Ayckbourn Archive at the Borthwick Institute for Archives:</strong> The Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York acquired the Ayckbourn Archive for the nation in 2011 and holds tens of thousands of items from 1959 to 2006 from scripts to correspondence, notes to programmes and much, much more. In 2023, a second tranche of material added archival items from 2006 to 2019 adding several thousand more items to the collection. This includes – for the first time – archive multimedia items such as videos and reel-to-reels. The latter have already uncovered some material unheard since the 1970s and in 2024, there’ll be an appeal to help safeguard and digitally convert this extensive collection of audio material created by Alan Ayckbourn for the world premieres of his plays from the 1960s to 1980s. More details soon about this. As always, it’s been a pleasure to work on the archive with Gary Brannan, Keeper of Archives and Special Collections, and to spend nearly as much time (!) discussing <em>Doctor Wh</em>o as Alan Ayckbourn with a fellow afficiando…</p>

<p><strong>◦ The cataloguing of the Theatre in the Round collection at Scarborough Library:</strong> This is an ongoing job, stated in late 2023, which has revealed some startling discoveries concerning Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre and its formative years under Stephen Joseph. Scarborough Library holds a small but very significant collection relating to theatre in the round in town. There is correspondence from Stephen Joseph, his plans for the theatre and an unrivalled collection of original programmes for the company. I’ll be writing more about my work here soon, but this has been reel gem for me, cataloguing and sorting one of the most important and significant collections relating to theatre in the round in Scarborough. I’m delighted to have had the opportunity to work with Scarborough Library on this and how welcoming they’ve been.</p>

<p>What a year! And those were just the highlights. Check back each month for more insights into my work as an archivist and – fingers crossed – some of the theatrical rarities and treasures I might discover relating to Scarborough and theatre in the round this year.</p>
