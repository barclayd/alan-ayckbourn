<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p>In part 2, we’re looking at questions regarding Alan Ayckbourn’s writing career…</p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Writing</strong></p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn in 1976 (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p><strong>1) How many plays has Alan Ayckbourn written?<br></strong>As of 2023, Alan has written 89 produced full-length plays. By the time you read this, there may be more… A complete list of Alan’s full-length plays as well as his other writing can be found&nbsp;<a href="/plays/childrens-plays-index">here</a>.</p>

<p><strong>2) What was Alan Ayckbourn’s first play?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn’s first full-length, commissioned and produced play was <em><a href="/plays/the-square-cat">The Square Cat</a></em>, which opened at <a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/theatre-in-the-round-at-the-library-theatre">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a> on 30 July 1959. Prior to this, the playwright estimates he had written approximately a dozen unproduced plays of various lengths, only several of which have survived and are held in archive.</p>

<p><strong>3) Why did Alan Ayckbourn write his first play?</strong><br>Alan wrote his first play after complaining about the quality of his acting roles to&nbsp;<a href="/life/stephen-joseph">Stephen Joseph</a>, the Artistic Director of the&nbsp;Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre&nbsp;in Scarborough, who promptly challenged Alan to write a better role for himself and if the play was any good, he would stage it. Alan wrote <em>The Square Cat</em>, given himself star-billing and the rest if history.</p>

<p>For many years it was stated the play which drove Alan to complain was Jon Van Druten’s&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/actor/bell-book-and-candle">Bell, Book And Candle</a></em>. However, his biographer Paul Allen conclusively proved it was actually David Campton’s play&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/actor/ring-of-roses">Ring Of Roses</a></em>. As David Campton was a friend, Alan may have changed the ‘inspirational’ role during interviews so as not hurt Campton’s feelings.</p>

<p><strong>4) Is it true Alan Ayckbourn writes his plays in less than a week?<br></strong>It used to be. For much of his career throughout the ’60s to ’90s, Alan Ayckbourn wrote his plays very quickly, often in several days to a week. However, this must be seen in the context that he would often be thinking about them for upwards of a year. So by the time he put pen to paper or fingers to keyboard, the plays were essentially fully-formed in his head.</p>

<p>Following his stroke in 2006, Alan slowed down a bit and now spends several weeks writing his plays before returning to polish and finalise.</p>

<p>Famously, between 1960 and 1986, Alan also wrote to the latest possible deadline, generally starting writing the week before rehearsals were due to start! This ended when he had to submit his play <em>A Small Family Business</em> to the National Theatre a year in advance in 1986, leading him to begin writing to earlier deadlines.</p>

<p><strong>5) Why is there confusion about the number of plays?<br></strong>There isn’t! Alan Ayckbourn and his archivist know precisely how many plays he has written. Any inconsistencies or confusion are down purely to a lack of research or an over-reliance on unreliable sites such as Wikipedia. The correct number of plays can be found on the playwright’s website <a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a> and a complete play-list can be found&nbsp;<a href="/plays/childrens-plays-index">here</a>.</p>

<p><strong>6) Alan Ayckbourn is frequently labelled as a farceur, is this correct?<br></strong>No. It was a label lazily applied to Alan Ayckbourn early in his career by critics largely on the basis of the West End productions of his plays directed by people other than the playwright in which farcical elements may have been emphasised. Alan Ayckbourn believes he has written only one true, full-length farce with&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/taking-steps">Taking Steps</a></em>; although there are heavy elements of farce in&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/how-the-other-half-loves">How The Other Half Loves</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/love-after-all">Love After All</a></em>,&nbsp;<em>The Square </em><i>Cat</i> and <em><a href="/plays/birthdays-past-birthdays-present">Birthdays Past, Birthdays Present</a></em>. He has also written two one-act farces which together form&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/chloe-with-love-farcicals">Farcicals</a></em>. Whilst elements of farce do appear in his writing, Alan Ayckbourn’s plays do not objectively meet the requirements of pure farce. He now labels all his work as just ‘plays’.</p>

<p>And, before you say it,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce">Bedroom Farce</a></em>&nbsp;is not a farce; Alan just liked the title.</p>

<p><strong>7) Is Alan Ayckbourn a political writer?<br></strong>No. He is apolitical in his writing and has spoken frequently about this throughout his career. In 2019, he noted: ‘My characters have no politics because I don’t really.’ His writing reflects his own lack of interest in politics and he has rarely voted and, even then, out of no loyalty to any particular political party. Some of his plays could be considered state of the nation plays, such as <em><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business">A Small Family Business</a></em>, but he doesn’t consider them particularly political. Some of Alan’s quotes about his thoughts on politics can be found&nbsp;<a href="http://biography.alanayckbourn.net/styled-37/page-45/page-40/page39.html">here</a>.</p>

<p>The one exception is in 2023 a rehearsed reading of his 2020 play <em><a href="/plays/truth-will-out">Truth Will Out</a></em> – which was scheduled but cancelled due to the Covid Pandemic – revealed what could be dubbed his first political play with an angry look at the state of the country. However, as the play has only been seen once, it doesn’t really count in the context of the question.</p>

<p><strong>8) Why have the early plays never been published?<br></strong>Alan has frequently said that every writer has a period when they are learning their craft and that these plays represent that period; as a result he would not be happy to have them published and definitely not performed.</p>

<p><strong>9) Alan has said he tried to destroy all his early plays, is this true?<br></strong>This was probably said in jest as at least one copy of all of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays are now known to exist. However, Alan personally chose not to keep copies of his early plays, which led to the difficulty in tracing several of the early plays. Between 2005 and 2007, Alan’s archivist&nbsp;<a href="http://www.simon-murgatroyd.com">Simon Murgatroyd</a>&nbsp;recovered copies of all the previously thought lost scripts. A complete collection of Alan Ayckbourn’s manuscripts is now held in the&nbsp;<a href="http://www.alanayckbourn.net/page-10/page18.html">Ayckbourn Archive</a>&nbsp;at the Borthwick Institute for Archives at The University Of York.<br><br><strong>10) Which plays are not available for production and have not been published?<br></strong>The definitive list of plays which are now considered permanently withdrawn and unavailable for production are:&nbsp;<em>The Square Cat</em>,&nbsp;<em>Love After All</em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/dads-tale">Dad’s Tale</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/standing-room-only">Standing Room Only</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind">Christmas V Mastermind</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/the-sparrow">The Sparrow</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/jeeves">Jeeves</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/the-musical-jigsaw-play">The Musical Jigsaw Play</a></em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em><a href="http://virturalreality.alanayckbourn.net">Virtual Reality</a></em>. None of these plays have been published. As of writing, only&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/making-tracks">Making Tracks</a></em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/miss-yesterday">Miss Yesterday</a></em>&nbsp;also remain unpublished with all other plays published or due to be published.<br><br><strong>11) Which of Alan Ayckbourn’s full length plays have been specifically written for production in the end-stage?<br></strong>There are six Alan Ayckbourn plays which were originally written with end-stage performance in mind (although several of these have been performed in the round). These plays are:&nbsp;<em>Bedroom Farce</em>,&nbsp;<em>A Small Family Business</em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/haunting-julia">Haunting Julia</a></em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/things-we-do-for-love">Things We Do For Love</a></em>,&nbsp;<em>Virtual Reality&nbsp;</em>and<em>&nbsp;<a href="/plays/all-lies">All Lies</a></em>. Although&nbsp;<em>Jeeves&nbsp;</em>and&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/house-and">House</a></em>&nbsp;were originally performed as end-stage plays, unlike the other plays they were not specifically written for end-stage performance.<br>The vast majority of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays were written for performance in-the-round, but can be easily adapted to other staging configurations – with the exception of&nbsp;<em>Taking Steps</em>, which is intended specifically for performance in-the-round.<br><br><strong>12) What are the ‘Grey Plays’?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn has written a number of plays which have been produced and performed, but which are not considered part of the official canon. Generally this is because the plays were written for a specific and unique event (<em>An Evening With Palos</em>&nbsp;for The Colin Blakely Memorial;&nbsp;<em>Untitled Farce</em>&nbsp;for the 50th anniversary of the Stephen Joseph Theatre) or because they were from Alan’s earliest days as a writer and represent formative steps as a professional writer. Whilst all the plays have been produced in some form, they have never been published and are no longer available for production. The ‘Grey Plays’ are:&nbsp;<em>Double Hitch</em>&nbsp;(one act, 1960);&nbsp;<em>Love Undertaken</em>&nbsp;(one act, 1961);&nbsp;<em>Follow The Lover</em>&nbsp;(one act, 1962);&nbsp;<em>Dracula&nbsp;</em>(one act, 1975);&nbsp;<em>The Jubilee Show&nbsp;</em>(revue, 1977);&nbsp;<em>Backnumbers</em>&nbsp;(revue, 1983);&nbsp;<em>An Evening With PALOS</em>&nbsp;(one act, 1987);&nbsp;<em>The Fearsome Threesome</em>&nbsp;(one act monologue, 1989);&nbsp;<em>Ron &amp; Julie</em>&nbsp;(one act sketch, 1991);&nbsp;<em>Between The Lines</em>&nbsp;(revue by Paul Todd incorporating Ayckbourn songs, 1992);&nbsp;<em>Untitled Farce</em>&nbsp;(one act, 2005); <em>Truth Will Out </em>(full length, 2023). Full details can be found in the&nbsp;<a href="http://plays.alanayckbourn.net/page17/styled-10/index.html">Plays</a>&nbsp;section.</p>

<p><strong>13) Is there an Ayckbourn trilogy called&nbsp;<em>Things That Go Bump</em>?<br></strong>No. It has inaccurately been reported that Alan Ayckbourn’s three supernatural plays form a trilogy called&nbsp;<em>Things That Go Bump;&nbsp;</em>mainly incorrectly perpetuated by Wikipedia. However, the plays have never been referred to by this title by the playwright himself, Alan Ayckbourn, nor does he consider them a trilogy.</p>

<p>The misunderstanding arises from the fact the three plays (<em>Haunting Julia</em>,&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/snake-in-the-grass">Snake In The Grass</a></em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/life-and-beth">Life &amp; Beth</a></em>) were presented in a season called&nbsp;<em>Things That Go Bump</em>&nbsp;at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, in 2008. However, this title was purely for marketing purposes and was never considered as an overarching name for the three plays by the playwright himself.</p>

<p>And, yes, attempts to correct this on Wikipedia have been made but were dropped years ago as utterly pointless when Alan Ayckbourn himself was cited by the website as not being an objective or reliable source of information…</p>

<p><strong>14) Is&nbsp;<em>The Divide</em> a play?</strong><br>No. <em><a href="/plays/the-divide">The Divide</a></em> was conceived and written as a prose work which was published in 2019 by&nbsp;<a href="https://www.pspublishing.co.uk/the-divide-trade-paperback-alan-ayckbourn-4904-p.asp">PS Publishing</a>. It was written in 2015 and is a prose-work told through journals, diaries, reportage, transcripts and minutes.</p>

<p>Prior to publication, it was first revealed to the public in a heavily truncated reading as part of a charity event at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, in 2015. This then led The Old Vic to request to adapt it into a play for the Edinburgh International Festival in 2017 and at the Old Vic in 2018. Alan agreed but was not responsible for the stage adaptation and does not consider <em>The Divide</em> to be a play nor is the play adaptation available to perform.</p>

<p><em>The Divide</em> is – and remains as of writing – Alan Ayckbourn’s first and only novel; which is what it was always intended to be.</p>
