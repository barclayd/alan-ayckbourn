<p>Fans of classic BBC Radio Drama are in for a treat this week with the release of two vintage 1960s thrillers written by Roy Clarke and directed by Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Events At Black Tor</em> and <em>The 17-Jewelled Shockproof Swiss-Made Bomb</em> are now available to <a href="https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/1447777/roy-clarke-classic-bbc-radio-thrillers/9781529196177.html">buy</a> and download, also marking the first time any of the radio plays directed by Alan Ayckbourn for the BBC have been made available to buy.</p>

<p>In 1965, Alan – temporarily – left theatre to work as a radio drama producer for the BBC, based in Leeds. He would enjoy a productive and very successful five years in the role, directing more than 250 radio productions.</p>

<p>Two of the most memorable and notable were plays by the then unknown northern writer, Roy Clarke, who would go on to huge success as a television writer and creator of such popular BBC series such as <em>Last of the Summer Wine</em>, <em>Open All Hours</em> and <em>Keeping Up Appearances</em>.</p>

<p>At the time, BBC Radio in Leeds was famed for its commitment to new, northern writing spearheaded by the famed radio producer Alfred Bradley. He had employed Alan to help deal with the massive amounts of scripts he was receiving and Alan would go on to produce many of the scripts sent to or discovered by Alfred, predominantly by new and upcoming writers.</p>

<p>In 1968, Alan directed Roy Clarke’s first radio play, <em>The 17 Jewelled Shockproof Swiss-Made Bomb</em>, a comedy thriller starring Peter Coke, Bob Grant, Robert Powell and Ben Kingsley. It was broadcast over six weeks from 18 January 1968 on BBC Radio 2.</p>

<p>Alan followed this up with <em>The Events at Black Tor</em>, broadcast for six weeks from 18 April 1968, also starring Bob Grant – of <em>On The Buse</em>s fame – alongside Brian Peck and Juliet Cooke. There’s also a cameo by John Nettles, who the previous year had made his professional stage acting debut at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, directed by Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p>Both radio plays were well-received and have become rather popular over the intervening decades – not least thanks to the success of Roy Clarke – and have been repeated a number of times by the BBC.</p>

<p>Now there’s an opportunity to own them and play them whenever you like with the release of <em>Roy Clarke Classic BBC Radio Thrillers</em> by the BBC Digital Audio imprint of Penguin Random House publishing.</p>

<p>The set of two audio plays is available to download now, priced at £9, and can be ordered from Penguin <a href="https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/1447777/roy-clarke-classic-bbc-radio-thrillers/9781529196177.html">here</a>. It’s also available on Audible at Amazon.</p>
