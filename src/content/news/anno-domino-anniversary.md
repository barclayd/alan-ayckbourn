<p><strong>Updated 26 May 2022:</strong> <em>The UK International Radio Drama Festival pulled the page with Anno Domino on the day of its anniversary and it is currently unavailable to stream.</em></p>

<p>Today marks the second anniversary of the unusual world premiere of Alan Ayckbourn’s 84th play <em><a href="/plays/anno-domino">Anno Domino</a></em>.</p>

<p>It was unusual because it marked the first time an Ayckbourn play was premiered as an audio production rather than on stage. This was, of course, due to the outbreak of the Covid pandemic which closed the UK’s theatres in march 2020.</p>

</div>

<p>Details had just been announced about the world premiere of his play, <em>Truth Will Out</em>, at the Stephen Joseph Theatre. But when this was cancelled, Alan dusted off a play he had previously written and decided to record it himself for the year’s world premiere.</p>

<p>He and his wife, Heather Stoney, played all eight parts which Alan recorded, mixed and edited at home – no doubt bringing back memories of his days as both an actor and a radio drama producer for the BBC – to produce an audio play of <em>Anno Domino</em>.</p>

<p>It marked the first time an Ayckbourn play had made its world premiere off-stage as an audio work, although it is still considered one of his full-length canon plays. Indeed he hopes it will at some point be performed on stage as originally intended.</p>

<p>The production garnered widespread media interest and was listened to around the world and it’s still available for free if you either missed it or want to hear it again.</p>
