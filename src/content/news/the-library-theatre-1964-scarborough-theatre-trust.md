<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1964 and the creation of the company which still runs the Stephen Joseph Theatre to this day.</p>

<p><strong>1964: Scarborough Theatre Trust</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>The early 1960s were a period of great uncertainty for Stephen Joseph’s theatre-in-the-round company in Scarborough.</p>

<p>By 1962, almost all of its funding, winter seasons and touring had been abandoned and Stephen Joseph was torn by a number of professional commitments. In short, prospects did not look good for Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>Yet amidst all this, Stephen formed a new company to run theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough which, despite all the odds, more than sixty years later is still running and responsible for the theatre Stephen founded in 1955. This is<strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-40/"> Scarborough Theatre Trust</a></strong>.</p>

<p>When Stephen Joseph founded Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1955, it was run under the auspices of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-40/">Studio Theatre Ltd</a></strong>, which Stephen had incorporated the same year; for most of the first decade, media reports referred to the Studio Theatre company rather than the Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre company.</p>

<p>Studio Theatre Ltd was one of the primary means Stephen used to raise finances for his new venture, issuing £1,000 of shares in 1955. The company ran Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre successfully until 1962.</p>

<p>In that year, however, Studio Theatre Ltd – along with the majority of its assets – moved to Stoke-on-Trent with the opening of the UK’s first in-the-round venue, the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong>. This was to prove problematic.</p>

<p>Stephen initially believed he could run both the Scarborough and Stoke ventures under Studio Theatre Ltd, but with the move came a substantial cut in subsidy from the Arts Council. Having applied for money for both venues, Stephen was shocked when the Arts Council gave Studio Theatre Ltd barely half of what was needed to run the new Victoria Theatre.</p>

<p>In a bid to cut its losses, the board of Studio Theatre Ltd decided to cut Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre loose. Realistically, it should have closed there and then. There was no money and no company.</p>

<p>However, Stephen would not give up on Scarborough that easily. The town had shown far more faith in the project than he had ever anticipated and, by that time, had embraced the company. He made a short term decision to form a new company, Theatre in the Round Ltd, in 1963 to run a summer season in Scarborough to see whether it could operate in its new reduced circumstances.</p>

<p>He and the co-director / theatre manager David Campton drew no wages and the season was produced on a shoestring budget, although still with an ambitious programme of seven productions encapsulating 10 plays.</p>

<p>The following year, having successfully kept the theatre alive during 1963, he formed another new company, Scarborough Theatre Trust Ltd, which took over running of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre. It was incorporated on 11 August 1964 by Herbert Simpson, Son &amp; Bennett in Leicester; the theatre manager, David Campton, was from Leicester hence the use of a business from there rather than Scarborough.</p>

<p>The company was established to ‘promote, maintain, improve and advance education, particularly by the production of educational plays and the encouragement of the Arts, including the arts of drama, mime, dance, singing and music, and to formulate, prepare and establish schemes therefor provided that all objects of the Company shall be of a charitable nature.’</p>

<p>It should be pointed out the company has over the past six decades not encouraged much in the way of mime! But then it’s probably over-achieved in the realm of encouraging drama.</p>

<p>The initial board consisted of Stephen Joseph, David Campton, Kenneth and Margaret Boden, Alfred Bradley, Maurice Plows and Stephen’s house-keeper Veronica Pemberton-Billing.</p>

<p>It’s perhaps interesting to note that Alan Ayckbourn is not part of this list, but at this point, he was no longer part of either Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre nor the Victoria Theatre, having left the latter to concentrate on writing during the summer of 1964. Alan would be invited to join the Scarborough Theatre Trust board in the years following Stephen Joseph’s death in 1967.</p>

<p>Although the company was formed to be of a ‘charitable nature’, Scarborough Theatre Trust did not actually become registered as a charity until 18 September 1967.</p>

<p>Ironically, no sooner had the company been formed with it lofty ambitions, then its end seemed imminent. Money was still very tight and the 1964 season saw just four productions over the summer. The 1965 season was only made possible due to a collaboration with the University of Manchester, where Stephen also worked, and saw many of his students employed for the season in all areas of the company from acting to backstage to front of house. </p>

<p>The company had a very successful season, but it was not enough to save it. At the close of it,  Stephen announced Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was to close and Scarborough Theatre Trust’s sole aim was to find a new home for the company, preferably not in Scarborough!</p>

<p>As we shall see in the next two articles, things did not quite go how Stephen imagined…</p>

<p>On 12 August 1966, Stephen stepped down as Chairman of Scarborough Theatre Trust and handed over control of the company – with all debts excused – to a new board. To all intents and purposes, that ended Stephen’s association with the company.</p>

<p>Scarborough Theatre Trust immediately began steps to relaunch professional seasons at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre during summer 1967. It never really looked back and, with a few mis-steps and bumps along the way, grew from strength to strength during the subsequent decades.</p>

<p>And the trust, although largely unrecognised, has been responsible for some of the most significant decisions in the company’s history. In 1972, it was the Trust which appointed Alan Ayckbourn as Artistic Director of the company – a decision which in no small measure ensured its long-term survival and success.</p>

<p>The Trust enabled the move of the company to its two future homes at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round and the Stephen Joseph Theatre and was responsible for the subsequent Artistic Director appointments following Alan Ayckbourn’s retirement in 2009.</p>

<p>In short, the creation of Scarborough Theatre Trust was one of Stephen’s most significant late decisions regarding theatre in the round in Scarborough, but it has proved to be one of its most enduring and influential ones.</p>
