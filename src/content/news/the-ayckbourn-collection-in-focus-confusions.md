<p><strong>Update (3/11/23): The Friends Gallery at Scarborough Art Gallery which holds the Ayckbourn display has been temporarily closed. Watch this space for re-opening details.</strong></p>

<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items within the display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong><em>Confusions</em> Poster / Programme</strong></p>

<p>It might seem odd that one of the rarest items in the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums and Galleries relates to one of the playwright’s most popular and performed works.</p>

</div>

<p>The collection includes a poster and programme for the world premiere of Alan Ayckbourn’s play <em>Confusions</em>, premiered at The Library Theatre, Scarborough, in 1974. The poster is believed to be unique, whilst the programme is one of the rarest from what we could consider his most popular works.</p>

<p>So why is this and why are both also significant in the history of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre?</p>

<p>The answer comes from the changes Alan Ayckbourn was making to the Scarborough theatre company. On 11 November 1972, he was appointed the Artistic Director and given the task of moving what was then just a summer season rep company into a year-round concern.</p>

<p>In 1974, he reviving winter seasons for the company for the first time since 1961 and also re-introducing touring (again, not seen since 1961). To kick these initiatives off, Alan decided to premiere his second new work of the year after <em>Absent Friends</em> with <em>Confusions</em>.</p>

<p>As a result, <em>Confusions</em> stands as a significant play in the history of the Stephen Joseph Theatre as it marked its transition essentially from a summer season rep company to a year-round theatre company, launching the first winter and touring season under Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/con-02-copy-w350.jpg" alt="" width="350" height="456" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Detail from the interior of the world premiere programme for Confusions (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>For the winter season, the company did not have access to the Concert Room at the library and instead had to utilise the Large Lecture Room, which meant performances were not in-the-round due to the smaller size of the room. They were instead performed three-sided.</p>

<p>The production was performed from just 30 September to 9 October before embarking on a six week repertory tour to Filey Sun Lounge, Whitby Pavilion and Scarborough Library. It was performed on 10 days across the six week tour. All told, a very short run for an Ayckbourn world premiere which had already been picked up for production in the West End.</p>

<p>Which explains why the poster and programmes are so rare. As far as can be ascertained, not many posters were produced for such a short initial run in Scarborough and the glossy bi-fold programmes were only produced in limited quantities for this run and the tour. For comparison, from an archival perspective, there are far more programmes known to exist for the world premiere of Alan Ayckbourn’s <em>Meet My Father</em> almost a decade earlier in 1965 at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre; this would later be retitled <em>Relatively Speaking</em> and become Alan’s first major West End success.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/1975-confusions-cover-w194.jpg" alt="" width="194" height="299" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>The poster for the 1975 revival of Confusions (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The following year, 1975, <em>Confusions</em> was revived for a longer run during the Scarborough summer season but with a completely re-designed poster and programme (now black and white with an image, rather than the colourful and quirky logo based poster of the original production).</p>

<p>Programmes for the revival are quite common – unlike the original programme – although as cheap staples were used, most of the surviving programmes have rust issues around the spine staple.</p>

<p>The world premiere Confusions poster in the Scarborough Museums and Galleries Collection is pasted onto board and is as far as I’m aware, as Alan’s Archivist, is unique. I’ve certainly never come across the poster at this size in any other collection. Which makes it a rather special piece in the collection even though it may not garner as much as attention as some of the other pieces.</p>

<p>The <em>Confusions</em> Poster and programme can be currently seen in the display of pieces from The Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Art Gallery.</p>
