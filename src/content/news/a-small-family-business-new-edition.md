<p>Alan Ayckbourn’s classic ’80s play <em><strong><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business">A Small Family Business</a></strong></em> has been republished by Faber in a new collection of 12 iconic plays with new cover designs.</p>

<p>The play, first published by Faber in 1987 and again in 2014, is now available with a new typographic cover design alongside 11 other notable play titles in the Faber drama range.</p>

<p>Dinah Wood, Editorial Director at Faber, said. “An exhilarating aspect of live theatre is its ephemeral nature. Once it’s gone, it’s gone. And the great counterbalance to that is making the script itself available in perpetuity. I have long dreamed of a classic livery for our plays that reflects this timelessness. Not grand but practical and pocketable, comfortable to hold and use. And the cover is unsealed, which means no plastic, just good old paper and ink. Jonathan Pelham’s elegant typographic design has a contemporary feel with more than a nod to Faber’s illustrious history. It’s a celebration of past, present and future, and I am delighted to be launching the list with these twelve diversely dazzling titles.”</p>

<p>The new editions have been designed by Jonathan Pelham, who talked about the inspirations behind the new design. “The design makes reference to books as being a ‘platform’ of sorts. With the simple introduction of four trapezia as a framing device, the cover takes on the elevated dimensions of a stage and a lick of colour in a single move. Meanwhile, the heavy sans serif shouts like a playbill. The bevelled edges closely resemble the designer Berthold Wolpe’s ‘ribbon’ motif, best known for appearing on the works of Eliot, Heaney, Lowell, Larkin and co. This emblem is a mark of quality for Faber, so it is fitting that it should adorn the covers of our celebrated playwrights.”</p>

<p><em>A Small Family Business</em> premiered at the National Theatre in 1987 starring Michael Gambon. It is described by Faber as a riotous exposure of entrepreneurial greed, as the family connections of its protagonist fade and rampant self-interest takes over and comic hysteria builds to a macabre climax.</p>

<p>The new edition of <em>A Small Family Business</em> is priced at £10.99 and is available from the Faber website <strong><a href="https://www.faber.co.uk/product/9780571315703-a-small-family-business/">here</a></strong> with the other titles available <strong><a href="https://www.faber.co.uk/product-category/drama/new-faber-drama-editions/">here</a></strong>.</p>
