<p>Sir Alan Ayckbourn is featured in Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries latest exhibition <em>Who Am I?</em> which opens today.</p>

<p>The exhibition features the first public display of the ‘kitchen sink’ artist John Bratby’s portrait of Sir Alan from the mid 1970s – it’s an unusual work and its style is in direct contrast to the other portraits on display, which are drawn from Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries’ collection and includes work from the 16th to the 20th century.</p>

<p>To mark the opening of the exhibition, here’s five other occasions when Sir Alan has been drawn or painted for posterity.</p>

<p>One of the most famous portraits of Sir Alan – more accurately a caricature – is the actor and artist Clive Francis’s 1988 drawing of the playwright at work. Clive has worked with Sir Alan on a number of occasions and apparently the original drawing had Alan holding a cigarette, which was then altered to a pencil given Alan had stopped smoking many years earlier!</p>

<p>In 1989, Allan Ramsay was commissioned to paint a portrait for the National portrait Gallery. The piece is oil on canvas and marked Sir Alan’s 50th birthday. The National Portrait Gallery holds this alongside three photographic portraits of the playwright.</p>

<p>In 1999, famed caricaturist Gerald Scarfe created an image for the celebration of Alan Ayckbourn’s 60th birthday with the <em>A Chorus of Approval</em> event. The playwright is portrayed next to a selection of some of his many plays. Although there is no connection between Scarfe and Sir Alan, the artist is married to Jane Asher who has worked on a number of occasions with Sir Alan.</p>

<p>One of the most unusual portraits of Sir Alan is by the award-winning Portuguese artist André Carrilho, whose portrait was commissioned for the cover of The Haileyburian magazine for an article about the playwright in 2005. Alan is an alumni of Haileybury.</p>

<p>Finally, one of the most recent portraits of Sir Alan is by the British artist June Mendoza, whilst Alan was Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre in Scarborough. Unlike the majority of the other works – with the exception of the Bratby – the original of the oil on canvas is owned by Sir Alan</p>

<p>You can see John Bratby’s portrait of Sir Alan at Scarborough Art Gallery from 4 April in the <em>Who Am I?</em> exhibition. Admission is free with a £3 annual pass which also gives fee entry to the Rotunda Museum.</p>

<p>Further details can be found on the Scarborough Museums and Galleries website by clicking <a href="https://scarboroughmuseumsandgalleries.org.uk">here</a>. The gallery is open Tuesdays to Sundays, 10am to 5pm.</p>
