<p>On 8 July 1965, Alan Ayckbourn’s breakout play <strong><em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></em></strong> premiered in Scarborough at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre – also celebrating its <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/">70th anniversary</a></strong> this year.</p>

<p>This would begin a journey that would take the play around the world over the next six decades. In the first of several articles celebrating the 60th anniversary of the play, we take a look at the facts behind one of Sir Alan Ayckbourn’s most famous and popular works.</p>

<p>And Relatively Speaking is also one of the plays featured in <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/">The Library Theatre at 70 exhibition</a></strong>, which is running at Scarborough Library from 10 – 12 July. More details can be found <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/">here</a></strong>.</p>

<p><strong>Relatively Speaking: In 20 Facts</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>1) <em>Relatively Speaking</em>&nbsp;is Alan Ayckbourn’s 7th play and was originally performed under the title <em>Meet My Father</em>; the title was changed for the West End.</p>

<p>2) <em>Meet My Father</em> premiered on 8 July 1965 at&nbsp;<strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong>, Scarborough. It was Alan’s first play to be produced at the venue where he began playwriting since 1961.</p>

<p>3) It was commissioned by <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong>, artistic director of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, with the instruction to create a “well-made play” that would attract tourists on rainy days!</p>

<p>4) The world premiere production was directed by Stephen Joseph, who cut about a third of the original script to give it a brisk running time of approximately 90 minutes; this version has never been performed subsequently.</p>

<p>5) It was the final Ayckbourn play not to have its world premiere directed by the author himself.</p>

<p>6) It is the first Ayckbourn play to mention his fictional town of Pendon, which is the location of a number of other Ayckbourn plays including <em><strong><a href="/plays/time-and-time-again">Time &amp; Time Again</a></strong></em>, <em><strong><a href="/plays/a-chorus-of-disapproval">A Chorus of Disapproval</a></strong></em>, <em><a href="/plays/ten-times-table"><strong>Ten Times Table</strong></a></em> and <em><strong><a href="/plays/improbable-fiction">Improbable Fiction</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>7) The play was optioned for London by the West End impresario Peter Bridge, who insisted the title be changed to something “less provincial” – hence <em>Relatively Speaking</em>.</p>

<p>8) The West End production launched with a pre-London tour during which Alan was still rewriting the play, as he was particularly unhappy with the climax. The solution of bringing the play back to the slippers from the first scene was suggested by Tom Erdhardt, then Peter Bridge’s assistant and later Alan’s agent.</p>

<p>9) The West End premiere – directed by Nigel Patrick – was held at the Duke of York’s Theatre on 29 March 1967. It closed on 3 February 1968 and is considered Alan Ayckbourn’s first major West End success.</p>

<p>10) The West End production made an impression upon Noël Coward who sent Alan a telegram which read: “Dear Alan Ayckbourn all my congratulations on a beautifully constructed and very very funny comedy I enjoyed every moment of it = Noël Coward.”</p>

<p>11) It has subsequently been revived twice in London in 2013 at the Wyndham’s Theatre and in 2021 at the Jermyn Street Theatre. It also had an acclaimed revival at Greenwich Theatre in 1986 directed by Alan Strachan.</p>

<p>12) <em>Relatively Speaking</em>&nbsp;was the first Ayckbourn play to be published. Despite being given to the publishers Evans in 1967, the play took more than two years to reach publication. It has never been out of print since.</p>

<p>13) It marked the first international success for Alan with Germany quickly translating and producing it in dozens of major venues over the next few years. It was also the first Ayckbourn play to reproduced in Australia with a production in 1968.</p>

<p>14) <em>Relatively Speaking</em>&nbsp;was both the first Ayckbourn play to be broadcast on television (in 1967 with scenes recorded from the West End premiere) and the first Ayckbourn play to be adapted for television (1969). It is also the only play to have been adapted twice for British television – both times by the BBC – in 1969 and 1989.</p>

<p>15) It was the first (of many) Ayckbourn plays to be picked up for a UK tour – in 1968 and quickly became a staple of repertory theatres in the UK that same year – it’s still one of his most produced works today.</p>

<p>16) Unusually, Alan Ayckbourn first directed the play with an amateur production by Leeds Art Theatre in 1970 at Leeds Civic Theatre.</p>

<p>17) <em>Relatively Speaking</em> had its North American premiere in 1970 at Westport Country Playhouse.  It was not professionally produced in New York until 1984.</p>

<p>18) It was adapted for BBC Radio 4 in 1975 by Kay Patrick and was later the first radio adaptation of an Ayckbourn play to be commercially released.</p>

<p>19) Alan Ayckbourn has directed the piece professionally twice. The first time in 1977 at the&nbsp;<strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-15/">Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round</a></strong>, Scarborough, and the second time in 2007 at the&nbsp;<strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-189/page-16/">Stephen Joseph Theatre</a></strong>, Scarborough, to mark the 40th anniversary of its West End debut.</p>

<p>20) It is the only Ayckbourn play to be featured in another Ayckbourn play. In <em><strong>Life of Riley</strong></em> (2010), the play opens with lines from <em>Relatively Speaking</em> spoken by characters running lines for an amateur production of the play.</p>
