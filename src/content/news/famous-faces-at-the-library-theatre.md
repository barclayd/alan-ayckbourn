<p><em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition is now open at Scarborough Library celebrating the 70th anniversary of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round.</p>

<p>The company was founded in 1955 by the theatre pioneer Stephen Joseph and would help launch the careers of some notable actors, directors and writers.</p>

<p>Whilst the company was resident at The Library Theatre between 1955 and 1976, there was no money for ‘star’ casting. But a number of people who worked at the theatre would subsequently go on to become stars in their own rights.</p>

<p>We’ve drawn 9 faces from the exhibition photographs. How many can you name?</p>

<p>The answers – very concisely! – are 1) Tom Baker (1968) – most famous as the fourth regeneration of The Doctor in the long-running TV series <em>Doctor Who</em>; 2) John Nettles (1967) – the star of TV’s <em>Bergerac</em> and <em>Midsomer Murders</em>; 3) Stephanie Turner (1970) – actor and director who gained fame as the star of the BBC series <em>Juliet Bravo</em>; 4) Robert Powell (1967) – starred in in the BBC series <em>Doomwatch</em> soon after working in Scarborough, but found international fame in the titular role of <em>Jesus of Nazareth</em>; 5) Martin Carthy (1960) – then a stage manager at the Library Theatre, who became one of the most influential figures in English folk music, inspiring the likes of Bob Dylan and Paul Simon; 6) Bob Peck (1970) – BAFTA winning and Olivier nominated actor most famed for the BBC series <em>Edge of Darkness</em> and <em>Jurassic Park</em>; 7) Christopher Godwin – famed stage and screen actor currently appearing opposite Mark Gatiss in the RSC production of <em>The Resistable Rise of Arturo Ui</em>; 8) Malcom Hebden (1976) – actor most fondly known as Norris in the soap opera <em>Coronation Street</em>; 9) Alan Ayckbourn (1957) – quite a famous playwright…</p>

<p>You can find all these and many more people who worked at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in the photographs on display in the exhibition</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition, curated by Simon Murgatroyd M.A., is now running on the first floor of Scarborough Library until 27 June. It celebrates the first 21 years of the famed company and includes 90 images, many of which have never been displayed in public before. The photographs covering productions at the theatre, staff and company, life at the Library Theatre as well as a section dedicated to Alan Ayckbourn’s plays premiered at the venue.</p>

<p>The exhibition is free and is open during normal library opening hours from 9am, Mondays to Saturdays.</p>

<p>More details can be found on <strong><a href="http://theatre-in-the-round.com/page-41/page-118/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>, a website which celebrates the most significant first four decades of theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough under Stephen Joseph and Sir Alan Ayckbourn’s direction.</p>

<p>The exhibition is also part of Scarborough 400. which celebrates the 400th anniversary of becoming the town becoming the UK’s first seaside resort and a number of events are planned throughout the year.</p>
