<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>We begin with the basics and the more general questions about Alan…</em></p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: The Basics</strong></p>

<div>

</div>

<p><strong>1) How do I pronounce Ayckbourn?</strong><br>Ayckbourn is pronounced Ache-born (ache rhyming with wake).</p>

<p><strong>2) How do I spell Ayckbourn?</strong><br>A Y C K B O U R N. Always include the Y and never add an E. Anywhere. Ever. Thank you.</p>

<p><strong>3) When was Alan Ayckbourn born?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn was born on 12 April 1939.</p>

<p><strong>3) Where was Alan Ayckbourn born?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn was born in Hampstead in London.<br>&nbsp;<br><strong>5) If he was born in London, why was Alan Ayckbourn named Yorkshireman of the Year in 1994?<br></strong>Ah yes, that one. The basic answer is poor research. The more complicated answer is, Alan has been associated with Scarborough and Yorkshire most of his life and is frequently mistaken for a Yorkshireman, despite the fact that he wasn’t born in Yorkshire. Best to move on quickly from that one… </p>

<p><strong>6) Who were Alan Ayckbourn’s parents?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn was born  to Irene Maud Worley (better known as ‘Lolly’ and who was also a very successful writer under the pen-name of Mary James) and Horace Ayckbourn, lead violinist with the London Symphony Orchestra.</p>

<p>Just to complicate matters, Alan was born out of wedlock and Lolly never married Horace. At the time of Alan’s birth, although separated, she was still married to her first husband Neville Monroe and when she later married Cecil Pye, she had to get a divorce from Neville; although Lolly had frequently stated she had married Horace – presumably to avoid the stigma of scandal.</p>

<p><strong>7) Who is Alan Ayckbourn married to?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn has been married to Lady Ayckbourn, Heather Stoney, since 1997. They met for the first time in 1962 at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, but did not begin a relationship until the early 1970s.</p>

<p>Alan first married in 1959 to the actress Christine Roland – who he also first met at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, Scarborough. They had two sons, Steven and Philip, but separated during the 1960s. They did not divorce, however, until 1997. As a result both Christine and Heather are Lady Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><strong>8) How is Alan Ayckbourn referred to?<br></strong>Officially, as he was knighted in 1997, he should be referred to as Sir Alan or, for completist’s sake, Sir Alan Ayckbourn CBE – as he was also appointed Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1987. Professionally, he prefers to work and be known as Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><strong>9) How many plays has Alan Ayckbourn written?<br></strong>As of 2023, Alan Ayckbourn has written and had produced 89 full-length plays (as well as numerous other revues, one act plays, adaptations, plays for young people and other pieces). By the time you read this, there’ll probably be more.<br>&nbsp;<br><strong>10) Is Alan Ayckbourn the second most performed playwright in the UK / World after Shakespeare?<br></strong>No. And just to emphasise that one. No. There is no way of knowing how popular any playwright in the world is, aside from one William Shakespeare. There is no single body that compiles details of professional productions even in the UK, let alone amateur productions or international productions. So no-one can say with any weight of evidence that they are the second, twenty-second or two hundred and second most performed playwright. No organisation collates this data. If you read it in an article, it’s just a sign of lazy journalism and research.</p>

<p>But this is still the single most mis-reported ‘fact’ about Alan Ayckbourn and it can be traced back to the early 1990s when an Arts Council report stated that for the specific previous year in regional, subsidised UK theatre, Alan was the second most performed playwright after Shakespeare. You can find out more details about this and the whole issue on Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website by clicking <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs/popularity">here</a>.</p>

<p>Alan is – and undoubtedly has been – one of the UK’s most popular, produced and successful playwrights of the late 20th / early 21st century, but no-one can say with any degree of accuracy, just how successful. </p>

<p><strong>11) When did Alan Ayckbourn suffer a stroke?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn suffered a stroke on 21 February 2006. This was a full week before it was reported in the press and media with news of the stroke only officially being announced once it was ascertained Alan was making a full recovery.<br>&nbsp;<br><strong>12) Can I send scripts for Alan Ayckbourn to read / Can Alan Ayckbourn offer me advice on writing?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn no longer accepts script submissions nor offers advice to writers. Whilst Artistic Director at the Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round, he spent 37 years reading and evaluating scripts (and five years at the BBC from 1965 – 1970 doing the same) as well as advising writers. With his retirement from the theatre in 2009, he felt it was time to pass the torch onto other people so that he could understandably concentrate completely on his own writing and directing for the first time.<br>With regard to advice to writers and directors, he sincerely feels that everything he has to say on the subject can be found in his book&nbsp;<em>The Crafty Art Of Playmaking</em>&nbsp;(Faber, 2002).<br><br><strong>13) Is Alan Ayckbourn on social media or are there any official Ayckbourn social media channels?</strong><br>No, Alan Ayckbourn is not on social media nor does he engage with social media. There are several official social media channels which are run by his Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, and which are affiliated with this website. These are&nbsp;<a href="https://www.instagram.com/ayckbourn_playwright/">Instagram</a>, <a href="https://www.facebook.com/alan.ayckbourn.playwright/">Facebook</a>, a&nbsp;<a href="http://archivingayckbourn.home.blog/">WordPress blog</a> and&nbsp;<a href="https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCw_wK39Pa4Hnfziu9On5nNg">Youtube</a>. Any other social media sites or platforms claiming to be Alan Ayckbourn or affiliated to Alan Ayckbourn are fake and should be reported.</p>
