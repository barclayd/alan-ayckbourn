<p>One of the most unusual and least well-known of Alan Ayckbourn’s work is his one-<a href="/plays/a-cut-in-the-rates">act play, <em>A Cut In The Rates</em></a>.</p>

<p>It is unusual because it is a rare example of an Ayckbourn world premiere being filmed and it was shown on television for the first time on 21 January 1984.</p>

<p>So what is <em>A Cut in the Rates</em>? It’s a one act play which was written specifically for a television education series exploring theatres and playwriting.</p>

<p>Comissioned by the BBC, for the series&nbsp;<em>English Files</em>, the impetus was to create a programme exploring the whole process of staging a play from read-through to rehearsals to production.</p>

<p>Alan was quite intrigued by the idea and essentially decided to write a play in order that the entire production process could be shown and recorded in less than a week.</p>

<p>The programme was produced by Scarborough-born Geoff Wilson and filming for the programme took place in Scarborough at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round between 8 – 12 November, 1983.</p>

<p>Alan and three members of his current company – alongside a reduced production team – were recorded going through the initial read-through, rehearsals and the production process before culminating in a complete performance of the short play.</p>

<p>For this, <em>A Cut In The Rates</em>&nbsp;was performed for the first time at the&nbsp;Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round&nbsp;on Saturday 12 November 1983 with a limited amount of tickets available to the public.</p>

<p>This single performance of the play was recorded live by the BBC. Produced on a limited budget, the play was written to take advantage of the current production at the venue, which was Michael Cashman’s&nbsp;<em>Before Your Very Eyes</em>. The play utilised the set and props from this play using the actors Lavinia Bertram, Liza Sadovy and Michael Cashman.</p>

<p>The&nbsp;<em>English Files</em>&nbsp;programme was broadcast on BBC2 at 2.30pm on 21 January 1984 and ran for 25 minutes. Unfortunately, it has never been made available commercially by the BBC. It was the first time a play had been filmed at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round and then shown on television.</p>

<p>Although it was never performed again in Scarborough, Alan was happy enough with the piece that he agreed to let it be published by Samuel French as an acting edition in 1991. It was made available for professional and amateur production and has proven to be particularly popular with amateur companies and schools.</p>

<p>The play features a rates collector, Miss Pickhart visiting the house of an illusionist, Ratchet, to enquire as to why he hasn’t been paying his rates. As she inspects the house, it transpires that Ratchet’s wife, Rosalinda, died seven years earlier in a tragic magic accident. Left alone in the cellar with the illusionist’s props, Rosalinda appears bearing the hideous marks of her accident and persuades Miss Pickhart to climb into the magic cabinet where she died. A deranged Ratchet reappears, thinking Miss Pickhart is Rosalinda and intending to kill her. Rosalind appears and Ratchet has a heart attack allowing Miss Pickhart to escape from the house, discovering that both Ratchet and his wife apparently died seven years earlier. Of course, it is all a ruse. Ratchet and Rosalinda are both alive and well and have concocted an elaborate plan to avoid paying bills on their house!</p>

<p>It is worth noting that&nbsp;<em>A Cut In The Rates</em>&nbsp;is considered in the Ayckbourn canon to be a play from 1984, this reflects the year it was broadcast (as it was commissioned for that programme) rather than 1983 when the play received its first public performance.</p>
