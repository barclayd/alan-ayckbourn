<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items currently on display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Ayckbourn Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>Theatre in the Round by Stephen Joseph</strong></p>

<p>One of the most notable aspects of Scarborough Art Gallery’s current display of items drawn from the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries is its focus.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Portrait of Stephen Joseph, held in the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums &amp; Galleries (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Equal parity is given to both Alan Ayckbourn and Stephen Joseph – possibly the first time any exhibition on the history of theatre in the round in Scarborough has chosen to take this route.</p>

<p>And, from this Archivist’s perspective, it is long overdue and gratifying to see both significant figures placed together in one exhibition – for it is no overstatement to say that without Stephen Joseph, there is no Alan Ayckbourn as we know him today and certainly, without Stephen, Alan would neither have come to Scarborough nor would his career have gone down the path it did.</p>

<p>Stephen Joseph was the single most significant influence and mentor figure to Alan. He encouraged and commissioned Alan to both write and direct and they had a strong friendship from the moment they met in 1957 until Stephen died in 1967. Without Stephen’s influence, it is difficult to imagine Alan would have become writer, director and artistic director. Without Stephen, there would be no theatre in the round in Scarborough today.</p>

<p>Alan frequently notes how Stephen was the most knowledgable person he ever met with regards to theatre and that his scope of knowledge was exceptionally broad and detailed. Part of this knowledge is reflected in the four books he wrote: <em>The Story of the Playhouse in England</em>, <em>New Theatre Forms</em>, <em>Scene Painting and Design</em> and his essential work, <em>Theatre In The Round</em>.</p>

<p>A copy of <em>Theatre in the Round</em> is held in the Ayckbourn Collection and is currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. It is a remarkable and even prescient work which looks at the history and development of theatre in the round and Stephen Joseph’s thoughts on how to create successful in-the-round spaces from a technical, practical and even philosophical perspective.</p>

<p>It is also vital to the understanding of the development of theatre in the round in Scarborough as two of its chapters look at the development of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre between opening in 1955 and 1965, when Stephen closed the company down with no intention of it re-opening; of course, largely thanks to the efforts of Ken Boden, it did re-open as a professional company in 1967.</p>

<p> It’s also worth emphasising a very good point that Stephen makes on the first page of Chapter Three as he looks at his Scarborough invention, noting how in 1955 Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was the first theatre company in the UK whose impetus was to promote new writing. Theatre in the Round opened its first season – consisting entirely of four new works – on 30 July 1958; nine months before the English Stage Company (now the Royal Court Theatre) staged its first production as a company also predicated on new writing (and which is, inarguably, the most successful new writing company this country has ever seen). But it is worth noting, Scarborough is a town which has consistently nurtured new writing in theatre for almost seven decades.</p>

<p>And the book also highlights how Stephen achieved his dream of creating a new writing company in the round  without any substantial capital to invest in the project – theatre in the round was not only a passion and dream, but also a practical necessity  in making the company viable.</p>

<p>Stephen’s ambitions and aims of promoting both new writing and new theatre forms such as theatre in the round are highlighted in this fascinating book and, of course, Alan Ayckbourn took forward Stephen’s ideas and ambitions when he took over as Artistic Director of the Scarborough company in 1972, ensuring Stephen’s legacy was not lost.</p>

<p>And whilst <em>Theatre in the Round</em> may have dated somewhat in its technical details, its exploration of how to practically explore theatre in the round and stage it, is as relevant and applicable today as it was then.</p>
