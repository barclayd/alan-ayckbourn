<p>Fifty years ago, Alan Ayckbourn appeared as a guest on BBC Radio’s popular series <em>Desert Island Discs</em>.</p>

<p>He’d just hit a peak of popularity with four plays running in the West End with <em><strong><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a></strong></em> and <em><strong><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests">The Norman Conquests</a></strong></em> trilogy and appearing on a such a high profile programme was a definite sign he had already become a household name.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn in 1974 (© Haydonning Ltd – Picture by Alec Russell)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>For those who aren’t familiar with the format, the show notably asks guests to name the music they would wish to be stranded on a desert island with along with a book and a luxury item.</p>

<p>The programme was recorded on 14 October 1974 and broadcast on BBC Radio 4 on 2 November 1974 with Alan interviewed by Roy Plomley with the episode produced by Ronald Cook.</p>

<p>It was notable for causing a little controversy because Pink Floyd was one of Alan’s choices and the BBC received several letters bemoaning the fact this wasn’t ‘real’ music and completely inappropriate for Desert Island Discs!</p>

<p>Here’s what Alan chose for his music and his ‘luxuries along with a brief transcription of why he made these choices.</p>

<p><strong>Music Choices</strong></p>

<p><strong>Young Person’s Guide To The Orchestra</strong> (Benjamin Britten)<br>“It’s terribly exciting the way Britten uses the full orchestra, and introduces it stage by stage and however much one writes, I don’t think, as a writer of words, I’ll ever quite capture the excitement of someone sitting witnessing a full symphony orchestra.”</p>

<p><strong>Concerto for Oboe and Violin in C minor</strong> (Johann Sebastian Bach)<br>“Bach is the sort of master craftsman that I’d like to be as a writer, and which I strive to be.”</p>

<p><strong>Flamenco Sketches</strong> from the album <em>Kind of Blue</em> (Miles Davies)<br>“This is slightly nostalgic… it reminds me of theatrical digs and late night parties and so on.”</p>

<p><strong>Cinderella</strong> (Sergei Sergeyevich Prokoviev)<br>“It’s very beautiful and it’s very romantic.”</p>

<p><strong>When Or Where from Babes In Arms</strong> (Peggy Lee &amp; Benny Goodman)<br>“One of their marvellous songs.”</p>

<p><strong>Welscher Tanz Wascha Mesa</strong> (Konrad Ragossnig &amp; Ulsamer Collegium)<br>“This is medieval, or Renaissance music. It sort of conjures up a picture of when the world was better, but we jolly well know it wasn’t.”</p>

<p><strong>Eclipse</strong> from <em>Dark Side Of The Moon</em> (Pink Floyd)<br>“I’m very fond of pop… and I think this is marvellous pop.”</p>

<p><strong>Agnus Dei</strong> from <em>Messe de Requiem</em> (Camilee Saint-Saëns)<br>“This sort of music, I think, if I were there long enough, I’d like to die by.”</p>

<p><strong>Desert Island Luxuries<br>Album:</strong> “It would have to be the master craftsman, Bach, I think – his double concerto.”</p>

<p><strong>Book:</strong> <em>Act One</em> (Moss Hart)<br>“One of the funniest books I know of… a terribly funny book about the theatre.”</p>

<p><strong>Luxury Item:</strong> Mellotron<br>“If I could take one of these with me, powered somehow, I could then play my own applause and all my own sound effects, and do my one-man dramatic society.”</p>

<p>One wonders what Alan’s choice would be today, five decades on? As it is, there’s an insight into the tastes of the playwright when he was first experiencing fame in the mid 1970s.</p>
