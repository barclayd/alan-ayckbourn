<p>Sir Alan Ayckbourn officially inaugurated the Portholes in Time sea wall heritage trail in Scarborough this afternoon.</p>

<p>He unveiled the first of the 31 ‘portholes’ which are situated along the length of the town’s Marine Drive sea wall, each telling a small part of the seaside resort’s rich and varied history.</p>

<p>Sir Alan – who is one of just four people included on the portholes – unveiled the ‘start’ Porthole by the Marine Drive Tollgate and acknowledged the work of Scarborough Martime Heritage Centre, Scarborough Civic Society and Scarborough Archeological Society as examples of volunteer run groups which contribute so much to the town. He also thanked the National Lottery Heritage Fund and Scarborough Borough Council for making the project possible.</p>

<p>The Scarborough Maritime Heritage Centre, now in its 13th year of operation, was awarded £39,968 of National Lottery Heritage Funding for the Sea Wall Heritage Trail – Portholes in Time project.</p>

<p>As a result, 31 engraved granite storyboards have been installed on the mile long Marine Drive. Each ‘Porthole in Time’ tells a small part of Scarborough’s rich and varied history and includes references to historical events, local traditions, significant building as well as people – such as Alan Ayckbourn, who has been associated with the town since 1957 and was Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre for 37 years, establishing it as an internationally known venue for new writing.</p>

<p>People walking the mile-long trail can learn more via a smartphone ‘App’ that links to more details on these topics including photographs and video footage. Residents and visitors, together with students from local schools, helped select the historical topics to feature in the project.</p>

<p>This project also invites people to become an active partner. The public can contribute their own Scarborough Memories, such as special moments from childhood or stories from within their families carried down the generations. A number of participating organisations including cafes, restaurants, hotels, guesthouses and tourism venues have committed to help gather the Scarborough Memories.</p>

<p>Project Manager, Huw Roberts, a Trustee of the Scarborough Maritime Heritage Centre, commented on the award and its benefits for the town.</p>

<p>“This is great news for Scarborough, and its residents and visitors. Whatever was happening in history, ancient or modern, Scarborough has been up there making the news and contributing to one of the most fascinating and unique heritages of any town in Britain.”</p>

<p>The Scarborough Maritime Heritage Centre is the lead organisation within a formal project partnership which also includes the Scarborough Civic Society and the Scarborough Archaeological and Historical Society. The partnership is also working closely with Scarborough Borough Council.</p>

<p>The trail and portholes were installed in November 2022 and inaugurated on 6 December 2022. Further details about the portholes and the work of Scarborough Maritime Heritage Centre can be found on the centre’s website by clicking <a href="https://www.scarboroughsmaritimeheritage.org.uk">here</a>.</p>
