<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>In part 7, we’re looking at questions regarding Alan Ayckbourn’s professional career as a director.</em></p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Alan Ayckbourn – The Director</strong></p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn directing Sisterly Feelings in 1979 (Photo by Alec Russell © Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p><strong>1) When and where did Alan Ayckbourn first begin directing?<br></strong>Alan began his professional&nbsp;<a href="/career/directing-career">directing</a>&nbsp;career in 1961 at&nbsp;<a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/theatre-in-the-round-at-the-library-theatre">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a>, Scarborough. Like his writing career, Alan was encouraged to take the step into directing by his mentor&nbsp;<a href="/life/stephen-joseph">Stephen Joseph</a>.</p>

<p><strong>2) What was Alan Ayckbourn first production as a professional director?<br></strong>The first play Alan directed was Patrick Hamilton’s classic gothic thriller&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/directing-career/gaslight">Gaslight</a></em>. It ran in rep at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, from 29 June to 26 July 1961. It starred Hazel Burt, David Jarrett, Stanley Page, Rosamund Dickson and Philip Clifford.</p>

<p><strong>3) What was the first Ayckbourn play to be directed by Alan Ayckbourn himself?<br></strong>The first Ayckbourn play to be directed by its author was a revival of&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/standing-room-only">Standing Room Only</a></em>&nbsp;at the Victoria Theatre, Stoke-on-Trent in 1963. The play had premiered at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, in 1961 directed by Stephen Joseph. When Stephen opened the Victoria Theatre in 1962, Alan joined the company as actor, writer and director. <em>Standing Room Only</em> was revived in rep from 15 April to 29 June 1963, directed by Alan and starring Elizabeth Bell, David Halliwell, Caroline Smith, Stanley Page, Peter King and Heather Stoney.</p>

<p>Intriguingly, <em>Standing Room Only</em> was initially credited to Alan Ayckbourn’s early writing pseudonym, Roland Allen, when first produced. For its revival, the author was credited as Alan himself. This makes Standing Room Only both the final play to be credited to Roland Allen and the earliest play to be credited to Alan Ayckbourn!</p>

<p><strong>4) What was the first Ayckbourn world premiere to be directed by Alan Ayckbourn himself?<br></strong>The first world premiere of his own work which Alan directed was the premiere of&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></em> in 1963 at the Victoria Theatre, Stoke-on-Trent.</p>

<p><strong>5) How many of his own plays has Alan Ayckbourn directed the world premiere of?<br></strong>Alan has directed the world premieres of – as of 2024 – 83 of his 90 full-length plays. The plays which he did not direct there world premieres of are: <em><a href="/plays/the-square-cat">The Square Cat</a></em> (1959), <em><a href="/plays/love-after-all">Love After All</a></em> (1959), <em><a href="/plays/dads-tale">Dad’s Tale</a></em> (1960), <em>Standing Room Only</em> (1961), <a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind"><em>Christmas V Mastermind</em></a> (1962), <em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></em> (1965) and <em><a href="/plays/jeeves">Jeeves</a></em> (1975).</p>

<p><strong>6) When did Alan Ayckbourn begin directing the London productions of his work?<br></strong>Alan began directing his own work in London with the National Theatre’s 1977 production of&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce">Bedroom Farce</a></em>. Although he is credited as co-director with&nbsp;<a href="/life/influences/hall-sir-peter">Sir Peter Hall</a>, it is no secret the piece was largely directed by Alan with&nbsp;Sir Peter Hall&nbsp;doing some preparation with the actors before leaving to direct&nbsp;<em>Volpone</em>&nbsp;once Alan had arrived at the National Theatre. The first West End production of his own work to be directed by Alan Ayckbourn was&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/ten-times-table">Ten Times Table</a></em>&nbsp;at the Globe Theatre in 1978.</p>

<p><strong>7) How many West End productions has Alan Ayckbourn directed?<br></strong>Alan has directed 32 productions in the West End. These include 12 productions at the National Theatre, 1 production for the Royal Shakespeare Company at the Barbican, 26 West End premieres, 2 revivals of his own plays and 4 productions by other playwrights. </p>

<p><strong>8) Has Alan Ayckbourn directed work by other authors?<br></strong>Definitely. As of 2024, Alan has directed approximately 400 productions since 1961, approximately half of which are plays by other authors. His most famous production as a director is the National Theatre’s 1987 production of&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/miller-arthur">A View From The Bridge</a></em>&nbsp;which its author, Arthur Miller, said he considered a definitive production of the play. Another American playwright, Herb Gardner, said he also considered Alan’s 1994 production of his play&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/directing-career/conversations-with-my-father">Conversations With My Father</a></em>&nbsp;(which transferred to London in 1995) as the definitive production.</p>

<p><strong>9) When did Alan Ayckbourn stop directing work by other authors?<br></strong>Alan announced on his 60th birthday in 1999 that he intended to lessen his workload at the Stephen Joseph Theatre by concentrating on his own work. He stopped directing plays by other authors from this point with the sole exception of Tim Firth’s&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/directing-career/the-safari-party">The Safari Party</a></em>&nbsp;which opened at the&nbsp;<a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre">Stephen Joseph Theatre</a>&nbsp;in 2002 and transferred to the Hampstead Theatre, London, in 2003.</p>

<p><strong>10) Is it true Alan Ayckbourn considers himself a director first and a writer second?<br></strong>Alan has frequently said he is a director first and a playwright second. Traditionally, Alan has spent less than one month a year writing with at least three months (if not more) of the year dedicated to directing. In recent years, the emphasis in his work has swung back to writing and he would now probably consider himself a writer first and foremost. But for the majority of his career, he has considered himself a director first and a writer second.</p>
