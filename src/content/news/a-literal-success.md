<p>On Sunday night, Sir Alan Ayckbourn brought the inaugural Whitby Lit Fest to a successful conclusion with a wonderful In Conversation event.</p>

<p>The playwright regaled a packed audience at the Eighteen91 venue with stories from the influence of his mother – a professional writer – to writing his first play all the way to his latest play for 2026. Hosting the event was the festival’s patron, Kate Fenton.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/231-export-1-w600.png" alt="" width="600" height="428" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><em><sub>Sir Alan Ayckbourn with the Whitby Lit Festival patron, Kate Fenton (photo © David Kirtlan)</sub></em></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Alan told the packed and appreciative audience he was “absolutely thrilled to be here” and recalled how his first professional visit to Whitby had not been quite so successful! In 1974, The Library Theatre toured his play, <em>Confusions</em>, to the town and was greeted with small audiences.  When he asked why this might be, the response was hugely memorable…</p>

<p>“A man promptly stood up and he said, “I’ll tell you three good reasons. 1) We don’t like Scarborough 2) We don’t like theatre-in-the-round and 3) we don’t like you! And I said, thank you so much for your honest reply. I will hurry home immediately to Scarborough.”</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/237-export-w600.png" alt="" width="600" height="428" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Sir Alan Ayckbourn in conversation at Whitby Lit Fest (photo © David Kirtlan)</figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Fortunately, he had a far more welcoming and appreciative response this time round and he is absolutely delighted to have played a part in the huge success of the inaugural festival, which also included leading authors such as Lee Child, Rob Rinder, Yvette Fielding, Greta Scacchi, Steph McGovern and Miriam Margloyes – who attended Sir Alan’s talk – among many other national and local authors.</p>

<p>A spokesman for the festival added that Sir Alan’s talk was “the perfect end to a wonderful four days.”</p>

<p>So successful has the four-day festival been that dates for 2026 have already been announced and it will run from 19 – 22 November 2026. Further details can be found at <strong><a href="https://www.whitbylitfest.org.uk">whitbylitfest.org.uk</a></strong>.</p>
