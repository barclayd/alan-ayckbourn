<p>The Guardian has recently published a fascinating insight into the creation of <em>The Woman in Black</em> – which originated at Alan Ayckbourn’s home theatre in Scarborough in 1987.</p>

<p>Then the director Robin Herford, designer Michael Holt and writer Stephen Mallatratt adapted Susan Hill’s acclaimed novella for the stage on a shoe-string budget with memorable results.</p>

<p>The play went on from Scarborough to become a global sensation and became the second longest running play in the West End. The article, which interviews Robin Hertford and Susan Hill, can be found <strong><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/stage/2025/aug/04/how-we-made-the-woman-in-black">here</a></strong>.</p>

<p>If you want to find out even more about the play, then it has its own dedicated section on the <strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">A Round Town</a></strong> website, dedicated to chronicling theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough between 1955 and 1996.</p>

<p><em>The Woman in Black</em> section (click <strong><a href="http://thewomaninblack.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk">here</a></strong>) features a detailed look tab how the play was created as well as interviews with its key creatives, articles, details about the Scarborough productions of the play, reviews as well as archival images from the Scarborough productions.</p>

<p>Learn a little more about this extraordinary play and its humble start at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round in Scarborough in 1987 at the <strong><a href="http://thewomaninblack.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk">A Round Town</a></strong> website, run by Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist and theatre historian <strong><a href="http://www.simon-murgatroyd.com">Simon Murgatroyd</a></strong> M.A.</p>
