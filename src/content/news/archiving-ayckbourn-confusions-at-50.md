<p>Searching through the archives for material to celebrate the 50th anniversary of Alan Ayckbourn’s play <strong><em><a href="/plays/confusions">Confusions</a></em></strong>, I recently came across this piece of Ayckbourn history.</p>

<p>This letter is the earliest known reference to <em>Confusions</em> in the Ayckbourn Archive, which is held in the <strong><a href="http://www.alanayckbourn.net/page-10/page18.html">Borthwick Institute for Archives</a></strong> at the University of York.</p>

<p>It was written on 1 September 1974 to his agent, <strong><a href="/life/influences/ramsay-margaret">Margaret ‘Peggy’ Ramsay</a></strong>, the day he completed writing <em>Confusions</em>. It would open the same month at <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong>, Scarborough, on 30 September 1974.</p>

<p>This then is the earliest reference to one of his most famed works.</p>

<p>The letter makes specific mention of <em>Mother Figure</em> – one of the five plays which comprise <em>Confusions</em> – as that was already in existence and had been commissioned  and, briefly, performed as part of the anthology <em>Mixed Blessings</em>. Alan then took it back for <em>Confusions</em>.</p>

<p>Heth is, of course, his then partner and future wife, Heather Stoney. Whilst the Michael referred to is his frequent London producer Michael Codron, who would be very enthusiastic about <em>Confusions</em> and bring it to the West End in 1976.</p>

<p>The musical being referred to in relation to Andrew is <em><strong><a href="/plays/jeeves">Jeeves</a></strong></em>. Alan and Andrew Lloyd Webber collaborated on this, one of the great flops of British musical theatre and this was being revised at the time of the letter by Alan prior to its opening in the West End in 1975.</p>

<p>The ‘agonies of New York’ preview was the Broadway premiere of <em><strong><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a></strong></em> which opened on October 1974 at the Music Box Theatre and would prove to be a huge success, despite the playwright’s fears.</p>

<p>Fifty years on, it’s intriguing to see Alan’s thoughts on one his greatest hits as we continue to explore and peek into his fascinating archive.</p>
