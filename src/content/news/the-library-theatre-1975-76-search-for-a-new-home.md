<p>Throughout 2025 and 2026, the Ayckbourn Blog has been the celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> series of regular articles charting the history and people behind <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> now comes to a close as we reach 1975 / 76 and the increasingly fraught search for a new home for the company…</p>

<p><strong>1975 / 76: Search For A New Home </strong>by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>The need for a new – preferably permanent – home for the Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre company had been a fixture of discussions since 1963, but came to a head in 1975.</p>

<p>The company had been founded on the first floor of Scarborough Library in 1955 by Stephen Joseph and, for the duration of its seasons, essentially took up all the public rooms on that floor: the Concert Room as well as the Large and Small Lecture Rooms. In an increasingly hostile environment and with the company trying to expand its output, the company and the library management had become diametrically opposed.</p>

<p>This led, in January 1975, to the company being told it had to vacate Scarborough Library by January 1976. A solution was urgently needed to enable the company to continue in Scarborough.</p>

<p>There had actually been enquiries about possible new homes for the theatre since 1963. Sites considered and ultimately rejected included Regent Street, Castle Road, Valley Gardens, Burniston Road, Falsgrave, Vernon Road, Westborough and Filey Road.</p>

<p>A number of new possibilities were now raised, the most convenient being a conversion of the former Christ Church on Vernon Road, next to the Library (and which was later demolished to make way for an Iceland store). Interest was shown and plans drawn up, although the building was later deemed unsuitable for conversion.</p>

<p>Another interesting proposal was for the former maze and crazy golf site on The Esplanade. The theatre’s architect George W Alderson designed a striking circular building that was submitted to the Arts Council and the town council and did receive initial planning permission – despite objections the two storey building would spoil the view of the houses around it and there was no parking available.</p>

<p>Extensive preparatory work was completed but when ‘discovery’ trenches were dug to gauge the suitability of the site, the clay beneath was deemed an issue. The plan – budgeted at £160,000 – was abandoned. In its place, the council sold the land for the present building, the five-storey Dulverton Hall.</p>

<p>The most serious contender for a new home was the former St. Thomas’s Church in East Sandgate, which had been converted into the Museum Of Bygones and since 1974 had been used by the theatre for carpentry and as a prop-store.</p>

<p>Plans were drawn up for an extensive conversion despite its less than accessible location and perceived parking issues. Granted change of use and planning permission, it was publicised in the media that the £180,000 conversion was proceeding with a planned opening date of June 1976.</p>

<p>All this came to a dramatic halt on 29 October 1975 when Scarborough Town Council revealed previously secret plans for a purpose-built theatre opposite the library on Vernon Road car park (later the rear of the former Brunswick Pavilion, now the Square One cinema development) with permission to proceed approved in the same meeting.</p>

<p>With this surprise news, plans for St Thomas’s Church were abandoned and attention turned instead to the pressing matter of a temporary home for the company whilst the new theatre was built.</p>

<p>Although North Yorkshire County Council had agreed to extend Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre’s use of the library for another six months – it actually became eight months – there was a definitive refusal to a several year extension.</p>

<p>Fortunately, an alternative was on offer with the former <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-110/page39.html">Westwood County Modern School</a></strong>, beneath Valley Bridge, offered as a short term home by the County Council with a three year lease until the new theatre was complete.</p>

<p>Scarborough Theatre Trust agreed to move into Westwood – as it was colloquially known – in late 1976 at an estimated cost of £30,000 to convert the ground floor of the building into a practical theatre space.</p>

<p>At the same time, the new Vernon Road theatre was budgeted at £500,000 of which the Trust agreed to raise £120,000. Given the somewhat precarious financial position of the company at the time and the fact the established development fund for a new theatre held no more than £30,000 – which was ear-marked to cover the costs of converting Westwood – it is fair to assume Alan Ayckbourn would probably have footed most of the bill had it come to fruition.</p>

<p>Twenty-one years after Stephen Joseph had opened Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, the company performed in the Concert Room for a final time on 11 September 1976 with a performance of Alan Ayckbourn’s&nbsp;<em><strong><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves">Just Between Ourselves</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>Sixty days later, Theatre In The Round At Westwood opened on 26 October with a revival – and first Scarborough production – of Alan Ayckbourn’s&nbsp;<em><strong><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></strong></em>.</p>

<p>The hoped for new theatre in Vernon Road never transpired though and a three-year temporary home became a twenty year permanent home. In 1978, Theatre in the Round at Westwood was renamed as the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-15/">Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round</a></strong> to honour its founder.</p>

<p>But that’s another theatre and another story.</p>
