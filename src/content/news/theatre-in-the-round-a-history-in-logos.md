<p>As anyone reading this blog will no doubt have gathered, this years marks the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> opening <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – which today is better known as the Stephen Joseph Theatre.</p>

<p>As the blog continues its look into the history of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company and where Alan Ayckbourn made his playwriting debut, we’re looking at some of the company’s logos for every decade on from 1955 demonstrating both how it’s altered and also incorporated its anniversaries.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/library-01-c2a9-studio-theatre-ltd-w350.jpeg" alt="" width="350" height="248" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>1955 (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre opened on 14 July 1955 on the first floor of Scarborough Library. Its initial logo was essentially that of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-40/">Studio Theatre Ltd</a></strong>, the company created by Stephen Joseph to run the Library Theatre. It’s considered a bit of a classic within the company’s history encapsulating the idea of the audience surrounding the stage. In the humble opinion of this archivist, it’s never been bettered!</p>

<p>Now as to who designed it, it’s not altogether clear. Stephen was a clever designer and it’s entirely possible the logo is entirely his image and design. However, there’s also the possibility it was created by Stephen Garrett, an architect who Stephen Joseph had met whilst studying at Cambridge and who would go on to design the Getty Villa, before becoming the director of the Getty Museum in Los Angeles. Garrett was closely involved with designing the layout of the Library Theatre in 1955 as well as an accompanying theatre exhibition – some of the designs are still being held by Scarborough Library – and it’s a nice idea and not out of the realms of possibility.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/screenshot-2024-07-24-at-12-50-09-w300.png" alt="" width="300" height="298" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>1965 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Moving forward a decade, we have the second of the company’s logos. By 1965, Studio Theatre Ltd had left Scarborough to concentrate solely on running the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong>, Stoke-on-Trent, and Stephen had to form an entirely new company to run the Library Theatre – <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-40/">Scarborough Theatre Trust Ltd</a></strong>.</p>

<p>With a new company came a new logo which was used for about a decade and, again, it’s a rather effective design encompassing the idea of the theatre simply and effectively.</p>

<p>We jump forward to 1975 now and the company’s 20th anniversary and its final year at Scarborough Library. The logo was adapted again and this circular ‘theatre in the round’ design would form the basis for several designs over the coming years.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/1975-library-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w750.jpeg" alt="" width="750" height="214" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>1975 / 1976 / 1978 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>The following year, the company moved to a new ‘temporary’ home and was renamed Theatre in the Round at Westwood in 1976. By 1978, it was apparent the ‘temporary’ home was going to be more permanent and the theatre was renamed the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/">Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round</a></strong> in honour of its founder. Each design featuring the same circular ‘theatre in the round’ element.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/1980s-westwood-02-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w250.jpg" alt="" width="250" height="233" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>1985 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>By the company’s 30th anniversary in 1985, the company had adopted a new logo which was predominantly used throughout the 1980s. The emphasis is still on the circular and emphasising theatre-in-the-round, but it feels a little more of its era and, arguably, it’s not as timeless as some of the earlier logos. </p>

<p>For those wondering about the bold choice of colour, this doesn’t necessarily reflect its everyday use. The colour of the text and the background varied depending on the programme, here it reflects the bright primary colours of the world premiere poster for Alan Ayckbourn’s <em>Woman in Mind</em>. It’s still an interesting design though and would serve the company for about a decade before it was updated again.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/1995-westwood-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w112.jpg" alt="" width="112" height="250" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>1995 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>For the 40th anniversary, the company created the first of its celebration logos to actually mark an anniversary. In this case, it’s like a little addendum beneath the quite simple text only logo which was in place throughout the 1990s.</p>

<p>I have to be honest and say, the 1990s logo is one of my two least favourite of the 70 years of the theatre’s existence as this doesn’t really say anything about the company and, typographically, the font is neither particularly interesting nor the design of the words: ‘Theatre’ seemingly only being placed as it is, to allow for the ‘J’ of ‘Joseph’ above it rather than for any aesthetic choice.</p>

<p>But we can at least see the 40th anniversary being marked, setting a trend for the next couple of anniversaries for the company. </p>

<p>The following year in 1996, the company moved to its new home at Scarborough’s former Odeon cinema and with it came an entirely new design for the its logo.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/2005-sjt-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w214.jpg" alt="" width="214" height="250" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>2005 / 1996 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The 2005 Logo celebrated the 50th anniversary of the company’s founding and a special logo was commissioned which incorporated the company’s existing logo – used since 1996 and pictured to the right – updated with anniversary stylings.</p>

<p>This is one of the logos I quite like as it incorporates the ’round’ back into the design by making it part of the ’50’. The first time, there’d been a circular element to the design for about 15 years.</p>

<p>For those unfamiliar with the original SJT logo of this period (1996 – c.2021 – see above right), the geometric shape at the base of the logo is the profile of the theatre, which was a conversion of a 1930s Odeon cinema in the classic art nouveau style for which the cinema chain was renowned.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/2015-sjt-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w250.jpg" alt="" width="250" height="190" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>2015 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>This profile design became the prominent part of the 2015 logo when the company celebrated its 60th anniversary. The encompassing rectangle used throughout the past decade was removed and the emphasis shifted to the 60th anniversary and the profile of the building.</p>

<p>It’s an eye-catching design but, personally, I think it does require familiarity with the original logo and how the design connects to the theatre. The company’s title – abridged to SJT – is reduced to fit within the confines of the graphic, which seems to diminish the point of the anniversary. Also, if you weren’t familiar with the existing logo or how it’s a silhouette of the building, it might seem a bit odd. However, the assumption was probably that – after 20 years – there would be a fair degree of familiarity with both the logo design and the building.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/sjt-600x600-1-w250.png" alt="" width="250" height="75" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>2025 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Which brings us up-to-date and the logo for the company in 2025 as it celebrates its 70th birthday. This logo was introduced several year ago and marked a complete break with that which had been used for approximately 25 years since the company moved into the Odeon.</p>

<p>So there’s a quick look at how the identity of Theatre in the Round has been altered and promoted over the years as its developed and moved homes.</p>
