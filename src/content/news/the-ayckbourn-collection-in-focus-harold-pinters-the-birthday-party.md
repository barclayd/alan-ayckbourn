<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items currently on display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Ayckbourn Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>Design for Harold Pinter’s <em>The Birthday Party</em> by Stephen Joseph</strong></p>

<p>A highlight of the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums and Galleries is a piece which connects the playwright Harold Pinter with Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round in Scarborough.</p>

<p>Stephen Joseph didn’t just launch Alan Ayckbourn’s playwriting career, but he also played a very significant part in the earliest days of Pinter’s playwriting.</p>

<p>On 19 May 1958, Pinter’s debut play <em><a href="/career/actor/the-birthday-party">The Birthday Party</a></em> opened at the Lyric in Hammersmith, closing just eight days later. It was a huge blow to Pinter being a commercial and largely critical failure. Ironic, given it is now regarded as a classic of the period.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/sag-01-w1024.jpg" alt="" width="1024" height="536" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Stephen Joseph’s design for The Birthday Party (© Studio Theatre Ltd. Photo by Tony Bartholomew <a href="http://www.bartpics.co.uk">www.bartpics.co.uk</a>)</sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>Stephen Joseph had met Pinter at Central School of Speech and Drama and had a good regard of him and the play. He contacted Pinter in 1958 and invited him to direct the play as he would like it  it to be seen with two provisos: it had to be in-the-round and with Joseph’s Scarborough Studio Theatre Ltd company.</p>

<p>Pinter agreed and in December 1958 came to Scarborough where he rehearsed the play in the Concert Room at Scarborough Library. This would mark Pinter’s professional directorial debut. The production also included the 19 year old Alan Ayckbourn as Stanley – eight months before his own playwriting debut – who found the experience electrifying.</p>

<p>Stephen Joseph designed the set for in-the-round performance – with the original design now held by Scarborough Museums and Galleries and on display at Scarborough Art Gallery – and the production toured to Leicester and Birmingham where it was well-received, if not particularly well attended.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2023/sag-02-w1024.jpg" alt="" width="1024" height="536" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>The programme for the Studio Theatre Ltd production of Pinter’s The Birthday Party with Alan Ayckbourn as Stanley (© Studio Theatre Ltd and held in a private collection)</sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>Pinter is later reported to have said this production restored his faith in both the play and his own abilities and in his book on Pinter, author Michael Billington notes: “Joseph became one of those influential father-figures who crop ups throughout Pinter’s early life.” It was a significant moment in Pinter’s professional career and would be an inspiration moment for Alan Ayckbourn, who was in awe of this revolutionary playwright and the pair would stay in contact throughout Pinter’s life.</p>

<p>So Scarborough cannot only claim to playing a pivotal role in Alan Ayckbourn’s life and career, but also Harold Pinter. Not a bad combination!</p>
