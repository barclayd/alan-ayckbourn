<p>Sir Alan Ayckbourn, one of the UK’s most celebrated playwrights, will feature at the inaugural Whitby Lit Fest.&nbsp;</p>

</div>

<p>The award-winning playwright of 66 years and 91 plays will be joining 40 other authors including thriller writer Lee Child and broadcaster, barrister and author Rob Rinder at the event which is running from 6 to 9 November 2025.</p>

<p>Sir Alan said: “I am delighted to be a part of the inaugural Whitby Literary Festival. A new festival is especially welcome. In this age of quick-fire verbal and visual digital communication, it’s more important than ever to celebrate and savour the leisure of the written word.”&nbsp;</p>

<p>Kate Fenton, Patron of the festival, author and a former producer of BBC Radio 4’s <em>Bookshelf</em>, will interview Sir Alan on stage at the festival.&nbsp;</p>

<p>Kate said, “Not many book festivals can boast of having one of the world’s most celebrated dramatists living just down the road. We can, though. And Alan Ayckbourn, in person, is as funny as his plays – and as penetrating about human nature. In plays, he flips uproarious comedy into tears within a heartbeat; in conversation he’s fascinating about the nuts and bolts of his craft. Also, about what has kept this Londoner anchored up here in the North for nearly seventy years. Roll on November!”</p>

<p>Sir Alan Ayckbourn is a Tony and Olivier award-winning playwright whose was knighted for ‘services to theatre’ in 1997. Almost 40 of his plays have been produced in the West End including the National Theatre and the Royal Shakespeare Company and his work has been translated into more than 35 languages and is enjoyed around the world.</p>

<p>The vast majority of his plays though have originated and premiered in his adopted home-town of Scarborough which he first came to in 1957, where he met Stephen Joseph and joined his theatre company at Scarborough Library. Stephen encouraged Alan to write and direct and, in 1972, Alan took over the company and ran it for 37 years including overseeing the move into its present home, the Stephen Joseph Theatre.</p>

<p>Despite retiring as Artistic Director in 2009, he still directs the world premieres of his works at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, the latest of which – <strong><em><a href="/plays/earth-angel">Earth Angel</a></em></strong> – opens on 13 September in Scarborough.</p>

<p>The Whitby Lit Fest aims to celebrate storytelling in all its forms – bringing together authors, playwrights, poets, and readers for a weekend of events, conversations, and performances.&nbsp;</p>

<p>Lois Kirtlan, Committee Chair of the Whitby Lit Fest, said: “From the haunting ruins of Whitby Abbey to its rich history of storytelling, Whitby is ripe for its own literary festival. The coastal town is famed for its dramatic coastline, gothic heritage, and literary ties to Bram Stoker’s Dracula. It’s a fitting backdrop to inspire authors and book lovers alike.”</p>

<p>Further details about the festival – which will soon include dates for events and booking – can be found on the Whitby Lit Festival website at <a href="https://whitbylitfest.org.uk">https://whitbylitfest.org.uk</a>. The blog will carry details as soon as booking opens for the Alan Ayckbourn platform.</p>
