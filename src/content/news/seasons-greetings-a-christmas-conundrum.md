<p>So, as a bit of seasonal fun for all you Ayckbourn fans, a question. How many of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays have been set at or around Christmas?</p>

<p>That’s one to mull over your mulled wine.</p>

<p>They don’t have to be entirely set at Christmas, but Christmas must feature at some point. So how many plays do you think there are?</p>

<div><figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2019/seasons-greetings-c2a9-scarborough-theatre-trust-w800.jpg" alt="" width="800" height="800" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><em>© Scarborough Theatre Trust</em></figcaption></figure></div>

<p>And – yes – there’s obviously one as it’s featured in the title! Three, six, nine plays even?</p>

<p>While you have a guess, let’s run through them and see how many you remembered and how close you came to the actual number.</p>

<p>For clarity, this is just the 83 full-length, canon plays and doesn’t include any of Alan’s other writing.</p>

<p>So, in alphabetical order….</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a>:</strong> Easy one this. Set in three kitchens over three Christmases. The second most obvious festive play of Alan’s.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/a-brief-history-of-women">A Brief History of Women</a>:</strong> A very recent play, so perhaps not so obvious. The third act is set in December and centres around rehearsals for the Christmas panto and includes a wonderfully inappropriate song by the Dame.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind">Christmas V Mastermind</a>:</strong> An early obscure one for the Ayckbourn Afficiandoes. This was Alan’s fifth play and featured the evil machinations of a evil genius trying to disrupt Christmas.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/dads-tale">Dad’s Tale</a>:</strong> Even earlier in the play canon, this is Alan’s third play and is set over a miserable Christmas in which imagination makes up for the lack of anything else to give or receive.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/gameplan">GamePlan</a>:</strong> The first part of the <em>Damsels in Distress</em> trilogy encroaches upon Christmas in Act Two, Scene Two, which is set the week before complete with Christmas tree.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/improbable-fiction">Improbable Fiction</a>:</strong> The Literary mash-up play is set at a meeting of Pendon Writer’s Circle the week before Christmas.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/intimate-exchanges">Intimate Exchanges</a>:</strong> One of the possible codas to the play which has multiple permutations is called <em>A Midnight Mass</em> and set on Christmas Eve.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/joking-apart">Joking Apart</a>:</strong> Alan’s play centred around the golden couple Anthea and Richard features an undoubtedly classic Ayckbourn scene on Boxing Day with a tennis match between Richard and Sven.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/life-and-beth">Life &amp; Beth</a>:</strong> One of Alan’s supernatural plays and this one sees an alternative Ghost of Christmas Past arrive on Christmas Eve in the form of Beth’s late husband Gordon.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/seasons-greetings">Season’s Greetings</a>:</strong> There are no prizes for guessing this one!</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/suburban-strains">Suburban Strains</a>:</strong> But I should probably award a prize for anyone who remembered scene 31 of Alan and Paul Todd’s musical is set at Christmas!</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/sugar-daddies">Sugar Daddies</a>:</strong> It opens with ‘Santa Claus’ having nearly been knocked down. I think Christmas is a given here….</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/time-of-my-life">Time Of My Life</a>:</strong> During Act 1, Adam and Maureen celebrate Christmas early on 20 December with a Christmas dinner at Essa de Calvi – quite possibly one of the last places anyone would rationally want to have a Christmas dinner!</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/wildest-dreams">Wildest Dreams</a>:</strong> Alan Ayckbourn’s very dark play is centred around Christmas during the second act. Not that there’s much festive spirit!</p>

<p>I make that 14 plays. That’s quite a number really. Did you get them all? Did you spot any that I missed? </p>

<p><strong>So how did you do?</strong></p>

<p><strong>14:</strong> Congratulations, that’s pretty amazing. Your New Year’s resolution should probably be not to visit <a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a> quite so frequently….</p>

<p><strong>10 – 13:</strong> Very respectable. A round of applause there as you’ve remembered some quite obscure Ayckbourn facts.</p>

<p><strong>4 – 9:</strong> Average. You’ve remember the obvious ones probably and possibly a few slightly less well-known plays. Still a good score.</p>

<p><strong>2 – 3:</strong> You weren’t really trying, be honest.</p>

<p><strong>1:</strong> That’s rubbish. There’s one given away in the title of the blog!</p>

<p><strong>0:</strong> Really? <em>Really?</em> Your New Year’s Resolution should be to visit <a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a> far more regularly!</p>

<p>With that, it remains only for me to say Season’s Greetings and to wish everyone a happy new year! Thank you for supporting the blog and the website throughout the year.</p>

<p>Look forward to seeing you in 2020!</p>
