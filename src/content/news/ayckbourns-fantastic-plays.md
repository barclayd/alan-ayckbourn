<p>My recent blog articles looking at science fiction in Alan Ayckbourn’s plays have drawn a lot more interest than I anticipated!</p>

<p>They’ve also generated a number of questions, most notably, is sci-fi and fantasy really a major part of Alan’s writing and which plays would you consider fall into this category?</p>

<p>So, two crows with one stone, here’s my list of what I consider to be Alan Ayckbourn’s ‘fantastic’ plays. These are plays which have elements of sci-fi / speculative fiction, fantasy, fairy tale, supernatural, the weird or unusual or other ‘fantastic’ elements. I’ve tagged each play appropriately.</p>

<p>Please note, these are only drawn from Alan’s 90 full-length produced canon works and don’t include his children’s plays or other works such as the revues. Where a play is one of his ‘family’ full-length works (and part of the official play canon), it is clearly marked as such to demonstrate the majority of these works are part of his ‘adult’ (for want of a better term) writing.</p>

<p>I hope the list shows how significant and influential the ‘fantastic’ is to Alan’s writing for both his adult and family audiences.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/dads-tale">Dad’s Tale</a></strong> (1960): A play with fairy tale elements and dream sequences. It culminates in the fairy-like ‘Tinies’ appearing and changing the titular Dad into a budgie! <em>Not published, held in the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/standing-room-only">Standing Room Only</a></strong> (1961): Alan’s first sci-fi play set in 2010 in a gridlocked country where the Government is clandestinely sterilising the population. <em>Not published, held in the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind">Christmas V Mastermind</a></strong> (1962): A family play – probably more fairy tale than fantasy – centred around a criminal mastermind’s attempts to take over Christmas whilst Santa deals with industrial action at his workshop. <em>Not published, held in the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></strong> (1963): A surreal comedy in which a mute piano tuner brings chaos into the lives of an aristocratic family when he falls in love with the daughter. Scenes take surreal turns in a play inspired by black and white movies by directors such as René Clair. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/way-upstream">Way Upstream</a></strong> (1981): Difficult to characterise but there are certainly metaphorical if not full on fantasy elements as a cabin cruiser’s trip up the River Orb to Armageddon Bridge is disrupted by modern day pirates. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/woman-in-mind">Woman in Mind</a></strong> (1985): A knock on the head sees Susan’s fantasy family becoming more and more real to her with devastating consequences as the boundaries between reality and fantasy blur as she suffers a breakdown. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/henceforward">Henceforward…</a></strong> (1987): A sci-fi play set in a dystopian near future where a composer, who lives with a malfunctioning android, attempts to create his magnum opus. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/mr-as-amazing-maze-plays">Mr A’s Amazing Maze Plays</a></strong> (1988): Fantasy family play in which young Suzie’s dog has his bark stolen by Mr Accousticus and whose home she must explore to find it and other stolen sounds. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/invisible-friends">Invisible Friends</a></strong> (1989): Fantasy family play in which Lucy’s imaginary friend becomes alarmingly real and supplants Lucy’s real family with a fantasy one. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/body-language">Body Language</a></strong> (1990): At an exclusive clinic, a controversial scientist accidentally puts the wrong heads on the wrong bodies when a helicopter accident chops off the heads from a model and a journalist. Not sure whether this is sci-fi or fantastic! <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/this-is-where-we-came-in">This Is Where We Came In</a></strong> (1990): Family fairy-tale in which the ‘Players’ are forced to act out stories by the ‘Storytellers’, but who begin to rebel against their situation. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/callisto-5">Callisto 5 / Callisto#7</a></strong> (1990 / 1999): A family sci-fi set on a space station where two children find themselves fighting to survive against an invisible predator and a malign computer. <em>Callisto 5 published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/wildest-dreams">Wildest Dreams</a></strong> (1991): A role-playing game group is interrupted by a new member who has an adverse effect on all the members, who discover fantasy may not be the escape from reality they believe it to be. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/my-very-own-story">My Very Own Story</a></strong> (1991): Family fairy-tale in which story-tellers arrive and impose their stories on each other. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/dreams-from-a-summer-house">Dreams From A Summer House</a></strong> (1992): Musical fairy-tale in which Belle finds herself in suburbia with an artist falling in love with her and the beast, Baldemar, kidnaps his angry ex-wife. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/communicating-doors">Communicating Doors</a></strong> (1994): Alan’s first time-travelling play is a cross between <em>Back to the Future</em> and <em>Psycho</em> in which a hotel’s communicating door can go back 20 years offering a chance to avert murders and change lives. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/haunting-julia">Haunting Julia</a></strong> (1994): Alan’s first supernatural play with a father seeking to discover the truth behind the suicide of his musical genius daughter, who seems to be manifesting herself in her old room. But who is haunting whom? <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/the-musical-jigsaw-play">The Musical Jigsaw Play</a></strong> (1994): Family fantasy in which a pop group finds themselves in the place where groups who drop out of the charts end up. <em>Not published, held in the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/a-word-from-our-sponsor">A Word From Our Sponsor</a></strong> (1995): A musical fantasy set in a near future where a community mystery play finds its funding is coming from the devil themselves. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/the-champion-of-paribanou">The Champion of Paribanou</a></strong> (1996): A family fantasy loosely inspired by the tale of <em>The Magic Carpet</em>. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/comic-potential">Comic Potential</a></strong> (1998): A sci-fi play set in a future where an android develops a sense of humour. Is it a glitch or a sign of the next stage in android intelligence? <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/the-boy-who-fell-into-a-book">The Boy Who Fell Into A Book</a></strong> (1998): A family fantasy in which a young boy and a hard-boiled fictional detective find themselves lost in the books on a bookshelf, trying to find their way out. <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/virtual-reality">Virtual Reality</a></strong> (2000): A developer of holographic ‘viewdows” (CGI generated windows) find the lives of all those around him falling apart when he has an affair whilst, meanwhile, his creation is having inexplicable bugs and glitches. <em>Not published, held in the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/whenever">Whenever</a></strong> (2000): A time-travelling family musical inspired by<em> The Wizard of Oz</em> and <em>The Time Machine</em> (with bits of many other sci-fi books and films thrown in). <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/snake-in-the-grass">Snake In The Grass</a></strong> (2002): A supernatural thriller in which two sisters are haunted – perhaps literally – by an abusive father. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/the-jollies">The Jollies</a></strong> (2002): A family fantasy in which a Mum finds herself in a teenage body while her young son find himself in an adult body. <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/orvin-champion-of-champions">Orvin – Champion of Champions</a></strong> (2003): A fantasy musical in which a useless squire finds himself caught up in palace intrigue. <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/my-sister-sadie">My Sister Sadie</a></strong> (2003): Family sci-fi play in which a mysterious girl appears at the farm of a recently bereaved family. Hunted by the military, Sadie is not what she appears to be, not even human… <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/drowning-on-dry-land">Drowning on Dry Land</a></strong> (2004): A drama with a ‘weird’ element featuring a folly in a house’s grounds which can never be ascended. An illusion which plays a pivotal role in the play. <em>Published by Samuel French.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/miss-yesterday">Miss Yesterday</a></strong> (2004): A time-travelling family play in which a young girl is faced with choice of going back in time to save her brother’s life or letting him die to save the future of the planet. <em>Not published, held by the Borthwick Institute for Archives.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/improbable-fiction">Improbable Fiction</a></strong> (2005): Pendon writing group is intrigued by a mysterious girl and, as their literary creations come to life, she becomes the focal point in a series of mysteries. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/if-i-were-you">If I Were You</a></strong> (2006): A married couple find their lives turned upside-down – and having to understand each other better – when they inexplicably find themselves swapped into each other bodies. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/life-and-beth">Life &amp; Beth</a></strong> (2008): A recently bereaved wife finds her late husband is not willing to leave her and allow her – or himself – to move on and Beth finds herself telling her family some home truths about the type of man he really was. <em>Published by Samuel French.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/awaking-beauty">Awaking Beauty</a></strong> (2008): Fantasy musical which asks what happens after ‘happily ever after’ as Aurora and her prince deal with the horror of suburban life and the witch finds herself in love – or lust – with the prince. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/surprises">Surprises</a></strong> (2012): Sci-fi play with three strands about love centring on time-travel, an android and virtual avatars. <em>Published by Faber</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/consuming-passions">Consuming Passions</a></strong> (2016): A woman appears to have a precognition of a deadly future event, but is it real or in her mind? <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/better-off-dead">Better Off Dead</a></strong> (2018): A writer, pronounced dead by the press, finds himself struggling with his final novel as his characters begin to alarmingly take on a life of their own. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="http://thegirlnextdoor.alanayckbourn.net">The Girl Next Door</a></strong> (2021): Set during the Covid lockdown of 2020, a man discovers his neighbour is living through the Blitz in 1942. <em>Published by Samuel French</em>.</p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/welcome-to-the-family">Welcome to the Family</a></strong> (2023): In a near future, people can relive past events through virtual memories with a son introducing his fiancée to his deceased parents for approval… <em>Expected to be published in 2025.</em></p>

<p><strong><a href="/plays/constant-companions">Constant Companions</a></strong> (2023): In a near future where androids are common-place, androids have discovered the ability to love and three plot strands explore three very different relationships between human and artificial life.  <em>Expected to be published in 2025.</em></p>

<p>So, out of 90 full-length works, 40 of them have elements of the ‘fantastic’ in them. Probably a lot more than you realised highlighting just how significant this theme is to Alan Ayckbourn’s writing and to understanding his work.</p>

<p>You can find out more about all these plays on the playwright’s official website <strong><a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a></strong>.</p>
