<p>The West End revival of Alan Ayckbourn’s <em><strong><a href="/plays/woman-in-mind">Woman in Mind</a></strong></em> starring Sheridan Smith is now in full swing and here’s a round-up of <em>Woman in Mind</em> reviews and news.</p>

<p>Reviews for the production have generally been good with Sheridan Smith marked for particular high praise in the role of Susan. The production has received 5 stars reviews from Metro and The Jewish Chronicle with four star reviews from The Times, The Telegraph, The Mail and The Mail on Sunday.</p>

<p>The Times says “The writing is simply brilliant,” with The Mail adding “It’s themes of mental collapse are timeless and compelling.” The Guardian notes the play “stands the test of time for its originality and boldness,” while What’s On Stage writes “It’s a brilliantly conceived idea, and often very funny” and “Ayckbourn’s writing straddles the line between light laughter and domestic trauma with considerable finesse.” The Telegraph praises Ayckbourn and the play, “I’ve always thought this play to be one of Ayckbourn’s very best – it mixes his particular blend of suburban comic pathos with a febrile dash of horror. Longhurst’s revival is correspondingly both a Middle England sitcom and a psychedelic fever dream.” </p>

<p>With regard to Sheridan’s Smith’s praised performance, The Guardian says “Smith plays the part with whimsical daintiness and subtlety, throwing out mischievous or slighted looks. You feel her vulnerability, especially in the first act,” The Standard says “She has a uniquely vivid physical presence, and her emotions are shimmeringly close to the surface,” The Mail writes ”&nbsp;joyfully eﬀervescent, yet also dangerously intense and even disturbed.”</p>

<p>In The Times, concerns for Smith’s suitability are quickly dismissed, “By the end of the evening, though, as the visions spin out of control, like an overheated version of <em>Alice In Wonderland</em>, Smith’s twinkling eyes grow more and more hypnotic. How does she get through a role as gruelling as this night after night, surviving a rainstorm in the process?” The Independent writes: &nbsp;“Sheridan Smith (Funny Girl) brings both wit and emotional depth to its central role.” The final word goes to Metro, which writes “the evening belongs to Smith, whose command of comedy and tragedy is unmatched.&nbsp;“</p>

<p>The play is running at The Duke of York’s Theatre until 28 February and tickets are available from <strong><a href="https://www.womaninmindplay.com">www.womaninmindplay.com</a></strong>.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, if you’re wanting some insight into how the play was created – and Alan Ayckbourn’s career at large – there’s an excellent recent interview with the playwright with Sarah Hemming in the Financial Times, which can be found <strong><a href="https://www.ft.com/content/2ae955a1-7d8a-4569-a4c5-8dc416c17537">here</a></strong>.</p>

<p>In other news, Faber has published a new edition of <em>Woman in Mind</em> in their new typographical cover range of significant modern drama. The edition, which follows on from the release of <em><strong><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business">A Small Family Business</a></strong></em> last year, features a new cover and marks the first time Faber has republished <em>Woman in Mind</em> since the company originally published into in 1986.</p>

<p>The new edition is priced at £10.99 and available from the Faber website <strong><a href="https://www.faber.co.uk/product/9780571145201-woman-in-mind/?srsltid=AfmBOoqICSSbkwQ62CbJ5ILb380W8tQdlG0klaSK0Za3v15skuTIshr1">here</a></strong>.</p>

<p>Back with the West End revival, if you can’t make it to The Duke of York’s Theatre to see <em>Woman in Mind</em> or just find London ticket prices a little too eye-watering, then there are still tickets left for the production’s short tour following the end of its London run.</p>

<p>The production is at Sunderland Empire from 4 – 8 March with booking and details <strong><a href="https://www.atgtickets.com/shows/woman-in-mind/sunderland-empire/">here</a>,</strong> before the production moves to the Theatre Royal, Glasgow, from 10 – 14 March with bookings and details <strong><a href="https://www.atgtickets.com/shows/woman-in-mind/theatre-royal-glasgow/">here</a></strong>.</p>

<p><em>Woman in Mind</em> is directed by Michael Longhurst and stars Sheridan Smith as Susan with Romesh Ranganathan making his stage debut as Doctor Bill Windsor. The rest of the cast consists of Tim McMullan as Susan’s Husband Gerald, Louise Brealey as Gerald’s sister Muriel,&nbsp;Taylor Uttley as Susan’s son Rick, Sule Rimi as Andy,&nbsp;Chris Jenks as Tony,&nbsp;Safia Oakley-Green as Lucy with Kate Buchholz, Michael Woolfitt, Cameron Robertson, Jade Lauren and Marc Small. It is produced by Wessex Grove and Gavin Kalin Productions.</p>
