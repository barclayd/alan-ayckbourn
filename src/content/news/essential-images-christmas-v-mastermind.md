<p><em>Essential Images is a regular feature which looks at each of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays through a single, significant photograph from its original production.</em></p>

<p><strong>Essential Images: Christmas V Mastermind</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>Arguably one of the most obscure plays in the Ayckbourn canon, <a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind">Christmas V Mastermind</a> (1962) is a play which has barely been seen, rarely read and all but ignored by the playwright.</p>

<p>It was Alan Ayckbourn’s fifth full-length play and his second attempt at a children’s play for Christmas. That he would not attempt to write another children’s play for 26 years tells you more than enough about his feelings towards<em> Christmas V Mastermind</em>.</p>

<p>Yet the play has a few interesting points of note, starting with todays’s essential image featuring Heather Stoney as a fairy and Alan Ayckbourn as the titular criminal Mastermind.</p>

<div><figure><figcaption><sub>Heather Stoney &amp; Alan Ayckbourn in the world premiere of Christmas V Mastermind (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure></div>

<p>This is the first photograph of Heather and Alan together in one of the playwright’s own works. There have been subsequently been quite a few photographs together over the past six decades given Heather is Alan’s wife!</p>

<p>Heather joined the Studio Theatre Ltd company – of which Alan had been a member of since 1957 – in the summer of 1962 shortly before the company left its original home of The Library Theatre, Scarborough, to set up the Victoria Theatre in Stoke-on-Trent.</p>

<p>Both Heather and Alan left Scarborough to become founding members of the new theatre and for which Alan wrote two plays, <em>Christmas V Mastermind</em> and <em><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></em>.</p>

<p>So this is a photograph of the first of only five plays by Alan Ayckbourn which have not premiered in his adopted home town of Scarborough. It is, as noted, the first time Heather appeared in an Ayckbourn play – she would go on to appear in many more world premieres notably <em><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce">Bedroom Farce</a></em> and <em><a href="/plays/woman-in-mind">Woman in Mind</a></em>.</p>

<p>It is also the final time Alan Ayckbourn would act in one of his own plays, so this picture is one of first and finals!</p>

<p>The picture in question sees Heather playing the fairy secretary of Santa Claus, being threatened by the criminal mastermind – played by Alan – who hopes to take over Christmas. The scene culminates in the Mastermind putting a bulldog clip on the fairy’s wings and pushing her out of a window….</p>

<p>Fortunately, she survives.</p>

<p>Sadly, there is no photograph of Heather wearing the eye-patch she was forced to wear for several performances after accidentally splashing cleaning fluid in her eyes – quite possibly the first pirate fairy to be seen on stage!</p>

<p>It’s also a notable photograph as there are only five surviving photographs from the production and little else is held in archive – just four press cuttings and a couple of programmes.</p>

<p><em>Christmas V Mastermind</em>, like <em><a href="/plays/dads-tale">Dad’s Tale</a></em> before it, was a disaster. Due to a lack of publicity, a lack of awareness of how to attract school-bookings and a freakish cold-spell in what was already a cold theatre, the play attracted very few people to see it; Alan has frequently told how one woman furiously announced she intended never to see another play after her experience!</p>

<p><em>Christmas V Mastermind</em> ran for three weeks and has never been seen again. It was withdrawn from production fairly quickly after its run finished at the Victoria Theatre and has never been published and, for many years, Alan told anyone who asked that he had hunted down and destroyed all copies of the play. A lie, there’s at least three copies in various archives around the country.</p>

<p>The play itself has little to recommend it – it’s a children’s play which features industrial action in Santa’s factory, not the most enticing of prospects – and is easily one of the most obscure and least well-known (and seen) of all of Alan’s plays.</p>

<p>The playwright has frequently said it marked the low point of his playwriting career. On the bright side, given there’s another 60 years of plays to follow, it means the only way is up!</p>

<p>“<em>Christmas V Ma</em>stermind was the most disastrous play I’ve ever done… I probably shouldn’t have written it… It was received in dreadful silence. None of it seemed to succeed and we died the death with it.” <em>Alan Ayckbourn</em></p>
