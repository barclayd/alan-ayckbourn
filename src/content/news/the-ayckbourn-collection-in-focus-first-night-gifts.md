<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items within the display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>The Crystal Goblet / First Night Gifts</strong></p>

<p>One of the longstanding, largely untold stories about Alan Ayckbourn is the first night gifts presented by the playwright to his company.</p>

</div>

<p>This has been a tradition for decades and yet very few people outside of Alan Ayckbourn’s world premiere companies are aware of the gifts or their significance. They are treasured objects and Scarborough Art Gallery is currently displaying the first example to be donated to a public gallery.</p>

<p>Ever since the early 1970s, Alan has given a commemorative drinking vessel to mark the first night of a world premiere of his work. The tradition can be traced back to 1973 and the first night of <em><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests">The Norman Conquests</a></em> trilogy; although it was actually only known as ‘the trilogy’ at the time.</p>

<p>Alan presented each of the six actors a pewter tankard with the name of the three plays and the date. Over time, these first night gifts became crystal goblets – initially hand-engraved – with the title of the play and the year, given to the actors and later stage management and occasionally company members who had contributed significantly to the production.</p>

<p>Sadly, Sir Alan doesn’t have any of the earlier pewter gifts as he only gave them to his company. Later he would have keep one of the goblets for each of the world premieres himself and, later still, his wife – Heather Stoney – had glasses etched for the plays prior to when the goblets began to be given out.</p>

<p>Sir Alan is the only person to have a complete set of the crystal goblets, one for every single play. Given there’s – as of writing – 89 full-length plays and numerous other sundry works, all with goblets, you can imagine they’re rarely all used at the same time! Although the playwright does use them and they’re not just for decoration.</p>

</div>

<p>The glass featured in the display – and which is now part of the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums and Galleries – is for his 2002 play, Snake in the Grass, and was donated by his archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</p>

<p>“I was well aware,” said Simon, “that very few people knew the lovely story about these previous first night gifts and thought it would be an interesting donation as part of The Ayckbourn Collection.</p>

<p>“I’m lucky enough to have ten – well, nine now actually! – of the glasses from my two decades of work with Alan Ayckbourn and each one is special because they’re not automatically given to anyone but the company. So when I receive one, I know I’ve done something which has caught Alan’s attention and he’s recognised it with a goblet.”</p>

<p>The tradition continues to this day and, pictured here, is the latest addition. The goblet for <em><a href="/plays/constant-companions">Constant Companions</a></em> which premiered at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, in September 2023.</p>
