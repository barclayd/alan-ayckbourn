<p>When someone has had a career so prolonged and prolific as Alan Ayckbourn, it’s occasionally easy to take his many achievements for granted.</p>

<p>Particularly since Alan abandoned the West End in 2001 letting only revivals of his plays and none of his new work appear in London, his success – and ongoing work – occasionally seems to be ignored. Ironic, given between 2005 and 2018 his productions in New York conversely generated critical and popular acclaim.</p>

<p>Sometimes, it’s good to be reminded of  just how much of an impact Alan Ayckbourn made on British theatre during the 1960s to 1990s – just how popular he was and how he dominated the West End.</p>

<p>And dominate he did, between 1972 and 2000, there wasn’t a single year when there wasn’t between one and five Ayckbourn productions running in the West End. He was working in the West End, at the National Theatre and even for the Royal Shakespeare Company. More than 35 of his individual plays were produced in the West End during that time – not including revivals.</p>

<p>And then there were the awards. Quite a lot of them, in fact. And that’s what I’m highlighting here.</p>

<p>For years, as the playwright’s archivist, I’ve wanted to take this picture but Sir Alan’s awards have been scattered in various locations. So this is the first time ever that Alan’s major playwriting awards have all been gathered in one place…</p>

<p>That’s seven Evening Standard Awards, two Oliviers, a Tony Award, the Writers’ Guild of Great Britain Lifetime Achievement Award and the Lloyds Private Banking Playwright of the Year.</p>

<p>It’s pretty impressive, isn’t it?</p>

<p>Starting at the back we have his seven (!) Evening Standard Awards received between 1973 and 1990. By my calculations – and I stand to be corrected – Alan is the second most lauded playwright in the Evening Standard Awards after Tom Stoppard with nine awards and just ahead of Michael Frayn with six.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/img-8246-w700.jpg" alt="" width="700" height="328" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>The Seven Sisters… Alan Ayckbourn’s seven Evening Standard Awards (© Simon Murgatroyd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>It’s truly an impressive haul and includes Awards for <em><strong><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a></strong></em> (Best Comedy); <strong><em><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests">The Norman Conquests</a></em></strong> (Best Play); <strong><em><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves">Just Between Ourselves</a></em></strong> (Best Play); <em><strong><a href="/plays/a-chorus-of-disapproval">A Chorus of Disapproval</a></strong></em> (Best Comedy); <em><strong><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business">A Small Family Business</a></strong></em> (Best Play) <em><strong><a href="/plays/henceforward">Henceforward…</a></strong></em> (Best Comedy); <em><strong><a href="/plays/man-of-the-moment">Man of the Moment</a></strong></em> (Best Comedy).</p>

<p>Then we have the two Olivier Awards, the first for Best Comedy with Alan’s production of <em>A Chorus of Disapproval</em> at the National Theatre in 1985. Next to it is his Special Award received in 2009 marking a lifetime of theatrical achievement.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/img-8255-w700.jpg" alt="" width="700" height="739" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn’s two Olivier Awards (© Simon Murgatroyd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>This was also recognised by the Tonys in 2010 when he received the Special Tony Award for Lifetime Achievement in Theatre. It’s worth mentioning that Alan’s famed trilogy <em>The Norman Conquests</em> is also the recipient of a Tony, but it wasn’t one of Alan’s own productions but rather the acclaimed 2009 transfer of Matthew Warchus’s production from the Old Vic.</p>

<p>And then there are two writing awards, the Writers’ Guild of Britain Lifetime Achievement Award and the Lloyds Private Banking Playwright of the Year Award – which was only awarded the once! So that’s truly a unique achievement – and a rather smart award.</p>

<p>What isn’t shown are the Variety Club of Great Britain Award for Best Playwright (stolen during the ’80s), the Montblanc de la Culture Award for Europe (Alan still uses the pen), his two TMA Regional Theatre Awards and the other numerous awards including recognition by Plays and Players and DRAMA as well as the awards received for productions of Alan’s work such as two Molière awards and various awards for the 2009 revival of <em>The Norman Conquests</em>.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/img-8260-w700.jpg" alt="" width="700" height="313" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Some of Sir Alan’s favourite awards in situ at his home (© Simon Murgatroyd)</sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>In all, Alan and his associated work has received more than 35 major awards over the course of his career. A phenomenal achievement and which does included other honours such as his knighthood, honorary doctorates and recognition such as Director Emeritus of the Stephen Joseph Theatre in Scarborough.</p>

<p>These awards demonstrate just how significant Alan Ayckbourn has been to British playwriting over the decades and the place he has in British theatre history, which should not be forgotten nor underestimated.</p>
