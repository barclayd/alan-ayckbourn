<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>In part 3, we’re looking at questions regarding Alan Ayckbourn’s relationship with Scarborough and the Stephen Joseph Theatre…</em></p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Writing</strong></p>

<div>

</div>

<p><strong>1) When did Alan Ayckbourn first come to Scarborough?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn first came to Scarborough in 1957 when he was offered the role of Acting Stage Manager with Stephen Joseph’s Studio Theatre company at <a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/theatre-in-the-round-at-the-library-theatre">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a>.</p>

<p>Prior to 1957, Alan had never visited the town and apparently had no idea where it was having never been further north than Potter’s Bar when growing up.</p>

<p><strong>2) When did Alan Ayckbourn permanently move to Scarborough?<br></strong>Alan moved back to Scarborough in 1972, when he was appointed <a href="/career/artistic-director">Artistic Director</a> of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre. He permanently moved back to the town in 1974 when the theatre essentially became a year-round concern.</p>

<p><strong>3) Does Alan Ayckbourn still live in Scarborough?<br></strong>He does. He continues to live in Scarborough’s old town where he has been resident since the early 1970s.</p>

<p><strong>4) What position does Alan Ayckbourn currently hold at the Stephen Joseph Theatre?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn is currently a guest director and writer at the Stephen Joseph Theatre. He also holds the honorary position of <a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/director-emeritus-sjt">Director Emeritus</a> (see below)</p>

<p>When Alan Ayckbourn stepped down as Artistic Director of the company in 2009, he made it clear he wished to make a clean break and would no longer be involved with the administration, running or oversight of the company.  He is not part of Scarborough Theatre Trust board nor holds any other permanent position at the SJT.</p>

<p><strong>5) What have been Alan Ayckbourn’s roles at the Stephen Joseph Theatre?<br></strong>Alan has held a number of roles at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in all it’s forms from Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (1955 – 1976), the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round (1976 – 1995) and the SJT (1996 – present). Further details can be found in the&nbsp;<a href="/career">Careers</a>&nbsp;section of Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website.<br>1957 – 1958: Acting Stage Manager (stage manager / actor)<br>1958 – 1962:&nbsp;<a href="http://careers.alanayckbourn.net/styled-3/index.html">Actor</a><br>1959 – 1962:&nbsp;<a href="/career/playwright">Writer in residence</a>&nbsp;(alongside David Campton)<br>1961 – 2008:&nbsp;<a href="/career/directing-career">Director</a><br>1965 – 2008:&nbsp;<a href="http://careers.alanayckbourn.net/styled-3/index.html">Writer</a><br>1969 – 1970, 1972: Director of Productions (appointed annually by Scarborough Theatre Trust)<br>1972 – 2009: Artistic Director (Permanent contract replacing the role of Director of Productions)<br>2009 – present: Guest director and writer<br>2018 – present:&nbsp;Director Emeritus</p>

<p><strong>6) When did Alan Ayckbourn become the Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre?<br></strong>12 November 1972. This has often been misquoted as 1970 (or even 1969 or 1971) but the minutes of Scarborough Theatre Trust are quite clear he was asked to take on the annually appointed role of Director Of Productions for the&nbsp;Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre&nbsp;in 1972 for the summer season and then, on 12 November,  was offered the permanent position of&nbsp;Artistic Director&nbsp;of the theatre. The confusion possibly arises as he held the annually appointed seasonal position of Director of Productions in 1969, 1970 and 1972. For a full discussion of this subject, click&nbsp;<a href="http://careers.alanayckbourn.net/styled-6/styled-7/styled-8/ArtisticDirector.html">here</a>.</p>

<p><strong>7) When did Alan Ayckbourn step down as Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre?<br></strong>Alan Ayckbourn stepped down as Artistic Director of the Stephen Joseph Theatre on 31 March 2009 – although to all intents and purposes, he left the position on 17 January 2009 when the 2008/9 winter season ended.</p>

<p><strong>8) Why are there no longer revivals of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays in Scarborough?</strong><br>There’s no definitive answer to this and it’s a question that should be directed to the Stephen Joseph Theatre rather than Alan Ayckbourn. However, from his perspective, it’s not because Alan is no longer interested in or does not want to revive his plays. As of 2023, Sir Alan would be delighted to be asked to direct revivals of much of his play canon – be it in Scarborough or further afield – and is discussing directing a potential revival in 2024 with another company.</p>

<p>In Scarborough, the last revival of an Ayckbourn play was&nbsp;<em><a href="http://http;//seasonsgreetings.alanayckbourn.net">Season’s Greetings</a></em>&nbsp;in 2019, directed by the playwright, at the Stephen Joseph Theatre. A revival of <em><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves">Just Between Ourselves</a></em> was scheduled and announced for 2020 also at the SJT, but was cancelled due to the Covid pandemic. Subsequently, there have been no revivals of the playwright’s work at his home theatre.</p>

<p>As noted above (see Question 4), Alan Ayckbourn has no input in the scheduling or administration of the SJT; the decision what the company stages is – obviously – the responsibility of the management and board.</p>

<p><strong>9) What is a Director Emeritus?<br></strong>On 20 April 2018, Alan Ayckbourn was appointed Director Emeritus of the Stephen Joseph Theatre. Director Emeritus is an honorary accolade which denotes the perpetual status of an individual who has moved an organisation to new heights. The appointment recognised more than six decades work with the company and Alan’s many contributions and achievements.</p>

<p><strong>10) How many of Alan Ayckbourn’s full length plays have premiered in Scarborough?<br></strong>As of summer 2023, 83 of Alan Ayckbourn’s 89 plays have premiered in Scarborough at either Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, the Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round or the Stephen Joseph Theatre (or, in the case of&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/anno-domino">Anno Domino</a></em>&nbsp;(2020), premiered as a stream on the SJT website). The plays which did not receive their premieres in Scarborough are:&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/christmas-v-mastermind">Christmas V Mastermind</a></em>&nbsp;(1962) and&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/mr-whatnot">Mr Whatnot</a></em>&nbsp;(1963), which premiered at the Victoria Theatre, Stoke-on-Trent;&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/jeeves">Jeeves</a></em>&nbsp;(1975), which premiered at Her Majesty’s Theatre, London;&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business">A Small Family Business</a></em>&nbsp;(1987), which premiered at the National Theatre, London;&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/all-lies">All Lies</a></em>&nbsp;(2022) and&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/welcome-to-the-family">Welcome to the Family</a></em>&nbsp;(2023) which premiered at the Old Laundry Theatre, Bowness-on-Windermere.</p>

<p><strong>11) Are Alan Ayckbourn’s Scarborough productions – particularly his world premieres – recorded?<br></strong>No. Historically – until 2020 – Sir Alan discouraged the recording of live productions of his work as he firmly believes theatre is ‘live’ and, essentially, transient. He has never been truly convinced filming a play captured anything but a shadow of the original work and does not reflect the live experience.</p>

<p>He has also been of the opinion that, until recently, it’s been very difficult to successfully film works in-the-round – where the vast majority of his work is conceived for and premiered.</p>

<p>However, with the Covid pandemic disrupting theatres during 2020 /2021, Alan agreed to let his play <em>The Girl Next Door</em> be filmed and streamed by the Stephen Joseph Theatre. Alan believed this was a success and it assuaged his doubts about recording his work in-the-round. The following year, he agreed his next play at the SJT, <em>Family Album</em>, could also be streamed. Since then, there have been no further recordings of his work at the SJT. As with the question of revivals (see question 8), the decision whether to record productions is entirely one for the producing theatre and not one Alan has any say over except to approve any proposal.</p>

<p><strong>12) Is – or was – Scarborough considered the try-out for Alan Ayckbourn’s plays before they are performed elsewhere?<br></strong>No. The playwright considers that the Scarborough productions are ‘the’ productions and frequently the definitive productions. He rarely alters the plays after their premiere in Scarborough and frequently cites them as his most memorable productions – produced in-the-round as intended with the ensemble company of his choice. As he wrote in 1991, “I&nbsp;<em>never</em>&nbsp;regard the Scarborough production as a trial run. To my mind, it is&nbsp;<em>the</em>&nbsp;production and, looking back, in some instances, they have been the definitive productions.”</p>
