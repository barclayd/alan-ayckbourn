<p>in 2024, Alan Ayckbourn’s popular play <strong><a href="/plays/confusions"><em>Confusions</em></a></strong> celebrates its 50th anniversary.</p>

<p>The Blog concludes its celebration of the play with a gallery of some notable posters associated with the play over the past five decades, drawing on resources held in the Ayckbourn Archive at the <strong><a href="http://www.alanayckbourn.net/page-10/page18.html">Borthwick Institute for Archives</a></strong> at the University of York.</p>

<p><em>Confusions</em> premiered at <strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">Theatre in there Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> on 30 September 1974 and, interestingly, it had the first colour glossy programme at the venue. The colour bi-fold programme is quite rare and marks the debut of one of Alan’s most popular works.</p>

</div>

<p><em>Confusions</em> immediately went on tour and the poster was re-designed with a new logo as seen by this programme for the Humberside Theatre at Spring Street in Hull – the forerunner of Hull Truck Theatre.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/1974-confusions-tour-hull-w500.jpeg" alt="" width="500" height="742" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions world premiere tour programme – 1974 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The company’s winter season was a bizarre hybrid as it only had limited use of it some space at the Library Theatre. This meant the season was split between weekly performances in Scarborough, Filey and Whitby. The poster for Filey Sun Lounge removes all logo and images just to present the details of the season.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/1974-confusions-filey-sun-lounge-w1024.tiff" alt="" width="500" height="653" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions winter season programme – 1974 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The production was then revived for the summer 1975 season at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, and underwent yet another change of poster image. The experiment into glossy programmes was very short-lived and the company moved to mono printing on card for the season. The image utilises a picture of Stephen Mallatratt as Stewart in <em>Gosforth’s Fête</em>.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/1975-confusions-cover-w500.jpg" alt="" width="500" height="766" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions revival – 1975 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The following year saw <em>Confusions</em> open in the West End with a production starring John Alderton and Pauline Collins – as clearly seen by their above the title prominence. Somewhat ironic given Alan specifically wrote this play as an ensemble work rather than a star-driven one.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/1976-confusions-flyer-w500.jpg" alt="" width="500" height="773" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions West End premiere – 1976 (© Michael Codron Productions)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Sadly, there is no image of an intriguing production staged at the playwright’s home theatre in 1991. It is very rare for Alan Ayckbourn not to direct his own plays in Scarborough and this production is one of the few examples of this – at the time Alan was involved in initiating his plan to move the company into the town’s former Odeon cinema and so the production at the <strong><a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre-in-the-round">Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round</a></strong> was taken on by the company’s Associate Director Malcom Hebden.</p>

<p>Despite the unusual nature of the production, very little survives in an archive relating to it and the posters are very rare. All we have is a picture of the programme which, as at that point the company produced season rather than individual production programmes, doesn’t offer any real insight into the production.</p>

<p>We skip forward a couple of decades now to Alan Ayckbourn’s 2015 revival at the <strong><a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre">Stephen Joseph Theatre</a></strong> to mark the company’s 60th anniversary. This cartoon image for <em>Confusions</em> was adopted by Bloomsbury for its reprint of the playtext and has subsequently been frequently reproduced (and occasionally plagiarised without permission!) by other companies. </p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/2016-confusions-cover-w500.jpeg" alt="" width="500" height="634" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions revival – 2015 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The version reproduced below is its concept design. At the time, the Stephen Joseph Theatre was giving tag-lines to its productions and for <em>Confusions</em>, the marketing department suggested ‘A shockingly good play’. Alan wasn’t impressed – as it really doesn’t sum up the play, just one very specific moment in <em>Gosforth’s Fête</em> – and it wasn’t used in the final poster. Note how the theatre logo is different too, at the time of creating the concept the final 60th anniversary logo hadn’t been finalised.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2024/screenshot-2024-08-29-at-09-05-22-1-w500.png" alt="" width="500" height="615" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Confusions concept design – 2015 (© Scarborough Theatre Trust)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The most recent image of note is really the play’s New York premiere in 2016 at the <em>Brits Off Broadway</em> festival at the 59E59 Theatres. The image features Elizabeth Boag as Mrs Pearce in <em>Gosforth’s Fête</em> and whilst not particularly memorable, it was designed within the design parameters for the venue’s programmes at this time.</p>

<p>So there’s our whistle-stop tour of how <em>Confusions</em> has been promoted over the decades. Do you have a favourite poster for <em>Confusions</em> – from the above or from another production? Why not let us know and leave a comment on the blog.</p>

<p>With that, it’s happy 50th birthday to <em>Confusions</em>. Here’s to the next five decades!</p>
