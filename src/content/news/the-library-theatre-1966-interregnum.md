<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1966 and the amateur season which helped save Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p><strong>1966: Interregnum</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>1966 is an odd – yet pivotal – year in the history of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>It marks a brief pause in the professional activities of the company founded by Stephen Joseph in 1955. On one hand, it is entirely unrelated to the professional company, but on the other, the events of the year ensured there was still a professional company in 1967.</p>

<p>In 1965, Stephen Joseph announced Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was to close. Several years of frustrated negotiations with Scarborough Borough Council had led him to the conclusion the support desperately needed for the company to expand and move forward was not there.</p>

<p>As a result, at the end of 1965 summer season, Stephen announced the theatre was to close. This was not a bluff on his part to kick-start negotiations, but a genuine decision to move onto something else.</p>

<p>He had delivered ultimatums in previous years to no avail and now – despite 1965 being one of the theatre’s most successful summer seasons – Stephen decided to call it quits, as can be seen in this extract from his book <em>Theatre In The Round</em> (Barrie &amp; Rockliff, 1967).</p>

<p>“It became clear that a place [Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre] that was admirable for its first steps, had been outgrown entirely; and just as there is discomfort in wearing clothes that are too small, and an embarrassment in wearing fashions that have just gone out, so the company felt increasingly un comfortable and unhappy at the Library. We wanted to develop in every way, and, if this was not possible, the sensible alternative seemed to be to abandon Scarborough altogether.”</p>

<p>However, the theatre manager – <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-7/styled-10/">Ken Boden</a></strong> – was not willing to give up quite so easily. Having worked with Stephen for more than a decade in Scarborough, he believed there was a future for a professional theatre-in-the-round company in the town and began steps to continue it.</p>

<p>It appears he began making plans for the future soon after Stephen announced the theatre’s closure. Late in 1965, he approached Stephen with a proposal Scarborough Theatre Guild organise an eight week in-the-round amateur season for 1966 at The Library Theatre. Stephen – apparently reluctantly – agreed, unaware of Ken’s long term ambitions.&nbsp;</p>

<p>For there is little doubt Ken was working to a larger plan. Stephen had previously said the venue would not survive a year’s closure; the amateur season would keep the venue open and give Ken time to plan a revival of professional theatre.</p>

<p>This wasn’t even such an extreme idea. Scarborough had a plethora of talented amateur companies at the time and they had been producing plays in-the-round in a pre-summer season for several years as well as participating in the annual theatre-in-the-round festival at the library.</p>

<p>Ken approched several companies and arranged for four of them to produce a play in-the-round with each playing for two weeks each during the eight week season – to be capped by the annual in-the-round festival.</p>

<p>The season included The Cresta Players presenting <em>Doctor in the House</em> by Ted Willis (7 – 13 July &amp; 4 – 10 August); Filey Drama Club with <em>Intent to Murder</em> by Leslie Sands (14 – 20 July &amp; 11 – 17 August); Phoenix Drama Club presenting <em>Bonaventure</em> by Charlotte Hastings (21 – 27 July &amp; 18 – 24 August); Outsiders Drama Club with <em>Father Matthew</em> by Aubrey Colin (28 July – 3 August &amp; 25 August – 27 August).</p>

<p>The season then ended with the Seventh In The Round Festival from 3 – 10 September, adjudicated by Rodney Wood.</p>

<p>The season was a success and gave Ken the time to finalise his ideas for the return of professional theatre to the Library Theatre during the summer of 1967.</p>

<p>A key piece of correspondence held at Scarborough Library from May 1966 explains that Ken hoped the British Drama League could run the 1967 season; in essence an amateur company presenting professional plays.</p>

<p>Contacting organisations such as the Arts Council, Scarborough Town Council and the Libraries Committee as well as other key figures, Ken ascertained there was support for reviving the venue and that financially it might be viable.</p>

<p>He then told Stephen of his more ambitious plans. Stephen was broadly supportive and offered to transfer Scarborough Theatre Trust to a new board. What sealed the revival, though, was Scarborough Town Council reversing a funding cut made in 1965.</p>

<p>All this came to a conclusion on 12 October 1966 at a meeting of Scarborough Theatre Trust’s board prior to the Annual General Meeting, which also marked a very sad point in history for the company. By this time, it was known that Stephen had terminal cancer and had been given only months to live.</p>

<p>This presented itself during the meeting, which was the last that Stephen was to attend of the company which he had founded. Having been resolved the company would relaunch in 1967, Stephen stepped down as chairman. </p>

<p>“Owing to the state of his health Mr Joseph felt that the time had come when he must hand over the Chairmanship of the Trust to someone else.”</p>

<p>Thus ended Stephen’s 10 year association with the company as, although the minutes also note he would stay involved with the theatre, in practise he had little contact with the company over the coming months due to both his deteriorating health and a fight over the control of the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong> in Stoke-on-Trent, which he had founded in 1962.</p>

<p>That Stephen was not well is sadly obvious from later in the minutes when it records: “After this point the meeting was continued without Stephen Joseph.” An innocuous enough statement, but immediately after this meeting closed, the Trust held its Annual General Meeting, whose minutes note “Mr [David] Campton took the chair in the absence of Stephen Joseph through illness.”</p>

<p>Ken Boden was appointed General Manager in charge of the voluntary workforce the company now depended on more than ever to run the theatre, due to its parlous financial situation. A Director Productions was appointed for the 1967 summer season in Rodney Wood – who had previously been a theatre manager at the theatre and who had brought Alan Ayckbourn into the company.</p>

<p>The stage was set for a revival of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1967, but it would be a year marked by both success and tragedy….</p>
