<p>Today’s item is a genuine historic curiosity. It dates back to 23 December 1986 and is the programme which accompanied Alan Ayckbourn being given the Freedom of the Borough of Scarborough.</p>

<p>It is part of the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums and Galleries – a number of items from which are currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery – and the Ayckbourn Archive at the Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York.</p>

<p>Why, you might well ask though, is it a historic curiosity?</p>

<p>On 1 April 2023, a unitary authority was created for Yorkshire. The former county council and seven district and borough authorities were combined to create a single unitary authority or North Yorkshire Council as it’s now known.</p>

<p>Scarborough Borough Council ceased to exist and, as a result, there will be no more awards of the Freedom of the Borough. So Alan Ayckbourn is one of the few surviving people who have received the Freedom of the Borough.</p>

<p>The last people to receive the freedom were the distinguished athletes Beth Mead and Zoe Aldcroft in 2023, both born within the old borough; Beth is a footballer who received the golden boot and player of the Euros 2022 tournament as part of the victorious England team. Zoe has represented the England national rugby union team and was named World Rugby Player of the year in 2021.</p>

<p>Returning to Alan, he received his Freedom of the Borough in 1986, as can be seen within the programme reproduced here. The cover and two interior pages are included highlighting the contents of the Freedom and the order of the day’s events.</p>

<p>The actual event was notable because Alan received the freedom alongside the noted violinist Max Jaffa, who had led Scarborough Spa Orchestra for 27 years and was credited with having played to approximately 3 million holiday makers over that duration.</p>

<p>However, what should have been a celebration of two people who had contributed much to the town became somewhat controversial. Several councillors objected to the fact Jaffa was to receive the Freedom as they argued, essentially, he was an employee of Scarborough Council and only doing his job!</p>

<p>As a result, when the ceremony took place, only half of the town’s council’s 48 members attended with the absentees including every single member of the Labour group as well as several Conservative and other party members.</p>

<p>Officially, the reason given  for this was bad weather had affected attendance and the fact  a number of councillors lived in rural areas was to blame. But it was widely known and reported that many of the absent councillors were deliberately boycotting the event.</p>

<p>Despite this, the ceremony went ahead and both men were praised for their contributions to the town and its cultural heritage. Both were awarded framed vellum scrolls, the contents of which were reprinted within the programme which is reproduced here. </p>

<p>There appears to be no accessible definitive list of who received the Freedom of the Borough over the decades,. but prior to Alan, only 28 people had received it since 1900 and subsequent to December 1986, probably an approximate equal number of people also received the Freedom of the Borough.</p>

<p>It is a select list and very few of the recipients still survive today. It is a recognition that Alan was proud to receive from his adopted hometown with which he has been associated with since 1957.</p>
