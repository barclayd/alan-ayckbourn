<p>We’re midway through the 70th anniversary of the founding of the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough – where Alan Ayckbourn made his playwriting and directorial debuts.</p>

<p>And for anyone wanting to look at the history of the theatre that Alan Ayckbourn and his pioneering mentor Stephen Joseph are synonymous with, there’s exciting news.</p>

<p><strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">A Round Town</a></strong> – the sister site to <strong><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a></strong> – has doubled in size with even more insight into Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (1955 – 1976) and the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round (1976 – 1996).</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>The website – created and curated by theatre historian and Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist <strong><a href="http://www.simon-murgatroyd.com">Simon Murgatroyd</a></strong> – takes a deep-dive into the first 40 years of this nationally significant company which launched not only Alan Ayckbourn’s career but also a host of writers, actors, directors and other creatives.</p>

<p>For 2026, the website has added <strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-2/page66.html">individual pages</a></strong> for every professional production between 1955 and 1996 – that’s more than 400 pages with details of the performances, the company and essential notes about each production.</p>

<p>A guide to the most notable <strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-14/page66.html">unproduced plays</a></strong> – including a lost Ayckbourn, a play by the philosopher Colin Wilson and work by Peter Tinniswood and Stephen Mallatratt – is also available.</p>

<p>Further details about the two venues – including ground-plans and details of schedules and prices over the decades – have also been added.</p>

<p>“The first 40 years of theatre in the round in Scarborough have always fascinated me,” said Simon Murgatroyd. “From how Stephen Joseph formed a pioneering and ground-breaking new writing company to how it became the launchpad and synonymous with Alan Ayckbourn. I hope with A Round Town, I can offer an insight into why I believe these four decades and two venues are so significant both to Scarborough cultural history and heritage but also to national theatre history.”</p>

<p>The site is also essential to anyone studying Alan Ayckbourn as it offers a broader context for his career and the theatre he is most associated with. Meanwhile, this own website <strong><a href="/">www.alanayckbourn.net</a></strong> – also curated by Simon Murgatroyd – continues to expand and develop each year.</p>

<p>If you’re interested in <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong>, there’s also good news in that  Simon’s photographic exhibition of Theatre in the Round at there Library Theatre in July 2025 is coming back to the library during Spring 2026 with more photos including some recently discovered never before seen images this spring for an extended period of time. Watch this space for more details.</p>

<p>There will be also  talks by Simon to accompany the exhibition – who will also be working with the library for Open Heritage Week. He will also be giving his delayed talk from 2024 – <em>The Woman in Black: From Page to Stage</em> – during October, celebrating the creation of one of the most famous non-Ayckbourn plays to originate in Scarborough. More details soon…</p>

<p>Until then, delve into A Round Town at <strong><a href="http://www.a-round-town.com">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong> and learn about the extraordinary history of the two most significant venues in the history of theatre in the round in Scarborough.</p>
