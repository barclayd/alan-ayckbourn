<p><em>Essential Images is a regular feature on the Official Alan Ayckbourn Blog in which the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, chooses a significant image from the Ayckbourn Archive relating to the plays.</em></p>

<p><i>This week, the image relates to his twelfth play,&nbsp;</i><u><em><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular">Absurd Person Singular</a></em></u><i>, which opened at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, on 26 June 1972 and would subsequently open in the West End at The Criterion Theatre on 14 July 1973.</i></p>

<p><strong>Essential Images: Absurd Person Singular</strong>&nbsp;by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>Where to begin with <em>Absurd Person Singular</em>? Arguably one of the most significant and popular of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays, it has been produced almost continually since its premiere in 1972 at the Library Theatre in Scarborough.</p>

<p>It celebrates its 50th anniversary this year and there is probably more material in archive replanting to this play than any other Ayckbourn work – as befits a work which holds the records for the longest run and most revivals of any Ayckbourn play in the West End and on Broadway.</p>

<p>I’m confining my image choices to the world premiere production though to give a sense of the history of the play as it celebrates its 50th anniversary.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Act II image from Absurd Person Singular (© Haydonning Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Up to this point, I would say there are very few iconic images from the world premiere productions of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays. There are interesting ones, but few which actually capture a moment so well from a play. With <em>Absurd Person Singular</em>, there are several notable images in archive including Sidney and Jane’s face-off at the end of Act I, Jane pouring water over Sidney in Act II and this image also from Act II where Ronald electrocutes himself whilst trying to fix the kitchen light.</p>

<p>Christopher Godwin is featured on the table as Ronald – a role he reprised this year for Alan Ayckbourn’s 50th anniversary rehearsed reading of the play – surrounded by (left to right) Philippa Urquhart as Jane, Piers Rogers as Sidney and Matyelok Gibbs as Marion.</p>

<p>The photographer, Ken Boden – also the theatre manager at the time – perfectly captures the ‘shocking’ moment and one of the comic highpoints of the second act. It also clearly offers a look at the simplicity of the set for this in-the-round production.</p>

<p>Due to the budget – or lack thereof – the same props were used in each act, slightly redressed but just moved to different positions. It’s an interesting glimpse into how the play was originally staged and the limitations Alan faced in what was essentially a temporary theatre with minimal resources.</p>

<p>The second image is one that has always fascinated me as it is the first promotional image to be created for an Ayckbourn world premiere.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2022/1972-absurd-person-singular-flyer-colour-c2a9-simon-murgatroyd-w400.jpeg" alt="" width="400" height="512" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Absurd Person Singular flyer (© Haydonning Ltd and held in private collection)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>This is a flyer for the play’s premiere at the Library Theatre in 1972 and the image – a lino-cut of some of the key prop elements from the play – marks the first time the Library Theatre commissioned a promotional image; prior to this programmes and posters used either a generic theatre-in-the-round logo or just listed the key details of the production.</p>

<p>So this image, used only on flyers and not programmes nor posters, is of historical significance both for Alan and the Library Theatre – there are no examples of productions from the company’s history with a promotional image before 1972 despite the fact the theatre was in its 18th year of performances.</p>

<p>And that’s it for this week’s Essential Images – although there’s some big new regarding <em>Absurd Person Singular</em> coming soon. Watch this space.</p>
