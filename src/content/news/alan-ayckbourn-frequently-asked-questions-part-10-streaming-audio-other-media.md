<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of these questions. The blog has been reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs, hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>In the final part of the series, we’re looking at questions regarding streaming, audio and other media adaptations of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays.</em></p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Streaming, Audio and Other Media</strong></p>

<p><strong>1) Which of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays have been adapted as audio plays?<br></strong>There’s actually a quite wide range of plays which have been adapted for radio, audio books and audio streaming over the years. These are the adaptations which have been broadcast in the UK: <em><a href="/plays/absent-friends/absent-friends-radio">Absent Friends</a></em> (BBC, 1997); <em><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular/absurd-person-singular-radio">Absurd Person Singular</a></em> (BBC, 1997); <em><a href="/plays/anno-domino/productions">Anno Domino</a></em> (Haydonning, 2020); <em><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce/bedroom-farce-adaptations-in-other-media">Bedroom Farce</a></em> (BBC, 2022); <em><a href="/plays/jeeves/by-jeeves-original-cast-recording">By Jeeves</a></em> (BBC, 1996); <em><a href="/plays/confusions/confusions-adaptations-in-other-media">Confusions</a></em> (BBC, 1985, 1987, 1988, 2012);; <em><a href="/plays/haunting-julia/haunting-julia-audio-play">Haunting Julia</a></em> (Haydonning, 2020); <em><a href="/plays/henceforward/henceforward-audio-drama">Henceforward…</a></em> (LA Theatre Works, 2011; BBC, 2012); <em><a href="/plays/intimate-exchanges/intimate-exchanges-film">Intimate Exchanges</a></em> (BBC, 1987); <em><a href="/plays/joking-apart/joking-apart-radio-1981">Joking Apart</a></em> (BBC, 1981, 1990); <em><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves/just-between-ourselves-audio-drama">Just Between Ourselves</a></em> (BBC, 1984, 2008; LA Theatre Works, 2000); <em><a href="/plays/man-of-the-moment/man-of-the-moment-radio-1992">Man Of The Moment</a></em> (BBC 1992, 2009; LA Theatre Works, 1994); <em><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests/the-norman-conquests-audio-drama">The Norman Conquests</a></em> (BBC, 1990, 2015; LA Theatre Works, 1999); <em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking/relatively-speaking-radio">Relatively Speaking</a></em> (BBC, 1975); <em><a href="/plays/the-revengers-comedies/the-revengers-comedies-radio">The Revengers’ Comedies</a></em> (BBC, 1996); <em><a href="/plays/seasons-greetings/seasons-greetings-radio-1985">Season’s Greetings</a></em> (BBC, 1985, 1999); <em><a href="/plays/a-small-family-business/a-small-family-business-radio">A Small Family Business</a></em> (BBC, 2009); <em><a href="/plays/things-we-do-for-love/things-we-do-for-love-radio">Things We Do For Love</a></em> (BBC, 1998); <em><a href="/plays/way-upstream/way-upstream-radio">Way Upstream</a></em> (BBC, 1997); <em><a href="/plays/whenever/whenever-radio">Whenever</a></em> (BBC, 2006); <em><a href="/plays/woman-in-mind">Woman In Mind</a></em> (BBC, 2000, 2014).</p>

<p><strong>2) Which of audio adaptations are currently available commercially?<br></strong>Not many! Of the BBC adaptations, only <em>Season’s Greetings</em> (1999) and <em>The Norman Conquests</em> (1990) are available and now only to stream or own digitally. Of the other adaptations, only the LA Theatre Works adaptations are currently available – again to stream or own in a digital format – with <em>Henceforward…</em>, <em>Just Between Ourselve</em>s, <em>The Norman Conquests</em> and <em>Man of the Moment</em>.</p>

<p><strong>3) Why aren’t the other BBC adaptations available commercially or available on BBC Sounds?<br></strong>You’d have to ask the BBC or its commercial arm. Alan Ayckbourn has no involvement in the decision making regarding the audio – or filmed – adaptations of his work. With only a couple of exceptions, he would welcome the release of the audio adaptations as many of them are notably good adaptations of his plays and generally far superior to the filmed ones. But it seems unlikely the plays are likely to be made more widely available in the foreseeable future.</p>

<p><strong>4) What is special about <em>Anno Domino</em> (2020 and <em>Haunting Julia</em> (2020)?<br></strong>These were audio productions created and streamed during the pandemic lockdowns of 2020. <em>Anno Domino</em> was a world premiere, having never been performed before. Alan Ayckbourn and his wife Heather Stoney recorded the play at home – playing all eight characters between them – with Alan then editing the play. It was streamed over the course of 2020 and 2021 over various hosts. Later in 2020, Alan Ayckbourn recorded <em>Haunting Julia</em> playing all three roles which again was streamed. Neither production is currently available, but may be streamed again in the future.</p>

<p><strong>5) Have any productions of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays been recorded and live-streamed?<br></strong>Yes, but not many. The first recording of a live Ayckbourn production was the National Theatre’s 2010 revival of <em>Season’s Greetings</em>, directed by Marianne Elliott. This was live-streamed around the world as part of the NT Live programme. Subsequently, the Stephen Joseph Theatre recorded the world premiere productions of <em><a href="/plays/the-girl-next-door/the-girl-next-door-stream">The Girl Next Door</a></em> (2021) and <em><a href="/plays/family-album/family-album-stream">Family Album</a></em> (2022). These were live-streamed during their original runs.</p>

<p>None of these plays have been subsequently streamed and the playwright is not aware of any plans to do so – although, theoretically, <em>Season’s Greetings</em> might be at some point be part of the NT At Home service. He is aware of no plans to make <em>The Girl Next Door</em> and <em>Family Album</em> available to stream in the future.</p>

<p>The playwright is also not aware of any plans by the Stephen Joseph Theatre to record or stream any future productions of his work; his most recent world premiere <em><a href="/plays/constant-companions">Constant Companions</a></em> (2023) was not recorded.</p>

<p><strong>6) Why haven’t more of Alan Ayckbourns’ world premieres been recorded?<br></strong>For most of his career – or at least since it’s been feasible to record live productions – Sir Alan has resisted the recording of his work. He believes theatre is a live experience and that recordings, even live streams, do not capture the true experience of being in a theatre experiencing a live theatre. Theatre has traditionally been a transient art, lost except to memory once the final performance  has been given, leaving only the script for new interpretations to take place. Alan has traditionally resisted recording his work as he believes it will never capture the live experience, which is what he writes for.</p>

<p>He has also been of the opinion that until recently it has been very difficult to capture performances in theatre in the round to an acceptable level – and given the majority of his work is written for and premiered in-the-round, this is a considerable issue. In recent years, technology has made it possible to film in-the-round successfully and he was pleased with the filmed productions of <em>The Girl Next Door</em> and <em>Family Album</em>. </p>

<p>But, generally speaking, Alan Ayckbourn believes his plays are best seen live on stage and whilst he is no longer so dead set upon his productions not being recorded, it’s not something he’s pushing for.</p>

<p><strong>7) Is anything else related to Alan Ayckbourn available in other media?<br></strong>Commercially, there is <em>Alan Ayckbourn: In His Own Words</em> which includes interviews with Alan Ayckbourn from some significant BBC broadcasts and programmes. There is also a 1964 audio recording of Shakespeare’s <em><a href="/career/actor/much-ado-about-nothing">A Midsummer Night’s Dream</a></em> starring Richard Johnson and Pauline Jameson on which Alan Ayckbourn played a number of small roles.</p>

<p>Incidental music from Alan Ayckbourn plays was also released by Paul Todd with <em>The P.T. Tape I and II</em>, released on audio cassette in the 1980s and by John Pattison on CD with <em>Ayckbourn Music</em> (2000) and <em>Ayckbourn Kids’ Music</em> (2003). All of these are rare and none are currently available to buy or stream.</p>

<p>There is also the soundtrack to Alain Resnais’s film adaptation of Alan Ayckbourn’s <em><a href="/plays/private-fears-in-public-places/coeurs">Private Fears in Public Places</a></em> (2006), composed by Mark Snow. This was originally released on CD and is also available to buy as a digital album. </p>

<p><strong>8) Are there any original cast recordings of Alan Ayckbourn’s musicals?<br></strong>Commercially, just <em>By Jeeves</em> (1996) with the original London cast., which is available as download. Notably, CDs were released of the original Scarborough company, the London cast and the New York premiere company. The original <a href="/plays/jeeves/by-jeeves-original-cast-recording"><em>Jeeves</em></a> (1975) was also released on vinyl in 1976 and is considered one of the rarest Ayckbourn collectibles.</p>

<p>Over time, there have also been original cast recordings of <em><a href="/plays/awaking-beauty/media">Awaking Beauty</a></em> (2008), <em><a href="/plays/dreams-from-a-summer-house/dreams-from-a-summer-house-original-cast-recording">Dreams From A Summer House</a></em> (1992), <em><a href="/plays/making-tracks/making-tracks-original-cast-recording">Making Tracks</a></em> (1981),  <em><a href="/plays/men-on-women-on-men/men-on-women-on-men-original-cast-recording">Men on Women on Men</a></em> (1978), <em><a href="http://sevendeadlyvirtues.alanayckbourn.net/page9/Media_Seven_Deadly.html">The 7 Deadly Virtues</a></em> (1984) and <em><a href="/plays/whenever">Whenever</a></em> (2000). These were recorded in Scarborough and made available on audio cassette (productions prior to 1994 or CD (productions subsequent to 2000) at the Stephen Joseph Theatre. They have never been re-recorded nor made commercially available subsequently.</p>
