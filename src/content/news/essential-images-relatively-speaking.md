<p><em>Essential Images is a regular feature on the Official Alan Ayckbourn Blog in which the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, chooses a significant image from the Ayckbourn Archive relating to the plays.</em></p>

<p><i>This week, the image relates to his seventh play, </i><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking"><span><i><u>Relatively</u></i></span></a><em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking"> Speaking</a></em><i>, which opened at the Library Theatre, Scarborough, on 8 July 1965 and opened in the West End at the Duke of York’s Theatre on 31 March 1967.</i></p>

<p><strong>Essential Images: Relatively Speaking</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>Given <em>Relatively Speaking</em> opened in the West End on 31 March back in 1967, it seems an appropriate week to look at this seminal Ayckbourn play in Essential Images.</p>

<p>With more than 50 years worth of productions of <em>Relatively Speaking</em>, there’s an ample choice of productions to choose from. However, we’re going to be slightly perverse this week by looking at the television production of the play from 1969.</p>

<p>This was broadcast by the BBC and marked the first time time a full-length Ayckbourn play has been adapted for television. It featured Celia Johnson reprising her role of Sheila from the West End, pictured here with John Stride as Greg.</p>

<p>The play was broadcast on BBC1 on 2 March 1969 but, sadly, this production doesn’t appear to have to survived in any television archive so we actually only have photographs from it. The production starred Celia Johnson alongside Donald Sinden – who had directed the post West End UK tour of the play, as well as John Stride and Judy Cornwell.</p>

<p>The play was directed by Herbert Wise, who would go onto direct <em><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests">The Norman Conquests</a></em> for television in 1977 as well as Alan Ayckbourn’s only screenplay, <em><a href="/plays/service-not-included">Service Not Included</a></em>, in 1974.</p>

<p>Alan Ayckbourn recalls that Wise had a slight issue when filming <em>Relatively Speaking</em>. Despite being gifted with an excellent cast, he had the issue that Donald Sinden was 15 years younger than Celia Johnson.</p>

<p>Given in the play, Sheila and Philip are married and of the approximate same age, Wise didn’t want to emphasise the age difference. According to Alan, he apparently solved this by filming most of the shots with Sinden and Johnson in long shot rather than close-up!</p>

<p>Given we can’t watch it now, we don’t really know what the quality of the adaptation was, although Alan recalls his agent – Margaret Ramsay – was less than impressed by it!</p>

<p><em>Relatively Speaking</em> was again adapted by the BBC for television in 1989 and, perversely, whilst this has survived in archive, there are no production images held in archive. All the Ayckbourn Archive holds is a rather unusual Christmas Card / promotional flyer which features sketches of the characters as they appear in the credits for the production.</p>
