<p><em>As The Norman Conquests celebrates its 50th anniversary this month, it’s brought back the age old question of, what is the right order to see The Norman Conquests?</em></p>

<p><em>Traditionally theatres have scheduled the trilogy following the original pattern as established from the world premiere in Scarborough in 1973, but that order was only imposed on the playwright due to the actor playing Norman not being available for the first week of rehearsals.</em></p>

<p><em>But the question has long persisted, is there a correct order or even preferred order to seeing The Norman Conquests? Fortunately, the playwright answered this very question back in 1974 and here he clears up any doubts whatsoever as to the right order to see the plays…</em></p>

<p><strong>How To Watch <em>The Norman Conquests</em></strong> by Alan Ayckbourn</p>

<p>If you are in the process of reading this Programme, the chances are that you are already about to see, are in the midst of seeing, or have already seen, at least one of the plays that form <em>The Norman Conquests</em>. In which case, this advice is not for you. Do not read on.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbournworking  in 1974; possibly trying to work out the right order to see the trilogy… (© TBC)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>For those who have seen none of the plays but may be wishing to do so, it is hoped that the following notes may prove useful.</p>

<p>The first thing to remember is, understandably, don’t see <em>Table Manners</em> first. This will give you a wrong time sequence and will only confuse you when you come to see, say, <em>Living Together</em> which, incidentally, you are strongly advised not to see second.</p>

<p>Ideally, <em>Round And Round The Garden</em> should not be seen before you have seen <em>Table Manners</em> – but do not, on the other hand, fall into that old trap of seeing <em>Round And Round The Garden</em> after <em>Living Together</em> as this again will confuse the sequences of dramatic events.</p>

<p>Do not see <em>Living Together</em> first as this will severely curtail a lot of the pleasure you gain from seeing <em>Table Manners</em> for the first time which latter play, for maximum enjoyment you should try and save till the end.</p>

<p>In short, do try and see all three plays first, or, if you really can’t manage this, last. This way you will avoid any disappointment. Like most things in this world, there is a logical progression i.e. Parts 1, 3 and finally of course, 2.</p>

<p>Alan Ayckbourn, 1974</p>
