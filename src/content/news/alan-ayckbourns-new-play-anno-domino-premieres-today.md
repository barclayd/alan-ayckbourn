<p>Alan Ayckbourn’s latest play <a href="/plays/anno-domino"><em>Anno Domino</em> </a>will premiere at noon today (25 May 2020) and be available for a month for free at the Stephen Joseph Theatre&nbsp;<a href="http://www.sjt.uk.com/">website</a>.</p>

<p><em>Anno Domino</em> is the playwright’s 84th play and marks the first time one of his plays has debuted as an audio stream, the first time he has written, directed and performed in one of his own works and, also, marks his return to acting 56 years after his last appearance on a professional stage.</p>

<p>Alan Ayckbourn says: “The inspiration for&nbsp;<em>Anno Domino</em>&nbsp;came from the idea that all relationships ultimately, however resilient they appear to be, are built on sand! And it only takes one couple to break up abruptly to take us all by surprise, then all of a sudden everyone is questioning their own unshakeable relationship.” &nbsp;</p>

<div><figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2020/ad-400-w400.jpeg" alt="" width="400" height="400" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure></div>

<p>The 81-year-old Olivier and Tony Award-winning author has teamed up with his wife, actress Heather Stoney, to record the new show at their home in Scarborough.</p>

<p><em>Anno Domino</em>&nbsp;looks at the break-up of a long-established marriage and the effect that has on family and friends.</p>

<p>In it, Ayckbourn and Stoney will play four characters each, with an age range of 18 to mid-70s. It’s the first time they’ve acted together since Ayckbourn’s last appearance on a professional stage, in William Gibson’s two-hander&nbsp;<em><a href="/career/actor/two-for-the-seesaw-2">Two for the Seesaw</a></em>&nbsp;at the Rotherham Civic Theatre in 1964.</p>

<div><figure><figcaption>Alan Ayckbourn &amp; Heather Stoney<br>(© Tony Bartholomew / <a href="https://www.bartpics.co.uk/">www.bartpics.co.uk</a>)</figcaption></figure></div>

<p>After that, Ayckbourn pursued a hugely successful writing and directing career, while Stoney continue to act, appearing in many Ayckbourn world premieres. Her last full season as an actress was at the SJT in 1985, during which she appeared in the world premiere of Ayckbourn’s&nbsp;<a href="http:://womaninmind.alanayckbourn.net">Woman in Mind</a>.</p>

<p><em>Anno Domino</em> is written and directed by Alan Ayckbourn, performed by Alan Ayckbourn and Heather Stoney, and produced by Alan Ayckbourn with final mix by Paul Stear.</p>

<p>You can listen to Anno Domino, for free, on the SJT website (<a href="http://www.sjt.uk.com/">www.sjt.uk.com</a>) from noon on Monday 25 May until noon on Thursday 25 June.</p>
