<p>It’s been a while since the Blog ran an Ask The Archivist article, but – bizarrely – I’ve recently received several enquiries along the same lines so it seemed apposite to answer  it here.</p>

<p>The question – which has also come up previously – is did Alan Ayckbourn specifically wrote the role of the android Jacie Triplethree in <strong><em><a href="/plays/comic-potential">Comic Potential</a></em></strong> for the actress Janie Dee?</p>

<p>For those familiar with the play, the role of Jacie is very challenging for actors as it requires playing multiple personalities, physical comedy, dancing and, most challenging of all, convincingly and truthfully playing an android’s journey to sentience and self-awareness.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Janie Dee in the world premiere of Comic Potential (©   <a href="http://www.bartpics.co.uk">Tony Bartholomew</a>)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Janie Dee starred in the world, West End and New York premieres of the play and was lauded for her performance – she is one of the few actors to have won the Olivier, Evening Standard and Critics’ Circle Awards for the same performance. Given her many talents, it’s a fair question to ask, did Alan write the role specifically for her?</p>

<p>The best answer to this in archive is actually a contemporary response to the Head of Comedy at the BBC on 21 November 1999, when Janie was performing the role in the West End. This is Alan’s response.</p>

<p>“Oddly enough, although I really can’t imagine anyone else playing it (though hopefully someone else will do somewhere, sometime!) it was not strictly written for Janie. I try never to have specific actors in mind when I write. In the end, I find it limits me rather whilst I’m writing and it also tends too mean that I write for what I know the actor can do, has done in the past, rather than what might surprise us in the future.”</p>

<p>Which is a pretty definitive no, he didn’t write the role specifically for Janie. In subsequent years he did elaborate on this and his biographer, Paul Allen, notes that Alan wasn’t aware of the full range of Janie’s talents until rehearsals began for <em>Comic Potential</em> – she met all the challenges and then some!</p>

<p>He’s also noted how it’s not wise for a playwright to generally write something with a specific actor in mind, in case something happens to that actor and they are then unavailable for the production, for whatever reason. As Alan was writing his plays about a year in advance of production at that point, there was no guarantee an actor as in demand as Janie would have been available for a summer rep season in a regional UK theatre the following year.</p>
