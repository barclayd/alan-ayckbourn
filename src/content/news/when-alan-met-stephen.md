<p><em>Last week, the blog’s history of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> hit 1957 when Alan Ayckbourn made the life-changing decision to join the Scarborough company.</em></p>

<p><em>In doing so, he came into contact with the single most influential figure in his entire life, <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong>. Thanks to Stephen, Alan would start down his defining paths of playwriting and directing and who would have an enormous influence on his life and professional career.</em></p>

<p><em>Alan would also go on, after Stephen’s untimely death in 1967, to become Artistic Director of the company Stephen founded, ensuring his legacy survived and thrived,.</em></p>

<p><em>To complement last week’s article, here we have Alan Ayckbourn talking about the memorable moment when he first met Stephen Joseph.</em></p>

<p><strong>When Alan Met Stephen</strong></p>

<p>In 1957, Alan Ayckbourn joined Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre as an acting stage manager.</p>

<p>He had been employed by a friend and the company’s new manager, Rodney Wood, and came to Scarborough having never met its founder and Artistic Director, Stephen Joseph.</p>

<p>Indeed, Stephen was something of a rarity during that summer season in Scarborough and Alan had no idea when or if he would meet this man who would, unbeknownst to him, become so pivotal to his life.</p>

<p>In 2017, in a talk to mark the 50th anniversary of Stephen’s death, Alan recalled his memories of meeting Stephen Joseph for the first time.</p>

<p>&nbsp;“I worked with Stephen for several weeks without knowing who the hell he was!</p>

<p>I was employed not by him, but by Rodney Wood – who became the general manager of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre – when he was my stage director at Leatherhead Rep.</p>

<p>I was just an ASM and when we had all finished the current season and he said the immortal words, ‘Anyone fancy a job in Scarborough?’ And I chimed up and said, ‘Where the Hell’s Scarborough?’ And he said, ‘Well just go up that way until York and turn right.’ And I said, ‘Oh, that sounds good, OK, I’m off.’</p>

<p>So without further ado, I was on a train to Scarborough. I changed at York and then it became really pretty with this beautiful scenery and I arrived in Scarborough. I met the director there, Clive Goodwin, who was directing a season of plays and there was no sign of this man, Stephen Joseph. So I proceeded to stage manage the opening production of&nbsp;<em>The Glass Menagerie</em>&nbsp;and then appeared in the second production of&nbsp;<em>An Inspector Calls</em>.</p>

<p>In between these roles, I was operating the lights as well and I would stand in this cramped little space in the narrow corridor between the dressing room and the stage, operating a lethal machine – a Strand Eight Way Slider Dimmer – which you got belting shocks off.</p>

<p>I was operating the lights sight unseen, so I couldn’t see the stage; I had no view of the stage and the curtains were drawn on both entrances to avoid light leaks. I was operating the lights and I got to the end of the first scene and I did a down-fade with eight fingers and I then brought them up again at the beginning of the second scene with the same eight fingers and I was suddenly aware of an enormous man standing at the side of me.</p>

<p>And he said, quite loudly, ‘There’s a better way to do that.’ And I said, ‘Excuse me sir, this is a restricted area. Professionals at work.’ And he said, ‘No, no, there’s a better way to do that.’ I said, ‘Yeah, I’m sure there is, thanks very much.’ </p>

<p>And he said, ‘If you get a bit of wood’, and he found a bit of wood on this untidy floor, ‘you can lay it across the dimmers. If you use it there it’s a much easier way to do a blackout.’ He brought it down and I said, ‘You’ve just blacked out the stage…’ and he said, ‘Oh Jesus’ and then he ran through the door.</p>

<p>At which point the actors came out very angry – ‘That was in the middle of my big speech’ – and I said – I was like Stan Laurel – ‘There was this great big man…’ – and one of the actors said, ‘Oh, that’s got to be Stephen.’ I said, ‘Stephen Who?’ They said, ‘Stephen Joseph, the guy who runs the company.’ </p>

<p>I said, ‘Oh, that’s Stephen. He’s crazy!’ </p>

<p>And that was our first meeting.”</p>
