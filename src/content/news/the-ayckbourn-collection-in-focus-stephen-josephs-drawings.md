<p><em>In August 2023, The Ayckbourn Collection was donated to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>It is a collection of approximately 60 significant items drawn from Sir Alan’s Archive and Simon’s personal collection exploring the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>Currently, items from the exhibition are on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. To mark the exhibition, Simon Murgatroyd – who curated the Collection and facilitated its donation – is taking a weekly look at some of the individual items currently on display, their significance and why they were chosen for the Ayckbourn Collection.</em></p>

<p><strong>The Theatre Stephen Joseph’s Drawings</strong></p>

<p>With the Ayckbourn Collection exhibition at Scarborough Art Gallery now re-open after a temporary closure, it’s time to return to turning the spotlight on some of the pieces.</p>

<p>This week, focus turns to Alan Ayckbourn’s mentor and the founder of theatre in the round in Scarborough, the theatre pioneer Stephen Joseph. He was a man of many talents including as an artist.</p>

<p>He illustrated all his books and also was a prolific producer of architectural plans for theatres – both realised and unrealised. An example of his work has previously been spotlighted with one of his designs for Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre (click <a href="https://archivingayckbourn.home.blog/2023/10/25/the-ayckbourn-collection-in-focus-theatre-in-the-round/">here</a>).</p>

<p>The Ayckbourn Collection holds two of his pieces of Stephen’s artwork – both currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery. One a colour sketch (pictured below) and the other (not pictured) is a mono, ink drawing probably intended for one of his books.</p>

<p>The striking colour sketch is of a theatre in the round with a gallery and a circular acting space – unlike the Scarborough space which has always been rectangular. It’s a dynamic and vibrant piece – pen on paper. What is interesting is it bears more than a passing resemblance to the New Vic, Newcastle-under-Lyme – also a round space with a gallery.</p>

<p>This is of interest as Stephen founded the Victoria Theatre in Stoke-on-Trent in 1962 – the UK’s first year-round theatre-in-the-round venue. It was based on Stephen’s design for Scarborough (where he had founded the UK’s first theatre in the round company in 1955) and was rectangular.</p>

<p>In 1986, 19 years after Stephen had died, the Victoria became the New Vic when it moved to a new, purpose-built home in Newcastle-under-Lyme. It has quite a different design to its previous form with a circular acting space, but still absolutely paid respect to Stephen Joseph’s ideas and designs for theatre in the round; which can be seen in his drawing which quite clearly illustrates the type of acting space the New Vic utilises.</p>

<p>The second image (not pictured – you’ll have to visit the gallery to see it!) is a mono picture – pen on tracing paper – illustrating a period performance piece in-the-round. What is interesting is Stephen shows the way the production might be lit with the lighting arcs visible from the lamps. It was undoubtedly intended for publication as Stephen illustrated his books for both practical and aesthetic effect</p>

<p>For illustrations such as this, Stephen tended to draw from photographs and this can be demonstrated with a similar type of illustration to the one in the Ayckbourn Collection, reproduced here from his book Theatre In The Round. The illustration is based on a publicity shot for the world premiere of Alan Ayckbourn’s play <em>Relatively Speaking</em> (or <em>Meet My Father</em> as it was known then). </p>

<p>Illustrations such as this and the one in the Ayckbourn Collection on display at the Scarborough Art Gallery are scattered throughout Stephen’s books such as <em>Theatre in the Round</em> and <em>The Playhouse in England</em>. They illustrate try breadth of skills Stephen brought to his work alongside his writing, directing and prolific knowledge of the history of theatre and its forms.</p>
