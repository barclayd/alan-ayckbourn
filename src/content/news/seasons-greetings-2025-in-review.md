<p>It’s that time of the year when the blog takes a break for Christmas and the New Year and to thank everyone who has supported the website and the blog over the past twelve months.</p>

<p>It has been a phenomenal year for <strong><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a></strong>. We have broken the nine year old record for page views, soaring for the first time past the two million pages mark. If you’ve visited the website in any capacity, thank you!</p>

<p>Numbers like this mean the website and the blog can keep going and more than justify their existence. So that’s always good news!</p>

<p>It’s been a great year for Ayckbourn fans too. The rather unexpected but no less welcome major West End revival of <strong><em><a href="/plays/woman-in-mind">Woman in Mind</a></em></strong> in the West End is now up and running. It is great to see after almost a decade away from London, an Ayckbourn play back in the West End. It’s running until February if you haven’t yet booked tickets!</p>

<p>Meanwhile, we had the world premiere of Alan’s 91st play <em><strong><a href="/plays/earth-angel">Earth Angel</a></strong></em> in Scarborough, which seems to have struck a chord with its note of optimism for the present day amidst all the negativity and cynicism.</p>

<p>And <strong><em><a href="/plays/snake-in-the-grass">Snake in the Grass</a></em></strong> proved to be a hit with the Bolton Octagon / Theatr Clywd revival which continues to support the theory <em>Snake in the Grass</em> is fast becoming one of Alan’s most significant plays of the ’00s.</p>

<p>Personally, it’s been an extraordinary year. My celebration of the 70th anniversary of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in Scarborough – the company which essentially launched Alan and led him to become synonymous with the town – was more successful than I ever expected.</p>

<p>My exhibition at Scarborough Library was wonderfully supported – and if you didn’t mange to catch it, look out for some very exciting news in early 2026 – and my book to accompany it, <em>A Theatrical Revolution</em>, sold out.</p>

<p>My talk marking the 60th anniversary of <em><strong><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking">Relatively Speaking</a></strong></em> which kicked off the <em>Actors, Audience and Ayckbourn</em> weekend at the Stephen Joseph Theatre was sold out and incredibly positively received – and I suspect opened a few people’s eyes as to how there is no realistic proposition the SJT would be here today if it hadn’t been for Alan Ayckbourn, his support and contributions.</p>

<p>All my other talks were very well received and I’m already booking up for 2026 with talks celebrating Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, the 50th anniversary of the opening of the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round and – one I’m very much looking forward to – a Halloween talk about the journey of <em><strong><a href="http://thewomaninblack.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk">The Woman in Black</a></strong></em> from page to stage…</p>

<p>Further details of all this will be announced in 2026.</p>

<p>Oh, and there’s also a little thing called <strong><em><a href="/plays/the-trial-of-romeo-oscar">The Trail of Romeo Oscar</a></em></strong>! Alan Ayckbourn’s 92nd play and his first courtroom-set drama, premiering during the autumn. </p>

<p>Thanks for all your support and for reading the blog and the website. I’ll be back in early with 2026 with more news and insights into Alan Ayckbourn, his plays and theatre in the round in Scarborough.</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd, 2025</em></p>
