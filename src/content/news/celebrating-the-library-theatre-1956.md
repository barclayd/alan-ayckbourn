<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1956 and a look at the play which launched the company 70 years ago…</p>

<p><strong>1956: Wuthering Heights</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>Having navigated the pitfalls of its inaugural season, Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was successful enough to launch a second season in Scarborough during the summer of 1956.</p>

<p>This built upon the previous year with five world premiere productions from a season of six plays. Like the previous season, there was a strong showing of female writers with two of the plays written by women – Ruth Dixon and Joan Winch.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>John Rees &amp; Shirley Jacobs as Heathcliff &amp; Cathy in the world premiere of Wuthering Heights (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>This is actually very significant for both the company and its place in British theatre history. Stephen Joseph, the found and Artistic Director of the company, strongly believed everyone should be encouraged to write and he drew his playwrights from a pool of whatever work interested him.</p>

<p>In the inaugural season, three of the four plays produced were written by women which means over the first two years, half the plays were written by female writers: Eleanor D. Glaser, Ruth Dixon and Joan Winch. This is a remarkable figure – as a theatre historian, I have yet to find any other professional company during the 1950s which so actively produced new plays by female writers. The company was pioneering not just in its championing of new theatre forms and new writing, but also where it drew those writers from.</p>

<p>One of these writers was Jurneman Winch, a pseudonym for the writer Joan Winch. Joan had written&nbsp;<em>Turn Right At The Crossroads</em>&nbsp;for the first season and for 1956, she attempted the challenge of adapting&nbsp;<em><strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-19/page-3/page23/page23.html">Wuthering Heights</a></strong></em>&nbsp;for the stage.</p>

<p>Joan is the only female writer at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre to employ a male pseudonym and, to this day, it’s not entirely clear why. Sadly, the most likely reason is a prejudice against female writing which led writers to use male pseudonyms in order to be produced or published.</p>

<p>Yet Stephen Joseph happily produced work no matter what the playwright’s sex and was just – rightfully – proud of the company’s commitment to new writing. Perhaps the reason Joan chose to work under her pseudonym in Scarborough is she had been previously professionally produced under her pseudonym and this is what she had become accustomed to.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Alan Ayckbourn &amp; Hazel Burt as Heathcliff &amp; Cathy in the 1960 revival (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Although<em>&nbsp;Wuthering Heights</em>&nbsp;marked the last of Joan’s plays for Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, it was judged a great success and was a box office hit. This success can be seen when, in 1960, Stephen Joseph decided to revive two notable plays from the theatre’s first five years. He chose&nbsp;<em>Wuthering Heights</em>&nbsp;– which starred Alan Ayckbourn as Heathcliff – and Ruth Dixon’s&nbsp;<em>‘Prentice Pillar</em>. It’s perhaps notable that this celebration drew on the work of two female playwrights.</p>

<p><em>Wuthering Heights</em> ran for two weeks in rep and was directed by Stephen Joseph and starred John Rees as Heathcliff, Shirley Jacobs as Cathy, Peter Bridgmont as Edgar, Walter Hall as Earnshaw, Harry Hancock as Joseph and Betty Cardno as Nellie.</p>

<p>Of the original production of <em>Wuthering Heights</em>, The Stage particularly praised Joan’s writing – although the writer assumed Jurneman was a man – noting the play “is shot through with his striking dialogue” and “here is, one feels, a playwright of decided promise.”</p>

<p>It was also the first play to have publicity shots taken outside of the rehearsal room or Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre itself. A photo-shoot was arranged on the moors with the director, Stephen Joseph, and John Rees &amp; Shirley Jacobs.</p>

<p>A notable shot of Stephen ‘directing’ the actors on the moors was published in the Yorkshire Evening Post alongside the unlikely note: “To get the atmosphere for their presentation of Emily Bronte’s&nbsp;<em>Wuthering Heights</em>, the cast has been holding rehearsals on the moors near Scarborough.”</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>Stephen Joseph ‘directing’ Wuthering Heights on the Moors (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>It seems more likely ‘publicity stunt’ rather than ‘rehearsals’ would be closer to the truth! The other photos feature the actors on the moors and were probably used for publicity images for the foyer in Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre during the summer season.</p>

<p><em>Wuthering Heights</em>&nbsp;not only played in Scarborough but also toured to London for a single performance with the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page95/page26.html">Studio Theatre Club</a></strong>. During the first three years of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre’s existence, Stephen Joseph was still hoping to find a home for the company in London and regularly took productions which had been presented in Scarborough alongside other work to London in the hope of generating interest in his project.</p>

<p>The performances took place at the Mahatma Gandhi Assembly Rooms in Fitzroy Square and this is where Alan Ayckbourn first saw the Studio Theatre Company and experienced theatre-in-the-round for the first time.</p>

<p>In fact, Joan’s 1955 play,&nbsp;<em>Turn Right At The Crossroads</em>, was the first of the Studio Theatre Club plays when they were launched on 1 September 1955.</p>

<p><em>Wuthering Heights</em> also marked the company’s first production of an adaptation of an existing work and it was the most popular play of the 1956 season.</p>

<p>Although Joan did not return to the company after 1956, there is no doubting that she and the other female writers who had new work produced by Stephen were instrumental in the success of the formative years of the company in Scarborough.</p>
