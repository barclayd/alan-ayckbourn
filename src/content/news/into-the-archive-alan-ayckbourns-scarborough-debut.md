<p>This year the Ayckbourn Blog celebrating the 70th anniversary of the founding of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> in Scarborough.</p>

<p>It is where Alan Ayckbourn’s long association with both Scarborough and the company which will eventually become the Stephen Joseph Theatre begins.</p>

<p>As we’re specifically celebrating the Library Theatre on the blog though, it seemed a good idea to take a look at Alan’s debut season in Scarborough and Alan’s earliest experiences with the theatre company he is now synonymous with.</p>

<p>Alan joined Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in 1957, two years after <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Josep</a></strong>h had founded it as the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company. He joined it as an acting stage manager – a stage manager with occasional acting roles. His main ambition, at 18 years old, was to become an actor. He had no thought of writing or directing at this time.</p>

<p><em><strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-2/page-106/page-135/">The Glass Menagerie</a></strong></em> by Tennessee Williams was the first productions Alan was involved with that season and this is the programme with him credited as Stage Manager.</p>

<p>Alan had small roles in the next two productions, <strong><em><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-19/page-3/page28/page28.html">An Inspector Calls</a></em></strong> by J.B. Priestley and <em><strong><a href="/career/actor/the-ornamental-hermit">The Ornamental Hermit</a></strong></em> by Catherine Prynne. In both cases, he was also stage managing the production, so he would be constantly reverting from one role to the other throughout the performances.</p>

<p>Pictured here is Alan as Eric Birling in <em>An Inspector Calls</em> – he’s pictured on the far right of the image.</p>

<p>Alan also appeared in <em>The Ornamental Hermit</em> in which he played the poet, Jack Bensted, pictured here again on the far right.</p>

<p>Although Alan only had small speaking roles in these productions, they were a chance to get noticed and – hopefully – to lead onto larger and better roles. They were also an opportunity to be noticed by reviewers.</p>

<p>For <em>The Ornamental Hermit</em>, Alan’s role was noticed by The Times – when the production had a single performance at Stephen Joseph’s Studio Theatre Club in London – and marks the first time he was singled out for attention with the company.</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/1957-09-16-times-ornamental-hermit-review-1-w600.jpg" alt="" width="600" height="245" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>The Times review of The Ornamental Hermit from 16 September 1957.</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Alan was back to solely stage-managing for the fourth and final production of the season, <strong><em><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-2/page-106/page-135/">Honey In The Stone</a></em></strong> by Ruth Dixon. At the end of the summer, although he had been asked by Stephen Joseph to return for the winter season, Alan – to his mind – had a better offer.</p>

<p>He joined the <strong><a href="/career/actor/connaught-theatre">Oxford Playhouse</a></strong> for the winter season having been promised more acting roles and he stayed with the company from October 1957 – April 1958, appearing in six plays. At which point, having been invited back to Scarborough also with the promise of more roles, Alan returned to Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre for the summer 1958 season.</p>

<p>He then stayed solely with the Scarborough company until 1962, when Stephen Joseph founded the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong> in Stoke-on-Trent. At which point – along with most of the Scarborough company – he upped sticks to The Potteries and only returned to Scarborough as an occasional playwright until 1969 when he became Director of Productions for the summer season.</p>

<p>By which point, all dreams of being an actor had gone. He was the writer and director for which he would gain international renown.</p>
