<p><em>This is the last of The Ayckbourn Collection In Focus features, which began in 2023 to coincide with the donation of The Ayckbourn Collection to Scarborough Museums and Galleries by Sir Alan and Lady Ayckbourn and the playwright’s Archivist, Simon Murgatroyd.</em></p>

<p><em>Items from The Ayckbourn Collection are currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery celebrating the contribution of Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round to Scarborough’s cultural heritage and history.</em></p>

<p><em>But this isn’t the end of the archive articles. From late February, I’ll be taking a regular peek into a variety of archives related to Alan Ayckbourn, Stephen Joseph and theatre in the round, highlighting some of the fascinating objects that can be found in archives held by the likes of the Borthwick Institute for Archives, Scarborough Library and Scarborough Museums and Galleries.</em></p>

<p><strong>The<em> Love After All</em> poster</strong></p>

<p>We come to the end of this series by returning to the very beginning of Alan Ayckbourn’s playwriting career and a poster which numbers amongst the genuine Ayckbourn rarities.</p>

<p>From an archivist’s perspective, there are four posters relating to Alan Ayckbourn which are considered the rarest amongst Alan’s play memorabilia.</p>

<p>These are the early production posters which have otherwise long since been lost or destroyed – but we are very fortunate in that the original posters for Alan Ayckbourn’s first two plays have survived (in context, no posters have survived for the three plays which followed).</p>

<p>In fact, there are two surviving posters for each of his first two plays, <em><a href="/plays/the-square-cat"><strong>The Square Cat</strong></a></em> and <em><strong><a href="/plays/love-after-all">Love After All</a></strong></em>. Of all these, the one in the best condition is for <em>Love After All</em> which is held in the Ayckbourn Collection at Scarborough Museums and Galleries and is currently on display at Scarborough Art Gallery.</p>

<p>Now when we talk about production posters, we probably immediately think of modern posters with eye-catching imagery and fonts dedicated to the one show. In 1959 in Scarborough, there wasn’t the budget for this. Individual shows generally did not have their own posters and there were no images other than the company’s eye-catching logo..</p>

<p>Most of the posters for this period of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre were seasonal posters listing  details of the plays and playwrights, dates and details about the venue. Not terribly exciting in hindsight, but there was no publicity officer and next to no publicity budget at the time.</p>

<p>The <em>Love After All</em> poster lists all the plays during the winter 1959 season at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre. The most notable aspect is, of course, the inclusion of Alan Ayckbourn’s second play, <em>Love After All</em>, credited to his writing pseudonym Roland Allen, which he used for his first four plays.</p>

<p><em>Love After All</em> is a bit of an enigma. Long since withdrawn and never produced since 1960, only one extant manuscript of the play survives today, held by the British Library. It’s also intriguing in that so successful was its original run during Christmas 1959, that it was revived the following summer. The director for this revival, Julian Herington, disliked the play and asked Alan to extensively alter it – not for the better in the playwright’s opinion. Updated from a period to a contemporary play with apparently many unnecessary changes, no copy of the revised manuscript has survived.</p>

<p>Going back to the poster, the other productions are notable as they include the first play by the then esteemed author Colin Wilson, who had shot to fame with his psychological work <em>The Outsider</em>. Its also worth noting the production of <em>In Camera</em> as the company had previously produced the play in 1957 in London; this was both the first production Alan Ayckbourn ever saw in the round and performed by the company he was to become so closely associated with.</p>

<p>As for the other <em>Love After All</em> poster, this is held by the playwright himself and is on display in his rehearsal room adjoining his home in the old town of Scarborough. Affected by the sun and all but inaccessible over the decades, it is now so faded as to be barely legible making the poster held by Scarborough Museums and Galleries even more valuable from a historical perspective.</p>

<p>It’s worth noting that <em>Love After All</em> is a play which is particularly close to my heart as it was believed lost until 2007. Then, working with the British Library, I found the only surviving copy held in the Lord Chamberlain’s Collection at the British Library, restoring the full Ayckbourn canon for the first time since the 1960s.</p>

<p>With regard to <em>The Square Cat</em>, Alan’s first play, two posters survive in archive. Both damaged, but still important and fascinating to see. One is on display in The Bob Watson Archive at the Stephen Joseph Theatre and the other (pictured with visible water damage) is held in the Ayckbourn Archive at the Borthwick Institute for Archives at the University of York.</p>
