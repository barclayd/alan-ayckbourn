<p>A photographic celebration of the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough returns this spring to the venue where it all began.</p>

<p>The Library Theatre at 70 exhibition returns for two months to Scarborough Library where, in 1955, Stephen Joseph founded Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre on the first floor of the venue.</p>

<p>Originally organised for a three-day run in July 1955 by theatre historian and Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist, Simon Murgatroyd, the exhibition returns with even more and new photos for a two month run at the library.</p>

<p>“I’m absolutely delighted,” said Simon. “The response to the exhibition last year was beyond anything I imagined. To be given the opportunity to expand the exhibition and bring it back for longer as part of the Scarborough 400 celebrations and Local and Community History Month is a huge privilege.”</p>

<p>The exhibition consists of more than 90 photographs, half of which are new for 2026 and many of these are taken from recently discovered negatives which have not been seen by the public before.</p>

<p>Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre ran from 1955 to 1976 before the company moved to its second home, the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round, beneath Valley Bridge. The exhibition celebrates these first 21 years and includes a production photo from each year, behind-the-scenes images of the company both in and out of the theatre, company photographs, images from the final night in 1976, significant people and – new for 2026 – a section dedicated to Alan Ayckbourn with a photograph from all of the plays he premiered at the venue including <em>Relatively Speaking</em>, <em>Absurd Person Singular</em>, <em>The Norman Conquests</em> and <em>Bedroom Farce</em>.</p>

<p>“I’m particularly excited to be showing some recent discoveries,” added Simon. “We have the only contemporary portrait of the company’s only female Artistic Director, Caroline Smith, an extraordinary publicity shot of the 1967 company – which included Robert Powell and the stage debut of John Nettles, a number of new production shots and some fascinating obscure images such as the company celebrating the Charles Laughton Festival, the workshops built on the roof of the library and Bob Peck promoting one of his first professional productions in Manor Road Cemetery!”</p>

<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2026/banner-c2a9-haydonning-ltd-w900.png" alt="" width="900" height="188" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption><sub>Examples of images from The Library Theatre at 70 exhibition at Scarborough Library</sub></figcaption></figure>

<p>The free exhibition, which has been made possible by North Yorkshire Council and Scarborough Library, will run from Friday 1 May to Saturday 27 June on the first floor of the library in the crush area next to the Concert Room – where the company first performed. Normal library opening hours apply to the exhibition.</p>

<p>More details can be found on <strong><a href="http://theatre-in-the-round.com/page-41/page-118/">www.a-round-town.com</a></strong>, a website which celebrates the most significant first four decades of theatre-in-the-round in Scarborough under Stephen Joseph and Sir Alan Ayckbourn’s direction.</p>

<p>Scarborough is this year celebrating the 400th anniversary of becoming the UK’s first seaside resort and a number of events are planned throughout the year.</p>
