<p>With the launch of <em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition at Scarborough Library fast-approaching, there will also be a chance to buy some rare publications and a new book pertaining to theatre in the round.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/a-theatrical-revolution-w350.png" alt="" width="350" height="459" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
</div>

<p>The exhibition, which celebrates the 70th anniversary of Stephen Joseph founding the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company at Scarborough Library in 1955, will see the launch of <strong><em><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-144/page-176/">A Theatrical Revolution</a></em></strong> by Simon Murgatroyd – Alan Ayckbourn’s archivist and theatre historian.</p>

<p>This exclusive edition is limited to just 70 signed and numbered copies and includes an exclusive interview with Sir Alan Ayckbourn about his work at the Library Theatre between 1957 and 1976 as well as an in-depth look at the company’s inaugural year in 1955 amongst other content.</p>

<p><em>A Theatrical Revolution – Celebrating Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</em> will be available at <em>The Library Theatre at 70</em> exhibition priced at £5.</p>

<p>There will also be a small collection of other books available including a rare second-hand copy of Stephen Joseph’s seminal work, <em>Theatre in the Round</em> (Barrie &amp; Rockliff, 1967), which contains two chapters dedicated to the first ten years of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre.</p>

<div>
<figure><img src="https://pub-4c23c36058c0491eaa4d6d55c25b33de.r2.dev/news/2025/theatre-in-the-round-w250.jpg" alt="" width="250" height="389" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
</div>

<p>There will also be a copy of one of his most significant works <em>New Theatre Forms</em> (Pitman, 1968) and a rare copy of the first play published by Stephen’s company, Studio Theatre Ltd, <em>Alas, Poor Fred</em> by James Saunders (Studio Theatre Ltd, 1960) – the very first imprint of this play which premiered at the Library Theatre in 1959.</p>

<p>There will also be a first edition hardback of Ian Watson’s fascinating work <em>Conversations With Ayckbourn</em> (MacDonald, 1981) – still, regarded as one of the best works about Alan Ayckbourn as well as a hardback of Alan Ayckbourn’s <em>The Crafty Art of Playmaking</em> (Faber, 2002) – which is regarded as one of the definitive books on playwriting and directing and has never been out of print since it was first published in 2002.</p>

<p>There will also be a chance to buy the most recent edition of Simon Murgatroyd’s previous work <em><strong><a href="http://www.alanayckbourn.net/styled-13/page28.html">Unseen Ayckbourn</a></strong></em> (Lulu Books, 2023), which explores the playwright’s unpublished, unwritten and withdrawn plays.</p>

<p>All these books will be available – whilst stocks last – from the exhibition, which is running at Scarborough Library from 10am to 5pm on Thursday 10 an Friday 11 July and from 10am to 2pm on Saturday 12 July.</p>
