<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>In part 8, we’re looking at questions regarding Alan Ayckbourn’s popularity and the single most repeated inaccurate ‘fact’ regarding Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Popularity</strong></p>

<p><strong>1) Alan Ayckbourn is often quoted as being the second most performed playwright after Shakespeare in the UK. Is this true and where did it originate?<br></strong>No it’s not true. This is a quote that frequently appears in the media about Alan Ayckbourn but without any qualification or source. Truth be told, it’s a statement that is practically impossible to verify in any way.</p>

<p>Undoubtedly, Alan has been and still is one of the most frequently performed living playwrights in England (particularly if you include both amateur and professional productions) and is substantially performed in Europe and in North America. His plays have been translated into more than 40 languages and productions of his work have taken place consistently around the globe over the past six decades – but no organisation keeps track of all professional and amateur productions around England, let alone the world.</p>

<p>As such, it is impossible to state with any certainty Alan is the second most performed playwright after Shakespeare and, accordingly, should not be stated as such. It is far more accurate to say ‘Alan Ayckbourn is one of the world’s most performed living playwrights.’</p>

<p>As to where this quote about being the second most performed playwright after Shakespeare originated, for this we do have a definitive answer. The quote goes back to a press release issued by the British Arts Council in 2 November 1983. This states: “Plays by Alan Ayckbourn have been attracting larger audiences in the regional theatres than those of Shakespeare.”</p>

<p>This was the first such report to be published by the Arts Council and it showed between 1981 and 1983, there were 1034 professional productions of Alan’s work in the UK playing to 327,000 people (in comparison there were 1060 professional Shakespearean productions performed to 318,000 people).</p>

<p>Subsequent Cultural Trend reports during the 1980s and 1990s would see Alan fluctuate between first and third places in the annual performed playwright rankings. The initial 1983 report was widely reported in the media, such as The Stage newspaper which announced the news as “Alan Ayckbourn had upstaged William Shakespeare to take top billing in the first regional theatre popularity charts.”</p>

<p>It is essential to note though the quote is confined to a very specific time period and very specifically to British, subsidised regional theatres. These statistics did not include productions in commercial West End theatres or amateur productions. In the former case, this would have undoubtedly strengthened Alan’s position as, between 1970 and 2000, there was at least one and as many as five professional Ayckbourn productions in London every year (see question 3 below). With regard to the latter, Alan has long been one of the most performed authors by amateur groups in the UK according to the UK’s leading play publishers Samuel French Ltd.</p>

<p>The quote gained traction again circa 1990 when Alan’s public profile was arguably at its peak and the Arts Council’s Cultural Trends report stated he was the most performed playwright in the UK after Shakespeare. This was reported extensively in the print media – but crucially can only be applied with any certainty to that specific time (i.e. 1990) and within the specific context of regional, subsidised theatre in the UK – excluding commercial productions, touring productions and the West End. As far as this author is aware, this information has never been updated by the Arts Council in a similar form, so it is impossible to currently know where Alan Ayckbourn stands in terms of popularity.</p>

<p>When Alan is quoted as being the second most performed playwright in the UK, the only reliable statistics are more than three decades old and I really wouldn’t put much weight on that today!</p>

<p><strong>2) Can I describe Alan as a the second most performed playwright in the country after Shakespeare?<br></strong>Not really, as there is no statistical evidence to back it up. Whilst on the same measure no-one can factually contradict you, there’s no way to support the statement either. It becomes a matter of opinion not a statement fact.</p>

<p>In fact, other than being on fairly safe ground by saying Shakespeare is the most performed playwright in the UK today, it would be practically impossible to say whether Alan – or any other playwright for that matter –  is the second, twenty-second or two hundred and twenty-second most performed playwright in the country. There just isn’t the statistical evidence to back it up.</p>

<p>Neither Alan Ayckbourn himself nor his official website would ever make this statement. What the website does and suggests is describing Alan as is “one of the UK’s most successful living playwrights.”<br><br><strong>3) Alan Ayckbourn is often quoted as being one of the most successful playwrights to have work performed in the West End / London. Is this true?<br></strong>Again, there are no hard and fast statistics to either prove or disprove this statement.</p>

<p>However, what can be demonstrably proved is Alan had an extraordinary presence in London’s theatre-land for 30 years with more than 40 productions of his plays in major London venues.</p>

<p>Between 1970 and 2000, there was at least one and as many as five Ayckbourn plays being performed every year in London’s West End. This is a unique achievement for any living British playwright particularly as it is not due to the success of one play (such as Agatha Christie’s <em>The Mousetrap</em> or Stephen Mallatratt’s adaptation of Susan Hill’s <em>The Woman In Black</em> which have had tremendously long runs) but instead is due to 39 different plays and a couple of revivals produced during that period.</p>

<p>Of course, this record had to come to a close and this took place in 2003 when Alan Ayckbourn announced he would no longer bring his new plays to London or allow them to be produced in the West End. This led to a period of four years when no Ayckbourn play was produced in the West End until 2007 when&nbsp;<em>Absurd Person Singular</em>&nbsp;was successfully revived at the Garrick Theatre. Since then there has been frequent revivals of Ayckbourn plays in the West End.</p>

<p><strong>4) It has been quoted that during the peak of his popularity, an Ayckbourn play was being performed somewhere every day of the year. Is this possible?<br></strong>Actually this is far easier than one imagines and could quite easily apply to any popular playwright; it certainly applied to Alan Ayckbourn throughout most of the 1970s to mid 2010s.</p>

<p>To use an example of 2012: concentrating on significant productions during that year, there were three major UK tours of his work of&nbsp;<em>Neighbourhood Watch</em>,&nbsp;<em>Relatively Speaking</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>Haunting Julia</em>. (each running between two and five months). Add to this the two major productions of Alan’s plays at the Stephen Joseph Theatre,&nbsp;<em>Absurd Person Singular</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>Surprises</em>&nbsp;(running for five months including a short tour), the London productions of&nbsp;<em>Absent Friends</em>,&nbsp;<em>A Chorus Of Disapproval</em>&nbsp;(three months each),&nbsp;<em>Neighbourhood Watch</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>The Boy Who Fell Into A Book&nbsp;</em>and you practically have a full year covered before you begin to include any other professional productions of Alan’s plays in the UK (at least 10 professional repertory productions) or any amateur productions.</p>

<p>If this were to then include the many productions of Alan’s plays taking place around the globe, then it becomes obvious it is not terribly difficult for a popular playwright such as Alan Ayckbourn to reach the level where one of their plays is performed somewhere in the world everyday.</p>

<p>True, you’d probably struggle to find Christmas Day performances, but you can say that of everyone!</p>
