<p><em><a href="/">Alan Ayckbourn’s Official Website</a> constantly receives questions about Sir Alan Ayckbourn and his plays – many of which recur quite frequently.</em></p>

<p><em>As a result of this, there’s a substantial <a href="/life/ayckbourn-faqs">FAQs section</a> on the website looking to answer many of the questions. For the next few weeks, the blog will be reproducing – and occasionally expanding – the FAQs hopefully answering a few questions and offering a bit of insight into Alan Ayckbourn.</em></p>

<p><em>In part 9, we’re looking at questions regarding the filmed adaptations of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays.</em> In part 10, next week, we’ll look at live-streams, audio adaptations and other media.</p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions: Films, TV &amp; Other Adaptations</strong></p>

<div>

<p><strong>1) Which Alan Ayckbourn plays have been adapted into films?<br></strong>There have been five big screen adaptations of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays: Michael Winner adapted&nbsp;<em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page43/Media_Chorus_Of.html">A Chorus Of Disapproval</a></em> (1984), Malcolm Mowbray adapted&nbsp;<em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page37/Media_Revengers_Comedies.html">The Revengers’ Comedies</a></em>&nbsp;(1998) and Alain Resnais adapted&nbsp;<em>Intimate Exchanges</em>&nbsp;into the two films&nbsp;<em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page34/Media_Intimate_Exchanges.html">Smoking / No Smoking</a></em>&nbsp;(1994) as well as&nbsp;<em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page44/Media_Private_Fears.html">Private Fears In Public Places</a></em>&nbsp;(2006) and&nbsp;<em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page42/Media_Life_Of_Riley.html">Life Of Riley</a></em> (2014). Just to confuse issues, the BBC’s adaptation of <em><a href="/plays/way-upstream/way-upstream-radio">Way Upstream</a></em> (1988) is generally regarded as being made for TV but was actually made by BBC Films and was premiered at the London Film Festival.<br><br><strong>2) Which Alan Ayckbourn plays have been adapted for television?<br></strong>The following plays, short plays and revues have been adapted for television in the UK:&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/absent-friends/absent-friends-radio">Absent Friends</a></em> (1982);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/absurd-person-singular/absurd-person-singular-radio">Absurd Person Singular</a></em> (1985);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/bedroom-farce/bedroom-farce-adaptations-in-other-media">Bedroom Farce</a></em> 1980);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/jeeves/by-jeeves-original-cast-recording">By Jeeves</a></em> (2001); <em><a href="/plays/a-cut-in-the-rates/media">A Cut In The Rates</a></em> (1984);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/countdown/countdown-television">Countdown</a> </em>(1972);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/ernies-incredible-illucinations/ernies-incredible-illucinations-television">Ernie’s Incredible Illucinations</a></em> (1987);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/just-between-ourselves/just-between-ourselves-audio-drama">Just Between Ourselves</a></em> (1978);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/men-on-women-on-men/men-on-women-on-men-original-cast-recording">Men On Women On Men</a></em> (1979);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/the-norman-conquests/the-norman-conquests-audio-drama">The Norman Conquests</a></em> (1977);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking/relatively-speaking-radio">Relatively Speaking</a>&nbsp;</em>(1969);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/relatively-speaking/relatively-speaking-radio">Relatively Speaking</a></em>&nbsp;(1989);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/seasons-greetings/seasons-greetings-radio-1985">Season’s Greetings</a></em> (1986);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/service-not-included/productions">Service Not Included</a></em> (1974); <em><a href="http://sevendeadlyvirtues.alanayckbourn.net/page9/Media_Seven_Deadly.html">The 7 Deadly Virtues</a></em> (1984); <em><a href="/plays/time-and-time-again/time-and-time-again-television">Time And Time Again</a></em> (1976);&nbsp;<em><a href="/plays/way-upstream">Way Upstream</a></em> (1988).</p>

<p>A number of international adaptations have also been made for television but a comprehensive list of these has never been compiled..<br><br><strong>3) Which of these adaptations have been made available commercially?<br></strong>Very few of the filmed adaptations have been made available commercially. These are:&nbsp;<em>The Norman Conquests</em>;&nbsp;<em>A Chorus Of Disapproval</em>;&nbsp;<em>The Revengers’ Comedies</em>;&nbsp;<em>Smoking / No Smoking</em>;&nbsp;<em>By Jeeves</em>;&nbsp;<em>Private Fears In Public Places</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>Life Of Riley</em>. A complete list can be found at the foot of this page.<br><br><strong>4) Is there a specific reason why so few of these adaptations have been made available?<br></strong>There is no obvious reason why the television adaptations have not been made available for streaming or on DVD / Blu-Ray. In all likelihood it is a commercial decision by the specific TV / film producers, such as the BBC. </p>

<p>Their release is <strong>not</strong> being blocked by Alan Ayckbourn and he has never expressed any opposition to these filmed adaptations being made available commercially. However, he also has no say or influence in the decision as to whether to consider them for commercial release.<br><br><strong>5) Will&nbsp;<em>Season’s Greetings</em>&nbsp;or any of the other filmed adaptations be released commercially in the foreseeable future?<br></strong>At the present time, Alan Ayckbourn is not aware of any plans by the BBC or any other distributor in the foreseeable future to release&nbsp;<em>Season’s Greetings</em> or any of the other filmed adaptations of his work onto DVD / Blu-ray or streaming services.</p>

<p>Perversely, for some years a remastered and hi-definition version of the BBC’s adaptation of <em>Absurd Person Singular</em> was available to stream in North America, but has subsequently been removed. This version has never been made available commercially in the UK although it is held in archive by both the BBC and the British Film institute.</p>

<p>Since 2023, the BBC adaptation of<em> Way Upstream</em> has been available to stream, on Netflix and certain other streaming platforms (but not the BBC iPlayer). It is unknown how long this will stay available.</p>

<p>As mentioned in question 4, the decision to release these programmes is that of the commercial rights holder (in the case of&nbsp;<em>Season’s Greetings</em>, this being the BBC), not Alan Ayckbourn. Contrary to some reports, Alan Ayckbourn has no opposition to films such as&nbsp;<em>Season’s Greetings</em>,&nbsp;<em>Absent Friends</em>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<em>Absurd Person Singular</em>&nbsp;being made available commercially – indeed during 2019, he endorsed an Ayckbourn Film Festival at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, where a number of the filmed adaptations were shown.<br><br><strong>6) Has Alan ever written screenplays specifically for film and / or television?<br></strong>Despite his love of cinema, Alan has never written an original screenplay for film, nor has he personally adapted any of his plays into filmed screenplays.</p>

<p>Contrary to popular belief (and Michael Winner’s less than reliable accounts), Alan did not write the final screenplay of&nbsp;<em>A Chorus Of Disapproval</em>. Michael Winner adapted the play, this was then given to Alan who spent a day revising it and trying to restore elements which had been lost. This screenplay was then again adapted by Michael Winner into the final product which bears little resemblance to the original play or author’s intentions and which the playwright has always expressed deep dissatisfaction with.</p>

<p>Alan has written just one screenplay for television. <em><a href="/plays/service-not-included">Service Not Included</a> </em>is a half-hour piece, written for a BBC Masquerade drama series. Alan also wrote a number of sketches for Ronnie Barker’s comedy show <em><a href="http://recordings.alanayckbourn.net/page23/Media_Barker.html">Hark At Barker</a></em> under the pseudonym of Peter Caulfield.<br><br><strong>7) What was the connection between Alan Ayckbourn and the French film director Alain Resnais?<br></strong>The acclaimed French film director <a href="/career/recordings/film-adaptations/alain-resnais">Alain Resnais</a> was a long-term fan of Alan Ayckbourn’s plays and, unknown to the playwright, had been making regular trips to the <a href="/career/ayckbourn-and-the-sjt/sjt-venues/stephen-joseph-theatre-in-the-round">Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round</a>, Scarborough, during the 1980s to see his plays.</p>

<p>In 1989, during the world premiere run of&nbsp;<em>The Revengers’ Comedies</em>, Alan Ayckbourn was told by a member of the staff Resnais was in the audience – Alan disbelievingly replied ‘And Jean-Luc Goddard is in the toilet…’. The pair subsequently met for the first time and forged a long-standing and affectionate friendship.</p>

<p>It was Alain Resnais who approached Alan Ayckbourn about adapting his plays into films – it’s unlikely Alan would ever have believed Resnais would have been interested. Alain initially asked to adapt the epic play-cycle&nbsp;<em>Intimate Exchanges</em>&nbsp;into the two films <em>Smoking</em> / <em>No Smoking</em> (Alan again noted the madness of this and Resnais couldn’t possibly mean to film all 16 variations, to which Resnais apparently replied, of course not, just 12 of them…).</p>

<p>He later adapted&nbsp;<em>Private Fears In Public Places</em>&nbsp;into the film&nbsp;<em>Coeurs</em>&nbsp;and Resnais’s final film before his death in March 2014 was&nbsp;<em>Aimer, Boire et Chanter</em>&nbsp;(an adaptation of&nbsp;<em>Life Of Riley</em>). Prior to his death, Resnais was already working on a screenplay for his next project, an adaptation of Alan’s play&nbsp;<em>Arrivals &amp; Departures</em>.</p>

<p>Of all the filmed adaptations of his work, Alan is most fond of the Resnais movies and believes they are the ones which remain most true to the plays. As to why Resnais’s adaptations work so well, Alan has frequently noted he is a playwright who writes films for the stage and Resnais was a director who made plays for the screen.<br><br><strong>8) Has&nbsp;<em>Smoking</em>&nbsp;/&nbsp;<em>No Smoking</em>&nbsp;ever been released with English sub-titles or dubbed into English?<br></strong>When <em>Smoking</em> / <em>No Smoking</em> was originally released into cinemas in the UK, it had English sub-titles. This version has occasionally been screened on television subsequently. However, these sub-titles have not been included on any of the commercial DVD releases of&nbsp;<em>Smoking</em>&nbsp;/&nbsp;<em>No Smoking</em>&nbsp;– so they can currently only be seen in the original French language.&nbsp;<em>Smoking</em>&nbsp;/&nbsp;<em>No Smoking</em>&nbsp;has never been dubbed for an English audience on a commercial release.<br><br><strong>9) Which Ayckbourn plays are available to stream digitally?<br></strong>Currently, the only Ayckbourn plays to stream on UK platforms are <em>Way Upstream</em> (Netflix) and <em>The Norman Conquests</em> (occasionally shown on Acorn).<br><br><strong>10) Alan Ayckbourn Films / TV Adaptations Released Commercially<br></strong>○&nbsp;The Norman Conquests (VHS, 1980)<br>○&nbsp;The Norman Conquests (region 2 DVD, 2005)<br>○&nbsp;The Norman Conquests (region 1 DVD, 2011)<br>○&nbsp;The Norman Conquests (digital platforms – USA only, 2011)<br>○&nbsp;The Norman Conquests (region 2 DVD, 2014)<br>○&nbsp;Absurd Person Singular (digital platforms – USA only, 2016)<br>○&nbsp;A Chorus Of Disapproval (VHS, 1991)<br>○&nbsp;A Chorus Of Disapproval (region 2 DVD, 2007)<br>○&nbsp;Smoking / No Smoking (VHS, 1994)<br>○&nbsp;Smoking / No Smoking (region 2 DVD – French Language only, 2005)<br>○&nbsp;The Revengers’ Comedies (VHS, 2001)<br>○&nbsp;Sweet Revenge (region 1 DVD, 2001)<br>○&nbsp;By Jeeves (VHS, 2001)<br>○&nbsp;By Jeeves (region 2 DVD, 2002)<br>○&nbsp;Private Fears In Public Places (region 1 / 2 DVD, 2007)<br>○&nbsp;Life Of Riley (Blu-ray / DVD, 2015)<br>○&nbsp;Way Upstream (digital platforms, 2023)</p>
