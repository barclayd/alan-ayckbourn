<p>This month marks a very obscure anniversary in the Ayckbourn calendar. So obscure, in fact, it’s really only well-known by four people!</p>

<p>Ten years ago this month in 2015, Alan Ayckbourn’s first novel – <em><strong><a href="/plays/the-divide">The Divide</a></strong></em> – had its first public airing when it was read aloud in the Ayckbourn’s home by the author, his wife, her P.A. and his archivist.</p>

<p>It began a rather long and convoluted journey which would take another four years before the novel  was published as originally intended and conceived by Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p>To those who might only know <em>The Divide</em> through its 2018 stage adaptation by Annabel Bolton, it’s worth emphasising <em>The Divide</em> was both conceived and written as a novel. It was never intended for the stage and only by strange circumstance, did it do so.</p>

<p>Which brings us back to June 2015 and an extraordinary round-table experience.</p>

<p>Alan had first mooted the idea of writing something very different during 2014. Ironically, his intention was to create something which could never be staged and his intention was to write a novel which would be unencumbered by the practical limitations of writing a play for performance with the intention it would be published as a book.</p>

<p>This idea evolved into&nbsp;<em>The Divide</em>, a novel told through reportage (journal entries, cuttings, transcriptions etc) forming a single large prose work set in a post-catastrophic England where the sexes are segregated following the outbreak of a fatal disease which has devastated the male populace.</p>

<p>In a world where all birth is by artificial insemination, a literal north / south barrier sees the men confined to the north of the country with the women in the south. In this environment, a forbidden love affair blooms which will have far-reaching implications for everyone.</p>

<p>The first draft of the book was written during early 2015 and rapidly expanded from the author’s initial vision into a much more expansive piece of world-building. It was undoubtedly inspired by his early love of classic works of science fiction from the ’40s through to the ’60s and he finished writing the first draft in May 2015.</p>

<p>That same year marked the 60th anniversary of the Stephen Joseph Theatre – the venue with which Alan had become synonymous with over the decades. Fund-raising events were being planned and Alan suggested the idea of an exclusive public reading of his first novel.</p>

<p>Hence, on 4 June 2015, Alan and his wife Heather Stoney were joined by Kath Dunn-Mines and Simon Murgatroyd in the library and the 39 roles shared among them (as the piece consisted of diaries and reportage written by the various characters). Seven-and-a-half hours later, the reading of the 240 A4 page manuscript had been completed. </p>

<p>Satisfied with the novel, but realising it was too long for a straight-through reading, Alan then sat down with his wife’s P.A., Kath Dunn-Mines, and began to edit the epic into a condensed version suitable for the gala reading. Just to emphasise again, this wasn’t a play-script adaptation – it was just a condensed and edited form of the novel.</p>

<p>Having edited the piece fairly drastically, it was given a gala reading with special guests on 27 September 2015 in aid of the Stephen Joseph Theatre. Despite the edits, the event still ran for an extraordinary eight-and-a-half hours!</p>

<p>Following this – and still enthusiastic about the novel – Alan began adding further material to the prose, expanding the texture and world-building of the piece whilst looking to find a publisher; which was to prove harder than might be imagined given Alan’s only experience had been with publishers (or divisions thereof) which dealt with plays. </p>

<p>Concurrent to this, Annabel Bolton, Associate Director at The Old Vic, asked Alan for permission to adapt and develop&nbsp;<em>The Divide</em>&nbsp;for the stage, having attended the gala reading. Alan agreed, although he played little part in the adaptation process. This resulted in a production at the Edinburgh International Festival during 2017 and The Old Vic during 2018 and led to the often-mistaken belief that <em>The Divide</em> is a play and not a novel.</p>

<p>Still searching for a means to publish the now expanded and finished novel, Alan found help in the shape of the author and playwright – Robert Shearman – who made his professional stage debut at the Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round. He introduced Alan to the Hornsea-based publishers, <strong><a href="https://pspublishing.co.uk">PS Publishing</a></strong>. Already fans of the playwright’s work, the publisher’s directors Pete and Nicky Crowther expressed an interest in working with the author to publish&nbsp;<em>The Divide</em>&nbsp;as he had originally intended and envisioned it.&nbsp;</p>

<p>Once again, Alan returned to the manuscript re-editing and revising certain parts with this main desire to create a novel reflecting the idea of a ‘found’ work consisting of journal pages, official documents and the like. </p>

<p>PS Publishing met this challenge and the design of the book was created to reflect this with each entry formatted to portray the type of entry (i.e. handwritten pages for journals, typed pages for official documents etc). The final design more than met Alan’s expectations and hopes when he had embarked on the project.</p>

<p>Four years after being written,&nbsp;<em>The Divide</em>&nbsp;was finally published as originally envisaged and launched on 12 September 2019 at the Stephen Joseph Theatre, Scarborough, as a limited edition slipcased edition and hardback before later being released in softback.</p>

<p>The playwright’s first – and likely, only – novel had finally been published. And within it, a nod to its origins in 2015 with a dedication to Heather Stoney, Kath Dunn-Mines and Simon Murgatroyd, the people who along with Alan had given the novel its first airing back in June 2015.</p>
