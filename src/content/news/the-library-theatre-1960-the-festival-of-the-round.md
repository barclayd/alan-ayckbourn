<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We now move to 1960 and the UK’s first theatre in the round festival.</p>

<p><strong>1960: The Festival of the Round</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p>A key element of the formative years of Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was Stephen’s encouragement of the amateur community in Scarborough and further afield.</p>

<p>With little money behind the project and little prospect of the company making any notable financial gains in its first few years, Stephen knew he needed as much help as he could get. And he knew the perfect place to go.</p>

<p>An interesting fact about Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre was, thanks to Stephen, it embraced both professional and amateur theatre. The summer seasons were – with one exception – all professional productions, but they were preceded by an amateur season in-the-round. In fact, even after Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre moved on to a new home and became the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-15/">Stephen Joseph Theatre in the Round</a></strong> in 1976, the Library Theatre was home to many amateur productions in subsequent years, often still in-the-round.</p>

<p>Members of the amateur community were also instrumental in running the front of house operations for Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre on a voluntary basis. This was primarily organised by Ken Boden and his wife, Margaret, through the British Theatre Guild – which also had strong ties to the company since its inception.</p>

<p>Stephen genuinely believed a theatre serving the community should embrace both professional and amateur work and he was a strong advocate of this practically in Scarborough.</p>

<p>This was perhaps most demonstrably shown in 1960, when he founded the UK’s first theatre in the round festival at the Library Theatre in Scarborough, encouraging amateur companies to embrace this ‘new’ form of theatre. This was a fairly radical proposition at the time as theatre-in-the-round was still a relatively novel form of theatre in the UK and had not been adopted on any notable scale by professional companies around the UK.</p>

<p>It had only been five years since Stephen Joseph set up the UK’s first professional theatre-in-the-round company in the country in Scarborough and he was two years away from opening the UK’s first purpose-built theatre-in-the-round at the <strong><a href="/career/victoria-theatre">Victoria Theatre</a></strong>, Stoke-on-Trent.</p>

<p>Yet Stephen believed there was a genuine appetite for the form, which had received national exposure thanks to his work at Scarborough and through several other practitioners. Now he was to put that theory to the test to see whether an amateur in-the-round festival could be successful in a regional theatre.</p>

<p>It transpired it could. Although the archives in Scarborough are sadly lacking in any significant amount of material relating to the festival and its history, the newspapers cutting relating to it survive.</p>

<p>The festival was announced in April 1960 and received extensive coverage in the region’s major newspapers as well as being publicised as far as away as Glasgow with Stephen Jospeh frequently on hand to publicise the event. The aim of it, according to Stephen, was “to stir up theatre and see what it could do for amateur drama.”</p>

<p>The first event ran from 14 – 17 September, 1960, immediately following the end of the summer season at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre. It was promoted as “A Festival Of Plays To Be Performed In The Round” but in subsequent years was changed to the somewhat catchier title of the “In The Round Festival.”</p>

<p>Organised in conjunction with the British Drama League, the first festival attracted eight amateur companies, who performed 10 productions.</p>

<p>The companies were: Whitby Youth Club, Pickering Drama Club, Scarborough Theatre Guild, Bradford Experimental Theatre-in-the-Round Group, Hull Garrett Players, Mexborough Technical College, Hugh Bell Evening Institute Middlesborough and Merseyside Unity Theatre.</p>

<p>Plays performed during the first festival included Shakespeare’s&nbsp;<em>A Midsummer Night’s Dream</em>, Priestley’s&nbsp;<em>A Glass Of Bitter</em>, Jonson’s&nbsp;<em>Volpone</em>&nbsp;and Beckett’s&nbsp;<em>Endgame</em>&nbsp;as well as several more experimental pieces.</p>

<p>In subsequent years, the festival attracted groups from York, Rotherham, Manchester, Leeds, Stoke-on-Trent, Derby and even the Greenock Players from Scotland.</p>

<p>Stephen himself adjudicated each performance between 1960 and 1964 and was encouraging, if notoriously honest, in his appraisals – judging by the media reports at the time.</p>

<p>The success of the event was even noted by Scarborough Council and used as part of the argument for an early push for the theatre-in-the-round company to have a purpose-built home in the town, sadly something which would not be realised until 1996 and the move into the company’s present home.</p>

<p>Stephen was diagnosed with terminal cancer in 1965, but the festival continued until 1968 at the Library Theatre with adjudicators including the famed BBC Radio Producer Alfred Bradley as well as Sylvia Rice, Rodney Wood and Derek Slater.</p>

<p>The festival was later revived by Alan Ayckbourn after the company’s move to the <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-188/page-15/">Stephen Joseph Theatre In The Round</a></strong> in 1976 with the playwright himself adjudicating several festivals. Sadly, even less information is held in the archive with regard to the festivals held during this period, although it is known that Russell Whiteley also adjudicated for several years.</p>

<p>What is interesting about the festival though is it shows how important Stephen Joseph felt the amateur community was to his theatre-in-the-round during its formative years. One of his earliest ambitions for the theatre was that both professional and amateurs should be able to work in the same environment.</p>

<p>Whilst it is a great sadness the company has far fewer links with amateur dramatic organisations today, it must never be forgotten that the theatre owes its very existence to the amateur community which Stephen felt was so essential.</p>

<p>In 1965, Stephen Joseph closed Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre with no intention of it ever re-opening. It is only because Ken Boden and his army of volunteers drawn from the amateur community stepped in to save the company in 1966 by producing an amateur season, that the way was paved for the resumption of professional theatre in 1967. It’s fair to say if Stephen hadn’t so keenly encouraged the amateur community to be so involved, there would be no Stephen Joseph Theatre today.</p>

<p>You can still catch a glimpse of what Stephen Joseph was trying to achieve in the archives at Scarborough Library. It holds a substantial collection of programmes for professional and amateur productions produced in the building over the decades, commemorating all their contributions to theatre in the round in the town. Something Stephen would, I’m sure, very much approve of.</p>
