<p>Throughout 2025, the Ayckbourn Blog is celebrating the 70th anniversary of <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-4/">Stephen Joseph</a></strong> founding the UK’s first professional theatre in the round company in Scarborough in 1955.</p>

<p>Focusing on <strong><a href="http://www.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/page-187/page-14/">Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre</a></strong> – the company’s first home between 1955 and 1976 – we’re celebrating the venue and people involved as well as its historic connection to Alan Ayckbourn.</p>

<p><em>The Library Theatre</em> is a series of regular articles looking at key moments from each year highlighting the significance and achievements of the Library Theatre.</p>

<p>We begin in 1955 and a look at the play which launched the company 70 years ago…</p>

<p><strong>1955: The First Play</strong> by Simon Murgatroyd</p>

<p><em><strong><a href="http://plays.theatre-in-the-round.co.uk/offsite-19/page-3/page0/page0.html">Circle of Love</a></strong></em>&nbsp;is the first significant play produced by what was Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre, but is now better known as the Stephen Joseph Theatre. Its significance is drawn not from its success or notoriety, but from one simple essential fact.</p>

<p>It is where the company began.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>A publicity image for Circle of Love (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>It was the first play produced at Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre in its inaugural season in 1955. This play would launch Stephen Joseph’s radical concept of a professional theatre-in-the-round company that was also dedicated to producing new writing; both largely alien concepts in British theatre at the time.</p>

<p>It was the launch-pad for a risky concept in the Yorkshire seaside resort of Scarborough, a place which had been chosen for reason little more than having the right room at the right price at the right time.</p>

<p><em>Circle of Love</em>&nbsp;was written by Eleanor D. Glaser who, like the other inaugural season playwrights, had come into contact with Stephen through his playwriting courses. Although her work had been performed before, this was apparently her first professional production.</p>

<p>Eleanor was one of three women writing for the company that season; a remarkable figure given the paucity of female playwrights being actively promoted and produced in British theatre during the 1950s. It is a telling reflection of the period that the newspapers were less interested in Eleanor’s play than the fact she was a housewife who also wrote – apparently whilst ironing and with the aid of a dictation machine!</p>

<p>The play’s plot concerns a possessive mother and her attempts to thwart her son’s first love, whilst the boy’s estranged father is torn between new love and his moral commitments. Until 2005 that sentence would summarise practically all there was on record about the play in archive in Scarborough.</p>

<p>Sadly, no manuscript had been kept – not unusual during the company’s formative years – and most of the archival material relating to it was disposed off during the 1980s by a theatre press officer more concerned with having a bit more room in their office than the history of the company!</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>A publicity postcard showing a scene from Circle of Love (© Studio Theatre Ltd)</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Fortunately, in 2005, Eleanor’s papers were tracked to the University of Warwickshire which held an original copy of <em>Circle of Love</em> in archive.</p>

<p>The cast consisted of Kara Aldridge, Joan Cibber, Shirley Jacobs, Ralph Nossek, Morris Perry, John Sherlock and Helen Towers with Stephen Joseph as director. None of the actors had had any prior experience of acting in the round, although the reviews suggest they acquitted themselves well.</p>

<p><em>Circle of Love</em>&nbsp;opened Theatre in the Round at the Library Theatre’s eight week season on 14 July 1955 with an invited audience, largely drawn from members of the Scarborough branch of the British Theatre League.</p>

<p>Reviews were mixed, although the critics’ main concern was not with the play but with the round staging with barely a review not comparing it to a boxing ring. However,&nbsp;<em>Circle of Love</em>&nbsp;was described by the Northern Echo as “a moving play” and by the Yorkshire Evening Press as “good theatre.” More in-depth commentary came from The Stage which noted: “This is a workmanlike play…. [Glaser] draws her story out too long and comes to a rather abrupt ending which lacks dramatic force.”</p>

<p>Unfortunately, the play did not attract significant audiences during its initial week-long run; although much of the blame for this was subsequently put on what was apparently the hottest summer on record for fifty years.</p>

<div>
<figure><figcaption><sub>The Yorkshire Evening News reporting on the opening of the theatre on 15 July 1955</sub></figcaption></figure>
</div>

<p>Stephen believed the weather was instrumental in keeping audiences away and within two weeks of opening, he announced sales were so poor, the theatre might have to close. Fortunately for the theatre, the weather broke and Stephen reported: “We were saved by the first rainy day, when the theatre filled to capacity.”</p>

<p><em>Circle of Love</em>&nbsp;returned for a second week of performances from 4 August and attracted larger audiences. It was popular enough for Stephen to commission a second play from Eleanor,&nbsp;<em>Call The Selkie Home</em>, for the 1956 season.</p>

<p>It’s not recorded in Scarborough’s archives whether <em>Circle of Love</em> was ever produced again and there is no record of Eleanor staying in contact with Stephen Joseph after 1956, but the play stands as historically significant because it had the difficult task of launching the company and setting out its stall as theatre in the round promoting new work by new authors.</p>

<p>Stephen Joseph was always honest about the difficulties of the first season, but the chance to produce new plays such as&nbsp;<em>Circle of Love</em>&nbsp;and promote new writing in the round inspired him and offered him more than enough reason to continue his project.</p>

<p>“At the end of the first season a limited objective had been achieved. No great success; no real proof of either artistic or commercial viability. But acting on a central stage was exciting from every point of view, and there seemed all sort of opportunities yet to explore.”</p>

<p><em>Simon Murgatroyd (2025)</em></p>
